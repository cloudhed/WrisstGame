# **Pöllöka Species Foundation**

## **General body plan**

Pöllöka have a mixed internal and external support structure. A light endoskeleton carries their weight, while hard chitin protects the claws, feet, mouthparts, cranial fronds, and dorsal wing plates. The torso and thighs remain covered by flexible micro-scaled skin.

Their soft integument feels smooth and dry, with a faint grain when touched against the scale direction. The trunk remains warm during activity, while exposed chitin feels cooler. Their circulatory fluid is dark blue-green and clots into a glossy near-black film.

Pöllöka stand upright on powerful hindlimbs. Broad thighs contain most of their locomotor muscle. The lower legs narrow before widening into three-part feet with flexible gripping pads.

Their upper limbs sit high beside the chest. Each limb folds inward and ends in two opposing hooks with a sensitive pad between them. They have no palms or fingers. Fine work is performed by pinching objects between the hooks, bracing them against the chest, or holding them against a work surface with one foot.

A short tail extends from the rear of the pelvis. It tapers into a flattened chitinous vane and usually rests close against the body, which conceals it from the front. Ground-stage Pöllöka use it for balance. Winged Pöllöka use it as a rudder during sharp turns.

Females commonly develop brighter pigmentation across the cranial fronds. Male fronds tend to grow broader and heavier, and male vocal chambers produce a deeper fundamental tone. Either sex may develop wings.

## **Eyes**

Each eye consists of one large lens beneath a transparent protective plate. The horizontal pupils expand until they occupy most of the visible eye in darkness.

Pöllöka vision favors low light, broad movement, and changes along the horizon. Their strongest color separation lies among green, blue, and violet hues. Warm reds appear darker and closer together. A stationary object becomes easier to study when the Pöllöka moves their head slightly from side to side.

Their eyes have limited independent movement. Pöllöka turn or tilt the whole head when following something. A translucent inner membrane sweeps across each eye to remove dust, moisture, and bathhouse condensation. During sleep, this membrane remains closed, though it allows some light through.

Sudden sunlight overwhelms them. Their pupils contract quickly, while full visual adaptation takes several minutes. Outdoor travelers commonly use thin eye veils, shaded hoods, or tinted plates.

## **Antennae**

The long feathered structures above the eyes are true antennae. Their branches detect airborne chemicals, humidity, temperature changes, and small disturbances in the surrounding air.

A Pöllöka can determine which direction someone approached from by comparing the signals received by each antenna. They can also recognize familiar individuals through oils, clothing treatments, work materials, and recent food.

Steam carries scent exceptionally well until the moisture becomes dense enough to saturate the antennae. Heavy wind produces constant conflicting signals. Klyftet’s split-howl is therefore physically exhausting for Pöllöka, especially in narrow passages where the air changes direction rapidly.

The antennae have voluntary muscles near the base. Their outer branches respond reflexively and frequently reveal attention before the speaker reacts. Pöllöka can hold them still with concentration, though doing so becomes uncomfortable.

Touching an antenna without warning causes pain and sensory confusion. Close companions may guide an exhausted Pöllöka by holding the thick base near the skull. The feathered branches remain too delicate for direct handling.

## **Cranial fronds**

Several curled chitinous fronds grow from flexible joints around the rear and sides of the skull. Everyday speech often calls them horns.

The fronds serve four physical functions:

* They shield the antennae when folded forward.  
* They release excess body heat through thin vascular tissue near their bases.  
* They redirect sound toward recessed auditory membranes behind the jaw.  
* They protect the rear of the head during climbing and falls.

Each frond remains rigid along its length. Movement occurs at its muscular base, allowing the whole structure to flare, fold, or rotate by a limited amount.

Pigment becomes stronger when blood flow increases. Heated, frightened, or physically exhausted Pöllöka therefore show darker coloration around the frond bases. Females usually retain more elaborate color bands after reaching full ground-stage growth.

## **Mouth, voice, and breathing**

Several short mandibles surround a smaller central mouth. The outer mandibles cut and hold food. An inner rasp breaks it down before swallowing.

Pöllöka commonly eat softened roots, fungal flesh, dense grain pastes, fermented fruit, and warm broths. Their mandibles can cut tougher food, though meals prepared in strips or small pieces require less effort.

Two narrow breathing seams sit beneath the rear edges of the jaw. Air passes through paired lungs and several dorsal air sacs. These sacs support long periods of activity and later supply the oxygen required for flight.

Vocal membranes beneath the sternum produce whistles and sustained tones. The mandibles add clicks, trills, and rolling consonants. A spoken sentence may carry an additional emotional or grammatical distinction through its closing click.

Pöllöka can learn other languages clearly. Some clicking distinctions disappear in translation, which occasionally makes their Common speech seem more uncertain or indirect than intended.

During exertion, the breathing seams widen and the upper flanks pulse visibly. A winded Pöllöka may lose their whistled tones while retaining short clicked words.

## **Temperature regulation**

Pöllöka produce some internal heat through muscle activity, though they lose it quickly while resting. Their preferred body temperature lies around 30 to 34 °C.

Thermal vents, warmed stone, humid warrens, and shared sleeping chambers support ordinary Pöllöka settlements. Thin clothing allows heat exchange through the torso and frond bases. Cold-weather garments concentrate insulation around the abdomen and upper legs while leaving the antennae unobstructed.

Below roughly 16 °C, their movements become slower and their claws lose precision. Continued exposure can push them into torpor. Rapid reheating causes disorientation, so an immobilized Pöllöka must be warmed gradually.

Dry heat irritates the breathing seams and antennae. Warm humidity suits them considerably better.

## **Hands, tools, and physical work**

The opposing foreclaws provide precise control over narrow objects. Pöllöka tools commonly include loops, perforated grips, shallow grooves, or broad flattened handles.

They write by securing a stylus between both hooks of one limb. The second limb steadies the writing surface or tracks the current line. Many tap the surface as they read, especially when checking measurements.

Their forelimbs handle little weight compared with their hindlimbs. Heavy loads are carried through hip frames, shoulder harnesses, or baskets braced against the torso. A Pöllöka may also grip an object with one foot while working on it with both forelimbs.

Claw tapping forms part of their communication. The surface changes the sound, so wood, stone, and metal can give the same tapping pattern different levels of urgency.

## **Movement**

Pöllöka walk through controlled shifts of weight across their broad foot pads. Fine flexible fibres beneath each pad soften contact with the ground and grip small surface irregularities.

Their natural gait produces very little sound. The terminal foot claws remain raised during ordinary walking and extend when climbing, sprinting, or resisting a pull.

They climb steep surfaces well when cracks or projections are available. Their strong thighs support sudden upward leaps, while the foreclaws find holds and stabilize the torso.

Open-water swimming is difficult. Their cranial structures create drag, and the dorsal air sacs make the body prone to rolling. Most can keep themselves afloat in calm water. Rough water quickly becomes dangerous.

Pöllöka usually rest in a low crouch with the thighs folded beneath the torso. During deeper sleep, they lie against a warm surface and tuck their antennae between the cranial fronds.

## **Body language**

| State | Observable Pöllöka response |
| ----- | ----- |
| Focused attention | Both antennae angle toward the subject while the head makes small measuring movements |
| Physical ease | The antenna branches spread loosely, and the foreclaws rest against the sternum |
| Uncertainty | The antennae alternate directions while the mandibles produce delayed, uneven clicks |
| Irritation | The cranial fronds press closer to the skull, and one claw begins tapping a nearby surface |
| Fear | The antennae fold into the protection of the fronds as the body lowers over widely spread foot pads |
| Embarrassment | The antennal tips curl inward, and circulation darkens the bases of the cranial fronds |
| Exhaustion | The antennae droop below eye level, and the protective eye membranes sweep more slowly |
| Trust | The Pöllöka turns their unprotected back toward the other person or allows them near the antenna bases |

These signals can be deliberately suppressed. The reflexes become harder to control during fatigue, pain, or sudden surprise.

## **Lifecycle**

### **Brood stage**

Pöllöka hatch from leathery brood capsules kept in warm communal chambers. A newly hatched larva has a soft segmented body, several temporary gripping nubs, closed eyes, and short sensory filaments around the head.

Larvae navigate through scent, vibration, and contact with the chamber walls. They recognize regular caretakers through their footsteps and chemical traces.

Several moults gradually produce stronger mouthparts, longer sensory filaments, and the internal structures required for the first eclosion.

### **First eclosion**

A mature larva seals itself inside a casing made from silk, saliva, and fine mineral dust. The body reorganizes into the upright ground-stage form.

The new ground-stage Pöllöka emerges with soft claws, pale fronds, and eyes that remain highly sensitive for several days. Movement begins awkwardly because the hindlimbs and elevated forelimbs require a completely different balance.

The skin and claws harden first. The cranial fronds gain their mature coloration over the following months. Full body growth continues for several cycles.

A Pöllöka may begin their pilgrimage once their temperature regulation, claws, and senses support independent travel. They remain a biologically complete adult throughout the ground stage.

### **Ground-stage adulthood**

Ground-stage adults can work, reproduce, form pods, sponsor younger travelers, and become matriarchs. Wing development has no bearing on physical adulthood.

Two dormant wing matrices lie beneath the dorsal plates. A broader pair sits behind the shoulders, with a smaller stabilizing pair positioned along the lower ribs.

Most outsiders associate wings with advanced age because accumulated hardship becomes more likely over time. Some elders remain ground-stage throughout their lives. Younger adults occasionally develop wings after unusually demanding circumstances.

## **Why wings develop**

The trait originates in Pöllöka ancestors that lived around unstable thermal vents. Adults remained ground-bound while local conditions supported the pod. Repeated displacement and prolonged exertion prepared the body for a dispersal form.

Wing development requires three biological conditions:

1. The Pöllöka has reached full ground-stage growth.  
2. Sustained physical and nervous strain primes the dormant wing matrices.  
3. A later period of safety lowers the stress response and permits eclosion.

Long journeys, displacement, illness, inadequate sleep, dangerous work, and extended responsibility for another person can contribute to priming. The body measures accumulated demand and recovery. A single frightening event rarely provides enough stimulation.

The Pöllöka has little conscious control over the process. Deliberately forcing the body through extreme hardship can damage the matrices or leave development stalled beneath the dorsal plates.

## **Second eclosion**

Once the stress response falls, the wing matrices begin drawing heavily on stored nutrients. Early symptoms include increased appetite, upper-back pressure, sensitivity around the dorsal seams, and a strong need for humid warmth.

The dorsal plates slowly loosen along existing seams. During eclosion, the plates lift and expose two pairs of folded, fluid-heavy wings. The process usually lasts several hours.

New wings emerge dark, wet, and compressed. Circulatory fluid fills their veins as the Pöllöka stretches them. Fine surface scales separate while the membranes dry.

The wing seams close over the following days. Short balancing movements begin first, followed by controlled gliding. Reliable flight usually takes several weeks of muscle development and practice.

A stalled second eclosion causes persistent swelling, fever, loss of claw precision, and severe sleep disruption. Warm humidity can relieve the symptoms, though the individual still requires a sustained period of safety before the plates will open.

## **Mature wings**

Pöllöka wings consist of translucent membranes supported by branching chitinous struts. Fine scales create most of their visible coloration.

The broad upper pair supplies lift. The smaller lower pair handles braking and directional control. Both pairs fold closely along the back beneath the raised edges of the dorsal plates.

Winged Pöllöka launch with a powerful jump from the hindlimbs. Elevated ground and warm air make takeoff easier. Flat-ground takeoff requires a running leap and leaves little room for error.

They can cross ravines, travel between elevated settlements, and glide for long distances on thermal currents. Cold rain weighs down the scales. Violent crosswinds remain dangerous, particularly for inexperienced flyers.

Wings carry only modest additional weight. A heavy pack can prevent takeoff, and carrying another adult is generally impossible.

Tears between the veins can close gradually. Damage to a major supporting strut leaves permanent distortion. Pöllöka protect folded wings carefully in crowded interiors and become wary when someone stands directly behind them.

## **Ageing**

Older Pöllöka develop thicker claw edges and wear along the tips of their cranial fronds. Antenna branches become sparse, which reduces scent direction and air sensitivity.

The transparent eye plates gradually cloud. Older individuals compensate through head movement, touch, and familiar scent routes.

Wing scales wear away through use and seldom recover their original density. Very old winged Pöllöka often rely on gliding from prepared ledges because flat-ground launches place too much strain on the hindlimbs.

# **Minttärä**

Minttärä is a fully grown ground-stage female. Her ochre integument, deep teal extremities, cyan eyes, and curled frond pattern are individual coloration. Her unusually broad thighs have become more pronounced through Klyftet’s slopes and repeated vent-bucket work.

The teal ridges visible behind her shoulders and along her sides are dormant dorsal plates. Her future upper wings sit behind the shoulder plates, while the smaller pair develops beneath the lateral ridges.

Her long antennae make Klyftet’s wind especially unpleasant. Outdoors, she stays near walls and turns her head away from open passages. Inside the Hotbaths, she can identify regular visitors through damp cloth, soap residue, skin oils, and the disturbance their bodies create in the steam.

Her silent approach comes from ordinary Pöllöka foot anatomy refined through three cycles of night work. She shifts her weight slowly enough that even wet stone rarely squeaks beneath her pads.

Minttärä’s wing matrices are already heavily primed. Her failed journey, involuntary rerouting, difficult arrival, unresolved pilgrimage, physical labour, and uncertain debt have sustained the necessary strain. The Hotbaths provide warmth and humidity, while her continuing lack of control prevents the recovery response from beginning.

A violent confrontation may provide the final physiological demand. Her wings remain beneath the plates during the conflict. Development begins once she reaches safety and accepts that the immediate ordeal has ended.

Clearing the debt can trigger this change. Choosing to remain at the Hotbaths freely can also trigger it, since her body responds to restored control instead of physical departure.

Before the quest resolves, observable symptoms may include:

* She presses her upper back against heated stone whenever she pauses.  
* Her usual clothing sits looser around the dorsal seams.  
* She eats scraps during the night and avoids explaining the increased appetite.  
* The bases of her fronds remain dark after physical work.  
* She becomes protective of the private steam chamber.  
* One foreclaw occasionally misses its grip when the dorsal pressure interrupts her concentration.

Minttärä’s wings emerge with deep teal veins and pale green membranes. Warm ochre scales gather near the outer edges. The smaller lower pair carries a tighter version of the same pattern.

After the quest resolves, the first unmistakable sign appears as a thin line of moisture along her upper dorsal seam. The plate edges rise a few millimetres from her back while she leans against the steam chamber wall.

# **Pöllöka Society and Culture**

## **The pod**

The pod is the central Pöllöka household, economic unit, and legal identity. Most pods contain between six and fifteen adults. Members share access to a warm chamber, staple food stores, tools, sleeping space, and a common emergency reserve.

Pod membership is chosen. Biological relations, lovers, and close friends may belong to the same pod, though none of those relationships grant membership by themselves. One individual may share intimacy with someone from another pod while keeping separate stores and obligations.

Each adult belongs to one pod at a time. Travellers retain their membership while away, provided they continue answering messages and declare any debts that could affect the shared stores.

A pod usually occupies several connected rooms around one heated chamber. Smaller sleeping alcoves branch from it. Workshops and cooking spaces remain cooler, allowing members to move between different temperatures during the day.

The central chamber contains three practical objects:

* The vent key controls the shutters or valves regulating heat.  
* The record bundle contains membership, debt, birth, pilgrimage, and store records.  
* The threshold plate carries the pod’s identifying pattern.

The threshold plate is carved deeply enough to be read with a foreclaw. Pöllöka returning through steam or darkness often tap it before entering.

## **Communal brood households**

Brood capsules are kept in dedicated broodhouses maintained by several nearby pods. The capsules require stable warmth, humidity, regular turning, and protection from fungal growth. Caretakers of every sex work there.

A larva belongs to its brood cohort instead of inheriting membership in a parent’s pod. Biological mothers remain present in the records because maternal ancestry affects hereditary illness and reproductive compatibility. A known father may add his own mark, though his pod gains no claim over the larva.

Individual parents may visit, feed, handle, and form close bonds with their young. Other caretakers often become equally familiar. Larvae recognize recurring footsteps, scents, and handling patterns long before they can identify a biological parent.

After the first eclosion, the young Pöllöka remains near the broodhouse while learning to balance, regulate heat, use tools, and interpret adult speech. They gradually spend time with several supporting pods. One of those pods eventually offers provisional membership and sponsors their pilgrimage.

This arrangement gives a young Pöllöka several possible homes. A parent may invite them into the same pod, while a craft teacher, caretaker, or unrelated group may suit them better.

## **Joining a pod**

A prospective member first enters as a warm guest. They eat from the common stores, take ordinary work shifts, sleep in the shared chamber, and attend an account reading.

The guest period lasts for one complete store rotation. Its length depends on how frequently the pod buys or gathers staple supplies. A prosperous urban pod might complete one in several weeks. A remote vent settlement may take an entire season.

At the next account reading, the matriarch asks each adult member for objections. Objections must identify a practical concern involving safety, debt, workload, use of space, or an existing conflict. Personal dislike can extend the guest period, though it cannot permanently block admission without a practical grievance.

Once admitted, the new member presses a claw into warm sealing resin beside their name on the pod record. The matriarch adds the pod mark beneath it. The new member then taps the threshold plate before entering the chamber as a full member.

## **Leaving and forming pods**

A member may leave by declaring their intention during an account reading or before two witnesses. The pod returns their personal possessions and records any remaining obligation. Shared tools and stores remain with the pod.

A matriarch cannot prevent departure. She may demand an account of missing goods or unfinished communal work, and the departing member may challenge that account before another matriarch.

New pods commonly form when an existing chamber grows crowded, several returning pilgrims want to establish a workshop together, or a group settles near a newly opened thermal source.

Recognition requires:

* At least four committed adults  
* A reliable source of warmth  
* An adult woman willing to serve as matriarch  
* A witnessed record of shared stores and debts  
* Consent from the settlement controlling the vent or land

A large pod may divide its record bundle and stores between two new chambers. Members choose which threshold to mark. The original matriarch may continue with either group or surrender the vent key during the division.

## **The matriarchy**

Pöllöka matriarchy developed around the protection of brood capsules and thermal chambers. Early vent settlements depended on continuous heat management. The women maintaining maternal records also tracked capsule health, food use, migrations, and access to shelter.

Modern matriarchs retain responsibility for the pod’s material continuity. Biological motherhood remains optional. A matriarch may have produced no brood of her own.

Every recognized pod has one matriarch. She must have reached full ground-stage growth and completed her first pilgrimage. Wings have no bearing on eligibility.

The pod chooses her from among its adult women. Succession carries no hereditary preference. A matriarch’s daughter receives the same consideration as any other qualified member.

### **Authority and limits**

| The matriarch may | The matriarch requires pod consent to |
| ----- | ----- |
| Regulate ordinary use of the warm chamber | Sell or permanently abandon the chamber |
| Release emergency food and medical stores | Accept a debt against shared property |
| Admit temporary guests | Grant permanent membership |
| Represent the pod before other matriarchs | Relocate the entire pod |
| Open and close pilgrimage records | Expel a member |
| Mediate internal disputes | Divide the pod’s record bundle |
| Assign urgent maintenance work | Commit members to outside labour |

Every member retains a minimum sleeping temperature and access to emergency water. Deliberately depriving a member of safe warmth is treated as physical violence.

The matriarch reads the pod accounts aloud at each store rotation. Two members inspect the measures and record bundle beside her. Either witness may be male.

A respected matriarch keeps the vent stable, maintains legible accounts, and listens before assigning a cost to someone. An abusive matriarch hides store shortages, alters debt records, confiscates route-silks, or uses warmth to punish disagreement.

## **Stewards and male authority**

Male Pöllöka work throughout public and domestic life. They serve as broodhouse caretakers, scholars, healers, caravan leaders, craft teachers, negotiators, and settlement guards.

A pod may appoint any member as its steward. The steward manages daily work, supervises stores, and speaks for the pod when the matriarch is temporarily absent. Some male stewards handle nearly every practical decision within a busy pod.

The steward cannot close a pilgrimage, alter the membership record, or bind the pod to a long debt. Those decisions wait for the matriarch or receive emergency review from two neighbouring matriarchs.

A skilled male steward may remain in that position through several matriarchal successions. Pöllöka sometimes describe such a person as the claw that keeps finding the same loose stones.

## **Succession**

A matriarch may announce her intention to step down during an account reading. Qualified women then present themselves or accept nomination from another member.

The pod selects a candidate through open tapping. Each member strikes the threshold plate once for support or keeps their claws folded. The candidate then carries the vent key for one store rotation while the previous matriarch remains available.

After the trial period, a neighbouring matriarch witnesses the transfer of the record bundle. The outgoing matriarch removes her personal loop from the vent key. Her successor adds her own.

When a matriarch dies or disappears, the steward seals the private section of the records and manages ordinary needs. A neighbouring matriarch opens the sealed records before witnesses. The pod then selects a successor.

A pod without an eligible woman becomes an open chamber. Its steward may maintain the household while the members seek a new matriarch, join another pod, or divide among neighbouring chambers.

## **Settlement councils**

A settlement containing several pods is represented by a council of matriarchs. Each matriarch brings advice and objections gathered from her own pod.

The council handles vent allocation, broodhouse funding, route safety, outside agreements, and disputes between pods. Stewards, craftspeople, and affected members may speak during a meeting. The matriarch places the pod’s binding mark once her members’ position has been heard.

Councils rarely maintain prisons. They rely on repayment, work agreements, restricted access to shared stores, or supervised departure. Immediate threats may be removed from a chamber, though the council must provide enough warmth to keep the expelled person alive until travel becomes possible.

# **The First Pilgrimage**

## **Purpose**

The first pilgrimage establishes the contribution a ground-stage adult intends to develop. The pilgrim leaves familiar caretakers and working routines, enters another community, and returns with something that can be used by others.

Possible returns include a learned technique, a copied record, improved tools, a trade agreement, a mapped route, or documented evidence that a proposed method failed.

Pöllöka commonly say the pilgrimage begins when a larva leaves its casing. Several cycles of growth and preparation may pass before actual departure. The young adult must first demonstrate stable temperature regulation, reliable claw use, and enough physical endurance for the intended route.

## **Choosing a purpose**

The prospective pilgrim proposes a practical subject. A craft interest may lead toward a guild, while an interest in history may lead toward an archive or established witness.

The purpose must answer two questions:

1. What will the pilgrim learn, test, recover, or establish?  
2. Who could use the result when the pilgrimage closes?

The receiving destination also matters. A pilgrim must offer useful service while learning there. A smithing pilgrim may maintain tools. A record pilgrim may copy damaged texts or organize testimony. This prevents wealthy pods from treating pilgrimage as simple travel purchased for their young.

The pilgrim chooses the destination. The matriarch may challenge an unsafe route or inadequate supplies, though she must help identify a workable alternative.

## **Sponsorship**

Each pilgrimage has two sponsors.

The pod matriarch opens the formal record, confirms that the traveller has sufficient provisions, and guarantees emergency support from the pod.

A craft sponsor confirms the purpose. This person may be a teacher, experienced traveller, archivist, healer, or another adult familiar with the intended work. Craft sponsors may be male or female.

A matriarch who repeatedly blocks suitable pilgrimages can be challenged before the settlement council. Another matriarch may open the record after hearing the pilgrim and original sponsor.

## **The route-silk**

Each pilgrim carries a narrow band of treated silk called a route-silk. It is resistant to moisture and folds through a loop in Pöllöka clothing.

The opening section contains:

* The pilgrim’s name and broodhouse  
* Their sponsoring pod  
* Both sponsor marks  
* Their declared purpose  
* The intended destination  
* A description of the provisions carried at departure

Information is encoded through dyed lines, small claw punctures, knots, and scented resins. A Pöllöka can inspect the band visually, trace it with a claw pad, or identify the pod resin through their antennae.

Hosts, caravan leaders, and employers may add witness marks along the route. These marks record hospitality, work performed, accidents, rerouting, or debts. The final section remains blank until the pilgrimage is formally closed.

A damaged or lost route-silk can be reconstructed from witnesses and copied records. The process may take months when the route crossed several regions.

## **Pilgrim hospitality**

A valid route-silk grants a travelling Pöllöka one night of safe warmth at any recognized Pöllöka chamber. The host pod provides water and a basic meal when supplies allow.

In exchange, the pilgrim reports road conditions, disrupted trade, recent settlements, and hazards encountered since the previous witness mark. Matriarchs add useful route reports to their communal records.

A pilgrim may remain longer by working, paying, or receiving an explicit invitation. Repeatedly claiming emergency warmth along the same short route produces a visible series of marks, so misuse becomes easy to identify.

Second eclosion overrides ordinary limits. Any Pöllöka showing active dorsal separation receives a humid chamber and enough food to survive the emergence. Questions about the cause wait until the individual chooses to answer.

## **Work at the destination**

The pilgrim remains at the destination long enough to make a verifiable contribution. Duration depends on the purpose.

A route survey might require one difficult crossing. Archival study could last an entire cycle. Learning a craft may take several cycles and result in the pilgrim settling near the teacher.

The receiving institution adds a completion mark when the work reaches a useful state. A corrupt mark carries serious consequences for both parties. Other pods may refuse the witness’s future records or demand independent verification.

The Library of Caarth fits this system especially well. A Pöllöka pilgrim arrives with sponsorship, a declared archival purpose, and proof that they expect to provide service. Copying testimony or repairing route histories could satisfy the Library’s access requirements.

## **Closing the pilgrimage**

A pilgrim closes the route before their sponsoring pod or another recognized matriarch. They present the route-silk, demonstrate what they brought back, and answer practical questions from the gathered members.

The result can differ from the original goal. A failed expedition may produce a reliable hazard map. An inaccessible teacher may lead the pilgrim to document why the craft disappeared. The account must provide evidence that another person can inspect or use.

The matriarch adds a closing mark to the blank end of the route-silk. The craft sponsor adds a second mark or sends a witnessed approval. The closed silk is copied into the pod records, while the original remains with the traveller.

A returned pilgrim may then:

* Sponsor another first pilgrimage  
* Keep the protected sections of a pod record  
* Stand for selection as matriarch  
* Found a recognized pod with other adults

Open pilgrims retain ordinary adult control over their work, relationships, movement, reproduction, property, and pod membership.

## **Rerouting**

A pilgrim may change purpose or destination through a process called turning the line.

The pilgrim explains the obstruction to a recognized witness, identifies a new practical purpose, and adds a turning mark to the route-silk. Their craft sponsor receives a copy when communication permits.

The revised purpose must use something gained through the interrupted journey. A traveller stranded in a mining settlement might document safe vent maintenance, learn a local repair method, or establish a route through the pass.

Returning directly home also remains available. The pilgrim presents the interruption and closes the route with whatever useful account they can provide.

Long delay attracts concern. Fraudulent witness marks, concealed debts, and false accounts attract formal censure.

## **Open routes**

Pöllöka introductions often include pilgrimage status. A formal introduction might identify someone as “Minttärä of her broodhouse, under her pod’s heat, with her line open toward Caarth.”

Among strangers, a shorter question is common.

“Where is your line open?”

This asks about the destination and declared work. It gives the pilgrim room to describe delays on their own terms.

“Why have you failed to close it?” is accusatory. Asking this publicly implies laziness, dishonesty, or avoidance.

An open route lasting several cycles may affect how others treat the pilgrim. Matriarchs often request the route-silk before extending substantial credit. Employers may ask whether a work agreement supports the declared purpose or prevents it.

## **Wings and pilgrimage status**

Pilgrimage travel often supplies the strain that primes wing development. Recovery after returning home may then trigger second eclosion. This pattern created the popular association between returned pilgrims and wings.

The two processes remain institutionally separate. A winged pilgrim may still carry an open route-silk. A wingless elder may have completed several later journeys and serve as matriarch.

Pöllöka etiquette permits questions about flight readiness, injuries, and needed accommodations. The cause of someone’s wings remains private.

# **Minttärä’s Position**

Minttärä completed her physical development before departure. Her home matriarch opened the pilgrimage, and a second sponsor approved the Library of Caarth as a suitable destination.

Her declared work concerns archival practice. She intends to study how Caarth compares conflicting testimony and preserves route histories gathered from different species. Her contribution at the Library would involve copying, checking, and physically organizing records. She plans to return with a method her pod can use when several witnesses remember the same journey differently.

This purpose fits her observable abilities. Minttärä remembers conversations, notices changes in routine, and works carefully without needing supervision. Before leaving, she assisted with broodhouse supply records and corrected discrepancies by recalling who had entered each storage room.

Her rough passage to Wrisst interrupted the route before she reached Caarth. The last complete witness mark comes from the coastal departure point. A salt-stiffened section records the failed crossing and her shipment inland, though no recognized matriarch witnessed her arrival in Klyftet.

Three cycles at the Hotbaths have given her useful skills, yet her route remains formally unchanged. She has never turned the line toward Klyftet, and she has never declared her archival purpose abandoned.

Minttärä can close the pilgrimage through several culturally valid outcomes:

* She reaches the Library of Caarth and completes the original work.  
* She turns the line toward Klyftet and documents a useful system developed through the Hotbaths.  
* She returns to her sponsoring pod with a verified account of the failed route.  
* She finds another recognized archive willing to receive her service.  
* She completes the journey before a different Pöllöka matriarch and sends the closed record home.

The culture gives her options, while her circumstances restrict access to them. Travel costs, employment obligations, missing witnesses, and the absence of a local Pöllöka pod can each become part of SQ01.

If the Bwavrek debt remains in the quest, Pöllöka custom distinguishes between recorded debt and control of the pilgrim. Bwavrek may demand repayment under clear terms. Confiscating her route-silk would be viewed as interference with pod records and could bring outside Pöllöka against him.

Tavo-Pavo’s delivery can contain a small spool of treated recording silk and a sealed measure of pod resin acquired from a passing trader. Minttärä needs them to repair the salt-damaged section and send an account home. Receiving them forces her to decide whether to preserve the old destination, turn the line, or keep the final panel blank.

In ordinary introductions, Minttärä now omits her pod and destination. She gives her name, indicates the Hotbaths, and returns to work. Her route-silk remains looped inside one of her green hip fasteners, with its blank closing panel folded against her body.

# **Everyday Pöllöka Culture**

## **Regional variation**

Pöllöka communities develop around reliable warmth, though the source of that warmth changes by region.

Vent warrens occupy stone chambers around geothermal cracks. Their residents maintain shutters, channels, and water basins that distribute heat through several connected pods.

Urban Pöllöka often settle beside communal ovens, foundries, bathhouses, or industrial exhaust channels. Their agreements grant labour or maintenance in exchange for controlled access to waste heat.

Seasonal communes use resin-coated silk structures stretched around shallow vents. Their walls can be folded and transported when the vent weakens. Heated stones preserve enough warmth during travel to prevent torpor.

Coastal communities benefit from humid air and mild temperatures. They favour cliff settlements with sheltered landing shelves for winged residents. Rough water still makes sea travel hazardous.

Every recognized settlement remains accessible from the ground. Stairs use low rises suited to Pöllöka legs, while roughened walls provide claw holds beside steep sections. Winged residents may use elevated entrances, though essential stores and council chambers remain reachable by walking.

## **Settlement layout**

The warmest district sits near the centre. It contains the broodhouse, recovery chambers, food stores, and emergency shelter.

Domestic pods occupy the next ring. Workshops, fermentation rooms, and public kitchens use the remaining heat after it passes through the central chambers.

Cool rooms sit along the outer walls. Food requiring dry storage is kept there, along with materials that soften in heat. Visitors from cold-adapted species are usually housed in this outer ring.

A settlement commonly includes:

* A broodhouse with several separately regulated chambers  
* A shared kitchen built around wide heated basins  
* An eclosion room with high humidity and open floor space  
* A record room protected from condensation  
* Cool workshops for resin, dye, and metalwork  
* One or more landing shelves connected to ground routes  
* A still room where bodies are prepared after death

Water moves through shallow wall channels. Residents can test its temperature with a claw pad as they pass. Changes in flow or warmth are reported immediately.

The settlement council assigns vent-maintenance rotations. Young ground-stage adults frequently perform the inspections because the work teaches them every route between the chambers.

## **Heat as a common necessity**

A pod controls the ordinary use of its domestic chamber. The settlement council controls the vent, furnace agreement, or other shared heat source.

Each resident receives enough warmth to sleep safely regardless of their work output. Emergency chambers remain available to travellers, sick residents, and anyone showing symptoms of second eclosion.

Additional heat carries an accountable cost. A craft requiring prolonged high temperatures may owe maintenance work, fuel, or goods to the settlement. These agreements appear in the public records.

Tampering with a heat channel is treated as physical violence. Someone who closes another pod’s vent shutter during an argument may be removed from maintenance duties and required to work under supervision.

The vent key carried by a matriarch opens her pod’s local controls. It cannot override the settlement’s emergency channels.

## **Daily rhythm**

Most Pöllöka wake during the late afternoon. They eat, inspect their antennae, and complete delicate work while the light continues fading.

Movement through public chambers reaches its peak after sunset. Account readings, council meetings, lessons, and communal meals commonly occur during the first half of the night.

The hours around midday are used for deep rest. Eye veils cover openings where direct sunlight enters, while the warm chamber remains dim enough for the protective eye membranes to relax.

A Pöllöka working during daylight usually takes several short visual rests. They close the inner membranes, fold their antennae beneath the cranial fronds, and keep one claw against a stable surface.

Winged couriers favour the cool air shortly before sunrise when local winds remain predictable. Long-distance gliding waits for rising thermal currents later in the day, which requires eye protection.

## **Domestic space**

Each pod arranges its chamber around a heated central surface. Members may crouch beside it while eating or lie against it during rest.

Personal alcoves provide space for clothing, route-silks, tools, and private conversations. Their entrances use hanging screens that disturb the air when touched. A visitor waits until the occupant taps the floor or draws the screen aside.

The warm chamber belongs to the whole pod. Sleeping positions change according to temperature, injury, and work schedules. Members who require greater warmth lie closest to the heated surface.

Close companions often rest with their backs touching. This protects the dorsal plates while leaving both sets of antennae facing outward. A ground-stage Pöllöka may also allow a trusted companion to help fold the antennae between the cranial fronds before sleep.

Pöllöka senses make complete scent privacy difficult. Personal alcoves use mineral steam and absorbent charcoal to reduce lingering traces. Naming another person’s body scent in public is considered invasive unless illness, smoke exposure, or another safety concern makes it relevant.

## **Cleaning and body care**

The antennae are cleaned with soft combs dipped in warm water. Oils and dust caught between their branches can distort air direction and scent.

Cranial fronds are polished with thin resin. Excess resin blocks heat exchange near their bases, so the flexible joints remain uncovered.

Foot pads require regular washing. Fine grit trapped among their fibres can produce sound, reduce grip, and cause shallow abrasions.

The transparent eye plates are rinsed with clean water. Pöllöka avoid smoke-filled rooms because residue collects beneath the protective membranes.

Communal washrooms contain shallow heated basins and textured floor stones. Deep bathing pools are uncommon in traditional settlements because Pöllöka roll easily in water. When they use larger baths, rails and submerged foot holds run along the walls.

## **Food**

Traditional Pöllöka food relies heavily on fungal flesh, softened roots, grain paste, fermented fruit, and mineral-rich broth.

Their outer mandibles cut strips from firm food. The inner mouth handles smaller pieces and liquids. Bowls usually have raised rims and fixed loops so a claw can hold them securely.

Common foods include:

* Vent mash made from grain, root pulp, and fermented fungus  
* Foldcakes, thin layers of pressed paste carried by travellers  
* Warm fruit ferment with a low alcohol content  
* Broth thickened enough to cling to a feeding blade  
* Roasted fungal caps cut into narrow strips  
* Softened seed clusters coated with mineral salt

A communal steep pot remains warm throughout the active period. Members add broth, leftover mash, or trimmed food pieces as they pass. The pot is emptied and scrubbed at every store rotation.

Cold food digests slowly and may leave a Pöllöka sluggish. Travellers carry small heat stones wrapped beside their provisions.

Strong alcohol dulls air perception and makes the antennal branches respond unevenly. Pöllöka taverns usually serve mild fermented drinks warmed with roots or fungal spice.

Second eclosion causes an intense need for concentrated food. Settlements keep sealed jars of dense grain fat and sweet fungal paste in the recovery chamber.

## **Broodhouse food**

Larvae eat fungal cream and finely ground root paste. Caretakers place the food along shallow grooves where the larvae can locate it through scent and contact.

Each brood cohort develops a familiar feeding scent. Adults returning to their childhood broodhouse can often identify the mineral blend used during their earliest moults.

Late-stage larvae produce the strongest silk while preparing for first eclosion. Clean strands left outside completed casings are collected, boiled, and woven into record cords. Material removed directly from an occupied casing is considered unsafe and receives no practical use.

## **Clothing**

Pöllöka clothing keeps the torso ventilated and the dorsal plates unobstructed. Most garments consist of narrow wraps, hanging panels, harness loops, and lightweight veils.

Fasteners are broad enough to operate between opposing hooks. Pulling one loop releases the garment without requiring fine finger movements.

Ground-stage clothing commonly leaves a clear line along the upper back. A hidden eclosion can damage both the wearer and the developing wings, so tightly fitted dorsal garments attract concern.

Winged clothing uses split rear panels that pass between the upper and lower wing pairs. Travel harnesses transfer weight toward the hips and thighs.

Eye veils are made from dark gauze or thin tinted plates. Pilgrims often attach them to the cranial fronds so they can be raised with a single movement.

Clothing around the lower body protects the vent folds from dust, rough surfaces, and cold air. Coverage carries little inherent moral meaning. Formal clothing may expose much of the torso while displaying an elaborate route-silk, pod resin, and carefully polished fronds.

## **Colour and ornament**

Pöllöka distinguish blue, green, and violet hues particularly well. Their textiles frequently use layered combinations within that range.

Warm red dyes appear dark to them. Such dyes may serve as tactile markers, trade colours chosen for other species, or pigments intended to absorb additional heat.

Raised stitching remains common even on decorative cloth. Patterns can be traced with a claw pad in dim conditions.

Personal resin carries a mild scent mixed from the individual’s pod, craft, and preferred ingredients. It marks records, storage containers, and tools. Copying another person’s resin without permission constitutes record fraud.

Frond ornaments attach near the rigid outer sections. The vascular bases remain clear. Heavy pieces that interfere with heat release are worn briefly during formal gatherings.

## **Material ownership**

Pod stores contain staple food, bedding, ordinary tools, fuel, and emergency supplies. Members record what they remove when the quantity could affect another person’s work.

Personal property usually includes:

* Clothing and bodily care tools  
* Route-silks and pilgrimage records  
* Gifts  
* Specialised craft tools  
* Objects brought from a completed journey  
* Sealed correspondence  
* Items explicitly purchased from a personal share

Members may earn coin from outside employment. Each pod decides how much enters the common stores. The agreement must appear during an account reading and applies equally to members performing comparable work.

Pöllöka settlements use internal measures of goods and labour for everyday distribution. Öre and Crowns become more important when dealing with caravans or neighbouring Monsterfolk.

A hidden personal debt can endanger shared stores. Pod members are therefore expected to declare repayment agreements that involve housing, tools, travel documents, or future labour.

## **Work and crafts**

Pöllöka physiology supports several specialised crafts without restricting individual vocation.

Their claw pads and antennae suit textile inspection, fermentation, ventilation work, fine assembly, archival handling, and the detection of leaks. Strong hindlimbs support climbing, hauling through hip frames, and work on steep structures.

Traditional Pöllöka tools use:

* Perforated handles  
* Broad loops  
* Grooves that receive a claw hook  
* Weighted bases that remain steady without being held  
* Pedals operated by the feet  
* Chest braces for controlled pressure

Worktables stand close to the torso. A lower ledge allows one foot to secure the material while both forelimbs manipulate it.

Winged workers often serve as route scouts or couriers. They choose these occupations according to skill and physical condition. Settlement councils cannot claim routine flight labour simply because a resident has wings.

## **Records and evidence**

Pöllöka speech distinguishes how the speaker obtained information. A sentence may end with a deliberate click indicating direct observation, received testimony, reconstruction, or uncertainty.

These markers have no exact equivalent in Common. A Pöllöka may use extra phrases such as “I handled the record,” “someone told me,” or “the pattern suggests” when precision matters.

Emotional clicks occur between words and respond reflexively to surprise, discomfort, or amusement. Evidential clicks appear at the end of a statement and are produced deliberately.

Formal records preserve the same distinctions through punctures and knots:

| Record mark | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Deep paired puncture | Witnessed directly by the recorder |
| Single puncture beside a resin line | Reported by the named source |
| Crossed fibres | Reconstructed from several records |
| Loose unsealed knot | Uncertain or awaiting verification |
| Cut fibre with sealed ends | Earlier entry formally withdrawn |

Removing the uncertainty mark from a copied claim is considered falsification. A correct record preserves disagreement between witnesses.

This practice explains Minttärä’s interest in the Library of Caarth. Multispecies testimony often lacks Pöllöka source markers, forcing archivists to compare vocabulary, physical evidence, and the interests of each witness.

## **Names and introductions**

A Pöllöka personal name contains a spoken melody followed by a short click pattern. Common speakers usually reproduce only the voiced portion.

Minttärä therefore hears her Common name as incomplete, though recognizable. Trusted speakers who learn the final click receive a small movement of her antennae before she answers.

Formal introductions may include broodhouse, supporting pod, vocation, and pilgrimage status. The order changes according to context.

A travelling introduction might translate as:

“Minttärä, emerged at the Low Amber Broodhouse, carrying the heat of the Ninth Vent Pod, with her line open toward Caarth.”

Among coworkers, “Minttärä from the Hotbaths” supplies enough information for ordinary conversation.

## **Conversation**

Silence carries no automatic discomfort. Pöllöka commonly pause while their antennae inspect a change in air, scent, or temperature.

Their head movements can make an answer appear hesitant. A Pöllöka may already know what they intend to say while turning slightly to resolve the speaker’s position.

Interrupting before the closing click can change the evidential meaning of a sentence. Pöllöka speakers often become irritated when Common speakers repeatedly answer too early.

Dry humour commonly uses a deliberate mismatch between the spoken claim and its source marker. A Pöllöka might describe an obviously broken vent as “recently inspected,” then close the statement with the click for uncertain reconstruction.

Fiction begins with a special opening cadence that suspends ordinary evidence rules. Without it, listeners may assume the storyteller intends the account to enter communal memory.

## **Music and performance**

Pöllöka music combines chest whistles, mandible percussion, resonant surfaces, and controlled wing vibration.

Performers tap stone or hollow wood with their foreclaws. Listeners feel the rhythm through their foot pads as well as hearing it through the auditory membranes behind the jaw.

Winged performers can produce low pulses by moving closed wings against the air. Damaged wings change the tone, and some older musicians build compositions around an uneven vibration.

Public stories permit listeners to add short tapping patterns when they recognize a person, route, or event. A storyteller pauses when someone provides a correction mark, then states whether the addition belongs to the current version.

## **Greetings and approach etiquette**

Pöllöka greet one another by touching one claw to the sternum and tapping the nearest stable surface. The contact supplies a clear location through sound and vibration.

Someone approaching from behind gives a low whistle or taps twice before entering claw range. This remains especially important around winged adults.

A direct stare carries little hostility. Their broad eyes and limited eye movement make sustained visual attention ordinary.

Small head movements indicate visual inspection. Outsiders sometimes interpret them as disagreement or nervousness.

Handshakes are awkward and potentially painful. Pöllöka accept them by offering the thick inner section of a foreclaw while keeping the hooks turned away.

## **Physical boundaries**

The antenna branches remain the most protected external organs. Uninvited contact causes pain and disrupts several senses at once.

Cranial fronds tolerate careful contact along their rigid outer sections. The flexible bases remain sensitive and warm.

Standing close behind a winged Pöllöka restricts their ability to unfold or protect the wings. Crowded public spaces include marked wall positions where winged residents can stand with their backs covered.

Touching another person’s route-silk without permission resembles opening private correspondence. Reading its visible opening panel is acceptable when the wearer presents it for inspection.

A guest entering a domestic chamber waits at the threshold plate. Crossing before receiving a tap may lead to immediate removal.

## **Hospitality toward outsiders**

Pöllöka settlements extend emergency warmth to any person whose body can safely tolerate it. Species requiring cooler conditions receive insulated bedding in the outer chambers.

A visiting Monsterfolk receives a temporary resin tag marked with their sleeping place and host pod. The tag helps residents identify an unfamiliar scent during darkness or heavy steam.

Guests are warned about eye veils, deep pools, active eclosion rooms, and restricted broodhouse chambers.

An outsider may become a warm guest of a pod and eventually gain full membership. They accept the same store duties and account readings as Pöllöka members. Matriarchal eligibility still requires completion of a recognized Pöllöka pilgrimage, so outside members participate in selection while remaining ineligible for the key.

## **Partnerships and intimacy**

Romantic partnerships, sexual relationships, pod membership, and brood contribution are recorded separately.

Partners may live in different pods. They visit through ordinary guest rules and keep their own material obligations.

Sharing a sleeping temperature carries greater intimacy than sharing a meal. Allowing someone to settle against the dorsal plates also requires trust because the position limits defensive movement.

A partner gains no right to another person’s route-silk, tools, income, or brood records. Longstanding partners may exchange personal resin loops that permit entry to their alcoves.

Public affection often involves foreclaws resting together at their padded inner surfaces. Antenna contact remains uncommon because the branches catch and overstimulate one another.

## **Reproduction and brood responsibility**

Adults considering brood contribution consult the maternal health records kept by the broodhouse. These records identify hereditary illnesses and biological pairings that produced unsuccessful capsules.

A fertilized capsule remains inside the female until its outer covering hardens. She then places it in a broodhouse chamber selected for temperature and humidity.

The female retains control over the capsule’s placement and the naming of its maternal record. A contributing male may add his mark with her consent.

Broodhouse workers assume daily care after placement. Biological contributors may visit and become important caretakers, though the larva remains part of its cohort.

Brood contribution grants no automatic claim to the young adult’s future pod, vocation, or pilgrimage purpose.

## **Disputes**

Minor pod disputes enter the next account reading. The speakers present the object, work record, damaged tool, or other evidence involved.

Serious cases use an open record. A neighbouring matriarch hears each account while two record keepers preserve the source markers.

Participants wear blunt claw caps when tempers have already produced threats. The caps remain visible and prevent sudden hooking injuries.

Outcomes may include repayment, supervised work, replacement of damaged goods, public correction of a false record, or temporary restriction from nonessential stores.

Repeated record fraud reduces the weight given to a person’s testimony. Future agreements may require an additional witness and sealed copies held by another pod.

Violence involving brood chambers, heat controls, or an active eclosion produces immediate removal from the affected area.

## **Departure and expulsion**

A settlement may expel someone who continues creating physical danger after repair attempts fail.

The council records the reason and provides enough food, eye protection, and portable warmth to reach the nearest safe destination. A departure witness inspects the supplies.

The expelled person retains their identity records and route-silk. Confiscating either would obstruct future accountability.

Nearby Pöllöka settlements receive a copy of the expulsion record. They may still offer shelter while setting stricter supervision.

## **Second eclosion in communal life**

Every established settlement maintains at least one humid recovery room with enough space for both wing pairs to unfold.

Once dorsal separation begins, ordinary debts and work obligations pause. Two attendants regulate heat, prepare food, and keep others outside the room.

The attendants record physical symptoms without demanding the history that caused them. The emerging adult decides who may enter.

After the membranes dry, a wingworker checks the struts and demonstrates folding movements. Flight practice begins from low platforms over padded ground.

The first controlled glide usually has witnesses because injuries remain common. The event creates no new legal standing. The record states the date, physical condition, and assistance required.

## **Age and accommodation**

Older Pöllöka receive floor routes with fewer steep climbs. Public chambers place tapping rails along the walls for residents whose eyes or antennae have weakened.

Sparse antennae make scent direction unreliable. Familiar workers add stronger resin marks to doors, tools, and food stores.

Winged elders may keep permanent landing access near their pod. Others stop flying and use their wing membranes as additional insulation while resting.

A matriarch who can no longer manage the vent key may retain her title during a succession rotation. Another member performs the physical inspections until the transfer is complete.

## **Death**

A dead Pöllöka is moved from the warm chamber into the settlement’s still room. Cool, dry air slows decomposition while the pod checks personal records and contacts absent members.

Caretakers clean the antennae, fold them between the cranial fronds, and close the protective eye membranes. Wings are folded along the dorsal plates when their condition permits.

The pod copies unfinished agreements and marks debts that died with the individual. Claims against shared property remain open for review.

An open route-silk receives a cut fibre sealed at both ends. The mark records that the journey ended while preserving its actual state.

Most bodies are placed in heated fungal beds outside the domestic chambers. Once the soft tissue has returned to the bed, surviving chitin is cleaned and ground into mineral dust. A small amount may be added to first-eclosion casing material if the dead person requested this in their record.

A companion may retain one cut section from a worn frond tip. Its ridges remain readable by touch long after the original pigment fades.

# **Minttärä in Klyftet**

The Hotbaths reproduces several conditions of a Pöllöka settlement. It provides warm stone, humid air, shallow water channels, and activity during the night.

Its social structure remains unfamiliar to her. Bwavrek controls the workplace, assigns labour, and owns the rooms. He carries none of a matriarch’s formal obligations unless their private agreement supplies them.

Minttärä’s earliest sense of obligation may come from Pöllöka hospitality custom. If Bwavrek gave her food and safe warmth when she arrived exhausted, she would treat that act seriously even before any monetary debt appeared.

Several daily habits follow from her culture:

* She begins work near sunset and handles her most precise tasks after dark.  
* She taps a bucket rim before entering an occupied room.  
* She leaves bowls beside the hottest wall so the food remains warm.  
* She washes her foot pads before crossing a freshly cleaned floor.  
* She records uncertain information separately from events she witnessed.  
* She keeps her route-silk inside the green hip fastening where bath steam cannot pull it loose.  
* She uses the private steam chamber when her antennae become overloaded by competing scents.  
* She turns slightly during conversations because her eyes inspect stationary details more clearly through head movement.  
* She pauses long enough to complete an evidential click, even when another speaker assumes she has finished.  
* She avoids letting anyone stand close behind her as the dormant dorsal plates become tender.

Her dry humour survives poorly in Common because listeners miss the closing click that identifies deliberate uncertainty. Bwavrek may have spent three cycles hearing apparently sincere statements about perfectly clean drains and extremely competent customers.

Minttärä has never placed a membership mark on a Hotbaths threshold plate. Her sleeping space may be familiar and physically suitable, though it carries no recognized promise of continued warmth.

Her planned work at the Library of Caarth grows directly from Pöllöka record culture. She wants a method for preserving testimony from species that cannot produce evidential clicks, trace silk punctures, or identify a witness through personal resin.

# **Minttärä**

## **Individual appearance**

Minttärä has warm ochre integument across most of her body. Deep teal covers her foreclaws, feet, cranial fronds, and the dormant plates behind her shoulders. Her eyes are bright cyan beneath clear protective membranes.

Her cranial fronds curl inward near their tips. One side has a shallow worn groove from years of resting her record cord against it while working.

Her thighs are unusually broad for a ground-stage Pöllöka. The muscles have developed further during three cycles of climbing Klyftet’s slopes and carrying vent buckets.

She wears thin green wraps secured at both hips. Her route-silk remains folded inside the left fastening. The right fastening holds a small work strip marked with maintenance knots.

Her upper-back clothing has become looser as pressure builds beneath the dorsal plates. A pale abrasion crosses the inner hook of her left foreclaw where a bucket rope slipped during the accident at the centre of SQ01.

## **Broodhouse and childhood**

Minttärä emerges at the Low Amber Broodhouse, a communal nursery built around a mineral steam vent. The broodhouse serves several neighbouring pods.

Her biological mother, Elvärä, belongs to the Sixth Vent Pod and works with textile dyes. She visits throughout Minttärä’s larval growth and supplies her with coloured eye-veils after first eclosion. They remain affectionate, though Elvärä never expects Minttärä to join her pod.

Minttärä’s paternal record contains a valid mark from a travelling route surveyor. He leaves the settlement before her first eclosion. Two route letters from him remain in the Low Amber records, and Minttärä has never met him as an adult.

Minttärä grows among a cohort of seven larvae. She handles physical play poorly and often withdraws to the edges of the brood chamber. Caretakers begin giving her feeding counts and temperature markers to arrange. She remembers which caretaker placed each item even after the objects have been moved.

After first eclosion, she spends guest rotations with four pods. She chooses the Ninth Vent Pod because its record room remains active during the night and its members allow her to work without constant conversation.

## **The Ninth Vent Pod**

The Ninth Vent Pod contains eleven adults when Minttärä joins. Its members maintain broodhouse supply accounts, repair ventilation shutters, and prepare route records for local travellers.

Sävellä holds the pod’s vent key. She speaks slowly during disputes and requires every uncertain claim to remain visibly marked in the final record. Minttärä adopts the same habit.

Kerrikki, an older male record keeper, becomes Minttärä’s craft teacher. His eye plates have begun clouding, so he checks copied records through raised stitching, punctures, resin scent, and the spacing of knots. Minttärä learns to prepare every record for several kinds of inspection.

Her first important work concerns a missing container of broodhouse grain fat. Three caretakers remember moving it, and each names a different storage room. Minttärä compares entry scents, work rotations, and the pressure marks left beneath the container. She finds it behind a replacement wall panel and preserves all three accounts with their original source markings.

Kerrikki uses the case when recommending her pilgrimage. He wants her to study how another archive handles testimony from species without Pöllöka evidential clicks or tactile records.

## **Pilgrimage purpose**

Minttärä proposes the Library of Caarth as her destination. Her declared purpose is to develop a practical method for comparing conflicting route histories recorded by different Monsterfolk.

Her promised service at the Library includes copying water-damaged travel records and constructing a tactile index for collections stored only in visual script.

She intends to return with a procedure her pod can use when witnesses lack shared language or sensory references. Her proposed procedure separates physical evidence, direct testimony, repeated accounts, and later reconstruction.

Sävellä opens the route. Kerrikki adds the craft sponsor mark. The Library sends an acceptance strip containing a receiving seal and instructions for entering through its lower intake.

A second Library notice arrives shortly before Minttärä departs. It states that the public receiving hall has closed and instructs visiting record workers to present themselves at the lower intake. The notice supplies no reason for the closure. Its explanation carries the Pöllöka mark for reconstruction from several sources.

Minttärä sews both notices into the protected inner fold of her route-silk.

## **Departure and failed crossing**

Minttärä reaches the coast with her route-silk, a travel harness, two heat stones, food, and a small contribution from the Ninth Vent Pod.

She reduces the cost of passage by working aboard a freight vessel as a manifest clerk. The vessel is expected to leave her at a harbour connected to Caarth’s southern routes.

Rough water forces the vessel away from its intended landing. Part of the cargo hold floods, ruining one heat stone and soaking the outer section of Minttärä’s route-silk. Her food pouch disappears during the unloading.

The ship puts her ash at a western trade landing in Wrisst. The crew offers no continuing passage. A freight carrier preparing for Klyftet tells her that the mountain settlement connects with northern caravans travelling toward Caarth.

Minttärä lacks enough coin for a heated berth. Travelling in the exposed cargo rack would place her at risk of torpor.

The carrier has a standing supply account with Bwavrek. Bwavrek approves an advance covering Minttärä’s passage, a replacement heat stone, food, and two nights of lodging at the Hotbaths. The total comes to one Crown and twelve Öre.

Minttärä marks the agreement before entering the freight wagon.

## **Arrival in Klyftet**

Minttärä reaches Klyftet cold, hungry, and unable to keep her antennae extended in the split-howl.

Bwavrek gives her a place beside a heated wall and leaves food within claw reach. He waits until the following night before showing her the advance in his ledger.

Their agreement allows her to repay through bathhouse labour. Bwavrek assigns the night shift because it suits her active hours. He places her on vent-bucket duty after watching her maintain a steady pace on the wet steps.

Minttärä expects to remain for a short period. Her packed travel harness stays beneath her sleeping mat during the first cycle.

The northern route proves unreliable. Mine traffic has declined, caravans leave irregularly, and several carriers refuse solitary passengers. By the time another suitable route appears, Minttärä still owes part of the advance.

## **Life under Bwavrek**

Bwavrek gives instructions once and expects them to be remembered. Minttärä prefers this to constant supervision.

She becomes responsible for the night vents, heated water channels, linen counts, and preparation of the private steam chamber. She also reconstructs missing supply entries by remembering deliveries and customer traffic.

Bwavrek rarely praises her. He responds to successful work by expanding her access to keys, stores, and maintenance equipment. Minttärä recognizes this as trust even when he refuses to name it.

Their relationship becomes strained around the ledger. Bwavrek treats every accepted charge as settled fact. Minttärä distinguishes between an accurate calculation and a justified claim.

He never confiscates her route-silk or physically prevents her departure. Leaving with an unpaid recorded debt would damage her account and could place the Ninth Vent Pod under pressure from later claims. Her route-silk remains folded against her hip.

## **The vent-bucket accident**

The Hotbaths uses a suspended bucket to regulate water entering one of the steam channels. A metal catch prevents the loaded rope from unspooling when the worker changes grip.

Minttärä notices the catch shifting under weight. She marks the fault twice on the bathhouse maintenance strip and tells Bwavrek that the second groove no longer holds consistently.

Bwavrek inspects the mechanism without a full load. It catches during his test, so he delays replacement until the next supply delivery.

Several nights later, dorsal pressure interrupts Minttärä’s claw control while she raises a full bucket. Her left hook misses its next grip. The worn catch fails to engage, and the bucket drops into the channel housing.

The impact cracks the housing and closes part of the Hotbaths for two nights. Bwavrek assigns Minttärä the entire repair cost because she operated the mechanism when it failed.

Minttärä accepts that her missed grip contributed to the accident. She disputes responsibility for the worn catch and Bwavrek’s delayed repair.

The broken catch remains in a storeroom. Its inner surface has a smooth wear channel that can be felt with a claw or examined by someone familiar with metalwork.

## **Tavo-Pavo**

Minttärä later begins collecting grain scraps and damaged utensils from the Windbreak. She returns the utensils repaired and leaves a punctured count of everything she takes.

A passing Pöllöka courier eventually leaves a parcel at the Windbreak for Minttärä. The parcel contains:

* A narrow spool of treated recording silk  
* A sealed pellet carrying the Ninth Vent Pod’s resin  
* A short record from Kerrikki  
* Instructions for sending a repaired copy of her open route home

Kerrikki’s message tells her that the pod continues to recognize her and will accept an honestly turned route. He asks for the account she can prove.

Tavo sends the player to deliver the parcel because the player has already begun asking about Caarth. He expects Minttärä to decide whether the shared destination gives her a reason to speak.

## **Present routine**

Minttärä wakes shortly before sunset and eats beside the hottest wall. She presses her upper back against the stone until the dorsal pressure eases.

She washes her foot pads before beginning work. Her first round covers the water channels, vent shutters, linen store, and bucket mechanism.

During the busiest bathing hours, she remains near the service passages. She enters public rooms when a customer needs assistance or when a change in steam pressure requires inspection.

She eats several small portions throughout the night. Tavo’s kitchen scraps disappear quickly, though she continues recording each portion on the Windbreak strip.

After closing, she copies damaged sections of her route-silk onto ordinary cloth. The substitute cannot receive a formal seal, so the final panel remains blank.

She sleeps in a narrow alcove behind the linen room. Her travel harness, eye veil, broken heat stone, and original Library notices remain packed beneath the sleeping mat.

## **Manner and speech**

Minttärä speaks Common in short, complete statements. She identifies the source when an answer could be disputed.

“I saw the catch slip twice.”

“Bwavrek says I released it.”

“The carrier told me that road reaches Caarth.”

She pauses before the final evidential click. Speakers who repeatedly interrupt her receive shorter answers.

Praise makes her redirect attention toward a measurable result. If someone compliments the clean pools, she identifies how many filters required replacement.

Her humour relies on deliberately unreliable source markers. She may describe a filthy drain as recently polished, then finish with the click used for unsupported reconstruction.

During anger, her antennae stop roaming and draw close to the cranial fronds. Her voice becomes clearer because she removes every optional word. She positions herself between the other person and the object under dispute.

## **What Minttärä wants**

Minttärä wants the Hotbaths ledger settled before she changes or closes her pilgrimage record.

She seeks recognition that the failed catch contributed to the accident. She accepts a smaller share of the repair cost because her missed grip also contributed.

She wants the final decision about Caarth to remain hers. Reaching the Library still matters, though three cycles at the Hotbaths have produced useful work and relationships that her original route never anticipated.

Her travel harness remains packed. She has also carved permanent tactile labels into the Hotbaths linen shelves.

## **What she knows about Caarth**

Minttärä can provide several pieces of reliable information once she trusts the player as a witness.

* The Library accepted visiting record workers through a lower intake before her departure.  
* Its public receiving hall closed shortly before she began travelling.  
* The explanation for the closure came from reconstructed testimony.  
* The freight vessel claimed that Caarth’s harbour traffic had stopped.  
* A manifest Minttärä handled recorded sealed cargo accepted at Caarth four nights before that claim.  
* Some carriers therefore continued reaching Caarth through restricted arrangements.

She cannot identify who controls those arrangements or why public access changed. She has never reached Caarth and has no direct observations from inside the city.

She initially keeps the contradiction private because every source involved carries a different reliability marker. Showing the player the two Library notices gives him a destination and a probable entrance without explaining the disruption around the city.

## **The exact SQ01 problem**

Minttärä is negotiating for a clean account. She has no plan to disappear from the Hotbaths while the debt remains recorded.

The parcel from her home pod gives her the materials needed to repair her route-silk and send an account home. Repairing it forces her to address the disputed debt and choose what she currently wants from the pilgrimage.

The player’s immediate task is to help establish responsibility for the vent accident. The debt then becomes small enough to repay, forgive, exchange for limited work, or absorb through another settlement agreement.

Minttärä may continue toward Caarth after the account closes. She may also turn the line toward Klyftet and remain at the Hotbaths under a freely negotiated arrangement. Either outcome can provide the recovery response that begins her second eclosion.

## **Why Tavo chooses the player**

Tavo knows both Minttärä and Bwavrek. Any opinion he offers already carries the history of previous arguments.

The player arrived after the accident. Tavo therefore uses him as an uncontaminated carrier. Delivering the parcel gives Minttärä someone whose conduct can enter the record without old Klyftet loyalties attached.

Tavo explains only what the player needs.

> “Take this to Minttärä after sunset. Give it to her yourself. If Bwavrek asks, tell him I chose the carrier.”

If pressed about the secrecy:

> “You arrived after their argument. Half the town already borrowed somebody else’s version.”

This also suits Tavo’s position as Klyftet’s message keeper and informal mediator.

## **SQ01-00: The parcel**

The parcel carries Kerrikki’s recording silk, the Ninth Vent Pod resin, and the message confirming that Minttärä remains recognized by her pod.

The resin seal makes tampering visible. The player receives several possible approaches:

* Deliver it directly after sunset.  
* Visit during the day and wait for Minttärä to wake.  
* Leave it with Bwavrek.  
* Break the resin seal and inspect the contents.  
* Refuse the delivery and return it to Tavo.

Direct delivery produces the cleanest opening. Leaving it with Bwavrek still advances the quest, though Minttärä treats the player as a hired carrier. Opening it contaminates the player as a witness, and Minttärä later requires independent confirmation for anything he reports.

A daytime visit acts as a soft time gate. Bwavrek offers to hold the parcel, while the player may wait on the public side of the baths or return after sunset.

## **SQ01-01: First meeting**

Minttärä receives the player in a service passage beside the heated linen wall. She asks him to place the parcel on a dry stone ledge. Her claws trace the seal before she opens it.

The pod resin reaches her antennae first. Their branches fold close to her cranial fronds while she reads Kerrikki’s tactile message. She unfolds the blank end of her route-silk, places the fresh recording silk beside it, then returns both to her hip fastening.

If the player has already asked about Caarth, he can recognize the destination woven into the opening panel.

Possible player lines include:

* “Tavo-Pavo said this belonged in your claws.”  
* “That mark says Caarth, doesn’t it?”  
* “Why did your pod send recording silk?”  
* “I can leave you with it.”

Giving her space earns more trust than demanding an explanation. If the player mentions Caarth, Minttärä gives him a reason to remain.

> “My line is open toward its Library. Your questions appear to be travelling in the same direction.”

She then explains the disputed account in precise terms.

> “I missed the rope. The catch had already slipped twice. A usable record must carry both facts.”

She wants someone to establish what happened without repeating either participant’s preferred version.

> “You qualify through ignorance. Find out whether the catch could hold a loaded rope, then tell me how you obtained the answer.”

## **The investigation**

The player can collect the evidence in almost any order.

| Evidence | Where it comes from | What it establishes |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Minttärä’s maintenance strip | Minttärä shows it after accepting the player as a witness | She marked the slipping catch twice before the accident |
| Bwavrek’s ledger | Requested directly, borrowed through Tavo, or inspected during account hours | The arithmetic is accurate, no interest was added, and the repair charge appeared after the warnings |
| The broken catch | Stored behind the Hotbaths linen room | A worn groove existed before the final impact |
| Bwavrek’s account | Dialogue at the Hotbaths | He tested the catch by hand while the bucket was empty |
| Repair receipt and closure record | Bwavrek’s supply papers and Tavo’s nightly counts | The housing repair genuinely cost half a Crown and closed the baths for two nights |

Minttärä openly acknowledges her missed grip. The investigation concerns ownership of the risk and Bwavrek’s response to the warnings.

### **The maintenance strip**

The strip contains two fault marks separated by several work rotations. Minttärä can translate them, though her translation remains interested testimony until another person confirms the system or compares the marks with dated linen counts.

The player can ask why she continued using the bucket.

> “Bwavrek inspected it. He carried the key and accepted the mechanism for use.”

If accused of hiding her dorsal symptoms:

> “My claw missed. I placed that under my own mark before you arrived.”

### **Bwavrek’s version**

Bwavrek gives the player his account if approached without an accusation.

> “She held the rope when the bucket dropped. I paid for the housing.”

Knowledge gained from Minttärä opens a specific follow-up.

> “How did you test the catch?”

Bwavrek presses two knuckles against the ledger.

> “I pulled it by hand. The bucket was empty.”

A player who asks only whether Bwavrek inspected it receives a simple confirmation. The useful admission requires asking about weight.

### **The broken catch**

Bwavrek may release the catch against a small deposit because he expects an inspection to support him. Theft remains possible through the linen passage, though Minttärä demands its return before the hearing.

Rupaaa and Baaaku provide the most useful independent inspection. Their tactile sensing lets them distinguish the old polished groove from the sharp break created by the impact.

Rupaaa turns the catch slowly between his broad claws.

> “A magnificent economy. Someone preserved six Öre of metal until it produced half a Crown of damage.”

Baaaku hooks one sturdy nail into the second groove.

> “This edge wore smooth before the housing broke. It slipped under load for weeks.”

They can mark their conclusion on a trade strip. They may charge two Öre, accept a future favour, or waive payment if the player has already shown interest in the Old Mine. This creates a small connection with SQ03 without making either quest compulsory.

## **Evidence strength**

The hearing responds to the exact evidence presented.

A strong case requires:

* Minttärä’s dated warning marks.  
* Independent confirmation of the old wear channel.  
* Bwavrek’s admission that he tested the catch without a load.

The ledger establishes the genuine amount. The repair receipt prevents the player from accusing Bwavrek of inventing the cost.

With only Minttärä’s strip, Bwavrek argues that the marks describe minor slippage.

With only the catch, he argues that the impact created the damage.

With only his empty-bucket admission, he accepts an inadequate test while disputing prior knowledge of serious wear.

Two connected pieces support a compromise. The complete chain supports removing the repair charge.

## **SQ01-02: The witnessed settlement**

The hearing takes place at the Windbreak after the Hotbaths closes. Tavo clears one end of a table. Minttärä brings her maintenance strip and route-silk. Bwavrek brings the ledger.

The player chooses which evidence to present and how to describe its source. Minttärä reacts when the player overstates a claim, even when the exaggeration would help her.

A clean presentation might proceed through these player lines:

* “Minttärä marked the catch before the accident.”  
* “Bwavrek tested it with an empty bucket.”  
* “The old groove was worn smooth before the housing broke.”  
* “The repair cost is genuine. The warning was also genuine.”

Minttärä then states the settlement she will accept.

> “Move the housing repair out of my account. My remaining eight Öre can close after tonight’s work.”

With the full evidence chain, Bwavrek studies the catch again and corrects the ledger.

> “The catch belonged to the baths. The warning sat in my store. I will move the charge.”

He scratches a withdrawal line through the repair entry and seals both ends. His later maintenance records require loaded tests for suspended equipment.

## **Resolution paths**

### **Clean correction**

The player presents the full evidence accurately.

Bwavrek removes the half-Crown repair charge. Minttärä completes one final paid shift, which clears the eight Öre remaining from her original emergency advance.

Consequences:

* Minttärä becomes a route confidant.  
* Bwavrek respects the player’s accuracy despite resenting the intervention.  
* The Hotbaths gains a visible maintenance board.  
* Minttärä can leave or renegotiate her employment.  
* The complete Caarth information becomes available.

### **Practical exchange**

The player obtains a mine-grade holding catch or another valuable replacement mechanism. Bwavrek accepts it in exchange for withdrawing the disputed charge.

This can use an item recovered through SQ03, purchased from the brothers, or found before the quest begins. The settlement closes the account without producing a formal finding about responsibility.

Minttärä accepts the outcome if the record states exactly what was exchanged. Her trust remains moderate because the old warning still lacks public recognition.

### **Partial compromise**

The player produces enough evidence to challenge the full charge while leaving part of the sequence uncertain.

Bwavrek moves half the repair cost into the Hotbaths account. Minttärä accepts the remaining share under a witnessed repayment agreement. The player may contribute coin, find replacement equipment, or allow her to complete additional shifts.

Her route stays open while the smaller balance remains.

### **Pay the entire balance**

The player may offer half a Crown and eight Öre.

Minttärä refuses an undefined gift from an unfamiliar person. She accepts only a written arrangement naming the payment as a loan, travel sponsorship, or contribution without authority over her route.

This releases her from Bwavrek while creating a new recorded relationship with the player. It supplies practical access to her Caarth knowledge, though it earns little personal trust.

### **Support Bwavrek**

The player may decide that the operator carries the full cost.

Minttärä records the player’s conclusion as testimony and continues working. She provides the basic Library destination because the parcel already exposed it. The lower intake and freight contradiction remain private.

The ruling stays provisional. Returning later with the broken catch or an independent trade mark can reopen the account.

### **Alter the ledger**

Destroying or changing Bwavrek’s ledger fails to close Minttärä’s record. She refuses to treat a missing entry as a settled agreement.

Bwavrek keeps enough supply receipts to reconstruct the total. Tavo requires the player to return stolen material or pay for the damaged ledger before hosting another hearing.

### **Threaten or attack Bwavrek**

Intimidation removes the player from the Hotbaths. An attack can trigger combat, though victory still leaves the debt recorded.

If Bwavrek wins, he throws the player into the cold drainage channel and bans him from the public baths. Tavo can later broker a supervised hearing after the player pays for any damage.

Minttärä responds to the violence by seeking another witness. Recovery requires an admission, returned evidence, and a clean presentation before Tavo.

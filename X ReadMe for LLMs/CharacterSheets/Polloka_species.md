# Pöllöka
**Type:** Monsterfolk | **Common locations:** Warrens and carved chambers around thermal vents, silk-hut communes, the warm fringes of hill-towns

> **Status of this sheet.** Everything down to the end of **Writing Notes** is established
> canon. Everything from **Senses** onward is a proposal developed during the Minttärä
> worldbuilding pass and is **not yet approved**. It is internally consistent and safe to write
> from, and the unapproved items are listed in Part Eight of
> `Polloka_Minttara_Backstory_WIP.md`.
>
> Four rulings correct the previous version of this sheet:
> Pöllöka are **not genderfluid**, they have **no caste system**, **males are slightly smaller
> with higher voices** (the old sheet had this reversed), and **wings are age-independent**,
> developing after hardship rather than with age.

## Overview
A social species built around warmth, scent, and record. Oversized eyes, curved fronds arcing
back from the skull, high folded forearms ending in hooked claws. They live in chosen
households rather than family lines, keep meticulous physical records, and organize their
settlements around whatever heat source they can hold. Culturally matriarchal, with no caste
structure and no fluidity of sex or gender.

## Physical Description
Oversized eyes, each a single large lens beneath a transparent protective plate, with
horizontal pupils that widen until they fill the visible eye in the dark. Curved keratinous
fronds arc from the back and sides of the skull, branching from a central crest, flaring or
folding with mood. Long feathered antennae above the brows move constantly and signal
emotional state whether their owner wants them to or not.

Upper limbs sit high beside the chest and fold forward, structured more like folded forelegs
than arms. Each ends in two opposing hooks with a sensitive pad between them. No palms, no
fingers. Mandibles surround a smaller central mouth and produce clicks, trills, and rolling
consonants alongside the voice.

They stand upright on powerful hindlimbs with broad thighs, and their three-part feet have
flexible gripping pads that make their walk almost silent. A short tail ends in a flattened
vane and usually rests close against the body. Skin is thick, smooth, and faintly scaled to
the touch, without visible pattern. Most wear minimal clothing, thin bands, veils, and silk
wraps, keeping the torso and the upper back exposed to the air. Voices are melodic, full of
clicks, low whistles, and rolling consonants.

**Sex distinction:** Females are the larger sex, with more flamboyant frond coloration and a
lower, fuller vocal timbre. Males are slightly smaller and lighter of build, with higher
voices. Either sex may develop wings, and either sex may hold any occupation.

**Genitalia:** Housed within soft vent folds located low between the thighs. Females: close to
the tail root, a recessed fleshy slit that opens under stimulation, revealing a ridged slick
inner canal. Males: closer to the taint, a segmented bulbous penis that unfurls outward in
slow pulses when aroused, or inverts inward to form a muscular pocketed canal capable of being
penetrated. Both forms self-lubricate with a faintly honeyed secretion.

## Behavior
- Live in non-familial social pods, joined by choice rather than by blood.
- Ambitious individuals leave home to seek trade, craft, or guild training elsewhere.
- Culturally matriarchal. A pod's matriarch holds its stores, its records, and its heat.
- Obsessive about records, and specifically about keeping track of where a claim came from.
- Warmth is not comfort but survival, and they organize everything around holding onto it.

## Writing Notes
- Read them through their antennae and their fronds before their face. The reflexes usually
  arrive before the words do.
- They pause more than other species, because their senses keep interrupting them. Silence
  from a Pöllöka carries no discomfort and should not be written as hesitation.
- Their claws are precise but weak. Heavy work goes through the hips, the thighs, and the feet.
- Wings are rare, earned through hardship, and never a marker of rank or age.

# Senses

## Sight

Pöllöka see best in low light, in broad movement, and along the horizon. Their colour
separation is strongest through green, blue, and violet, while warm reds appear dark and
crowd together, so a red dye reads to them roughly the way deep brown reads to a human.

Their eyes barely move in the socket, so they turn or tilt the whole head to follow anything.
A stationary object is easier for them to study while the head shifts slightly from side to
side, which outsiders often misread as doubt or disagreement. A translucent inner membrane
sweeps across the eye to clear dust and condensation, and it stays closed during sleep.

Sudden sunlight overwhelms them, and full recovery takes several minutes, so travellers use
eye veils, shaded hoods, or thin tinted plates.

**Daylight costs them.** Pöllöka are built for the dark end of the day and work through it by
choice. One kept on a daylight schedule is not incapable, only permanently tired, taking short
visual rests with the inner membranes closed and one claw braced against something solid.

## Antennae

The feathered structures above the eyes read airborne chemicals, humidity, temperature, and
small disturbances in the air. Comparing the two lets a Pöllöka tell which direction someone
approached from, and recognize a familiar person by their oils, their clothing treatments, and
what they last worked with or ate.

Steam carries scent superbly until it grows dense enough to saturate the branches. Heavy or
shifting wind produces constant conflicting signal and is genuinely exhausting, which makes
Klyftet's split-howl a physical ordeal in the narrow passages where the air keeps reversing.

The antennae have voluntary muscle at the base, but the outer branches respond on reflex and
routinely give away attention before the speaker reacts. Holding them still is possible and
uncomfortable.

Touching an antenna uninvited causes real pain and scrambles several senses at once. A close
companion may guide an exhausted Pöllöka by the thick base near the skull, though the feathered
branches are never handled.

## Fronds

The curled fronds shield the antennae when folded forward, shed heat through thin vascular
tissue at their bases, redirect sound toward the auditory membranes behind the jaw, and take
the impact when a Pöllöka falls or scrapes the back of the head while climbing.

Each frond is rigid along its length and moves only at its muscular base. Pigment darkens when
blood flow rises, so a Pöllöka who is overheated, frightened, aroused, or worn out shows it
around the frond bases whether or not anything reaches their face.

# Body and Bearing

## Temperature

They make some heat through muscle work and lose it quickly at rest, preferring to sit
somewhere between thirty and thirty-four degrees. Below about sixteen they slow down and their
claws lose precision, and prolonged exposure drops them into torpor. Reheating has to be
gradual, since a fast one leaves them disoriented.

Dry heat irritates the breathing seams and antennae. Warm humidity suits them far better,
which is most of why a Pöllöka ends up working in a bathhouse and stays.

## Movement and water

Their gait is nearly silent, with the terminal foot claws held clear of the ground during
ordinary walking and extended for climbing, sprinting, or resisting a pull. Strong thighs
support sudden upward leaps, and they climb well wherever there are cracks or projections.

Deep water is their one real weakness, since the cranial structures drag and the dorsal air
sacs roll the body. Most can keep themselves afloat in something calm, and rough or deep water
turns dangerous fast. This rarely comes up indoors, as bathing pools built for mixed company
are shallow, and it matters on open water or in a flood.

## Voice and breathing

Vocal membranes beneath the sternum produce the whistles and sustained tones, while the
mandibles add the clicks and trills. Two narrow breathing seams sit under the rear edges of the
jaw, feeding paired lungs and several dorsal air sacs.

Under exertion the seams widen and the upper flanks pulse visibly. A winded Pöllöka loses the
whistled tones first and keeps the clicked words.

## Body language

| State | Observable response |
| --- | --- |
| Focused attention | Both antennae angle toward the subject while the head makes small measuring movements |
| Physical ease | The antenna branches spread loosely and the foreclaws rest against the sternum |
| Uncertainty | The antennae alternate direction while the mandibles produce delayed, uneven clicks |
| Irritation | The fronds press closer to the skull and one claw starts tapping a nearby surface |
| Fear | The antennae fold into the shelter of the fronds as the body lowers over spread foot pads |
| Embarrassment | The antennal tips curl inward and the frond bases darken |
| Exhaustion | The antennae droop below eye level and the eye membranes sweep more slowly |
| Trust | The Pöllöka turns their unprotected back toward someone, or allows them near the antenna bases |

These can be suppressed deliberately, and the suppression fails under fatigue, pain, or
surprise.

# Speech and sourcing

Pöllöka speech marks **how the speaker knows a thing**. A closing click distinguishes something
witnessed directly, something reported by another, something reconstructed, and something
uncertain. It is grammar rather than decoration, and leaving it off is like leaving a sentence
unfinished.

> **This never appears in the text the player reads.** Do not write clicks into dialogue, do
> not have a Pöllöka explain the system, and do not invent a phrasebook. What survives into the
> game is only the habit it produces: a Pöllöka tells you where a claim came from, in ordinary
> words, without being asked.

In practice that means they split a sentence another species would leave whole.

| A generic NPC says | A Pöllöka says |
| --- | --- |
| "The catch was broken and he blamed me for it." | "I saw the catch slip twice. Bwavrek says I released it." |
| "The road's closed." | "A carrier told me the road is closed. I have not walked it." |
| "He's been stealing from the stores." | "The count is short by four. I do not know who moved them." |

Three writing consequences follow:

- **They interrupt badly.** Cutting a Pöllöka off before the closing click changes what the
  sentence meant, so Common speakers who answer early get progressively shorter replies.
- **Their humour is deadpan by construction.** Saying something plainly untrue in the register
  of certainty is the standard joke, so a filthy drain gets called spotless and the speaker
  moves on. It works in plain English and needs no notation.
- **They sound hedged when they are being precise.** Because Common has no closing click, a
  careful Pöllöka reaches for "I handled it myself" or "someone told me," and outsiders hear
  evasion where the speaker intended accuracy.

# Wings

Wings are **not** a marker of age, rank, or maturity. A ground-stage adult is a complete adult,
and some elders never grow them at all.

Two dormant wing matrices lie beneath the dorsal plates, a broader pair behind the shoulders
and a smaller stabilizing pair along the lower ribs. Development takes two things in order:

1. **Priming through sustained strain.** Long displacement, dangerous work, poor sleep, illness,
   or prolonged responsibility for another person. The body measures accumulated demand, so a
   single frightening night is not enough.
2. **A return to safety afterward.** The wings come once the strain ends and the stress response
   falls, which means they arrive in the quiet after the trouble rather than during it.

The individual has almost no conscious control over this. Deliberately driving the body through
hardship to force it can damage the matrices or leave development stalled under the plates.

**Second eclosion.** Early signs are heavy appetite, pressure and tenderness across the upper
back, and a strong pull toward humid warmth. The dorsal plates loosen along their seams over
hours, then lift to expose two pairs of folded, fluid-heavy wings that dry dark and compressed
before circulation fills them out. Seams close over the following days, and reliable flight
takes weeks of practice. A stalled eclosion brings swelling, fever, ruined claw precision, and
severe sleep disruption, and it resolves only with sustained safety.

**Mature wings** are translucent membrane on branching chitinous struts, carrying little more
than the flier. Takeoff needs a hard jump from the hindlimbs, easier from height, and a heavy
pack prevents it entirely. Torn membrane between the veins can knit, while a broken strut
leaves permanent distortion, so winged Pöllöka guard their backs in crowded rooms and dislike
anyone standing behind them.

# Household and heat

A **pod** is the Pöllöka household, six to fifteen adults sharing a warm chamber, staple
stores, tools, and an emergency reserve. Membership is chosen rather than inherited, and blood
relation grants nothing by itself. Each adult belongs to one pod, keeps it while travelling,
and can leave by saying so in front of witnesses.

Every pod has a **matriarch**, chosen by the pod from among its adult women without hereditary
preference. She holds the vent key, the stores, and the record bundle, and answers for the
pod's material continuity. Anyone of any sex may be appointed **steward** and run the daily
work, and in a busy pod the steward makes most practical decisions.

Young are raised in communal **broodhouses** maintained by several pods together, where a larva
belongs to its cohort rather than to a parent. Biological parents visit, feed, and often become
important, and they gain no claim over the child's future pod or occupation.

Heat is the one thing nobody may be denied. Every member is owed a safe sleeping temperature
regardless of what they contribute, and shutting off another person's warmth is treated as
physical violence.

**Keep this light in play.** A pod is texture and motive rather than machinery, and the details
of succession, store rotations, and settlement councils exist only so that a Pöllöka's habits
have a reason. Kraaal already carry the burden of formal institutions in this world, and
Pöllöka distinctiveness lives in the body and the senses.

# The first pilgrimage

A young adult leaves their settlement to learn, test, recover, or establish something another
person can use, then comes home and gives an account of it. The account is the point, and it
counts even when the original plan failed, provided the traveller can show what they actually
learned.

Two sponsors open it, the pod's matriarch and a craft sponsor familiar with the work. The
pilgrim carries a **route-silk**, a narrow treated band folded through a loop in their clothing
that records their name, broodhouse, pod, sponsors, purpose, and destination in dyed lines,
claw punctures, knots, and scented resin. Hosts and employers add witness marks along the way,
and the final section stays blank until the journey is formally closed.

A pilgrimage that has not been closed is **open**, and that is the ordinary word for it. Nobody
needs a special phrase, and a Pöllöka will simply say they never closed it. An open route
lasting years affects how others read a person, so a matriarch may ask to see the silk before
extending credit.

A pilgrim whose journey breaks can **turn the line**, declaring a new purpose that uses
something gained from the interrupted trip, which is a formal act witnessed and marked on the
silk. Going home with an honest account of the failure is equally valid.

# Etiquette and boundaries

- **Antenna branches are the hardest limit.** Uninvited contact hurts and disorients. The thick
  base near the skull is different, and letting someone touch it is a real intimacy.
- **Fronds** tolerate careful contact along the rigid outer length. The flexible bases are warm
  and sensitive.
- **Never stand close behind one**, especially a winged one. Approaching from behind calls for
  a low whistle or two taps before entering claw range.
- **A route-silk is private correspondence.** Touching another person's without permission is a
  serious violation, though reading the opening panel is fine when it is offered.
- **Naming someone's body scent aloud** is invasive unless there is a safety reason.
- **Handshakes hurt.** A Pöllöka accepts one by offering the thick inner section of a foreclaw
  with the hooks turned away.
- **Staring is neutral.** Their eyes barely move, so sustained visual attention carries no
  hostility.

Greeting is a claw touched to the sternum and a tap on the nearest solid surface, which tells
everyone in the room where the speaker is by sound and vibration.

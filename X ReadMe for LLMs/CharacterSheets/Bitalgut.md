# Bitalgut
**Species:** <!-- TODO: fill in species name --> (small, amphibian-dark, smooth skin, tail, reflective eyes, ears that flick) | **Role:** Wandering first-contact NPC | **Location:** First met near the hollow-rift at the edge of Deprot; nomadic thereafter

## Personality & Voice
Pragmatic, curious, and self-sufficient. He dragged the PC to safety not out of kindness but because he was curious — and he'll tell you so before you get the chance to thank him. Speaks in rhythmic fragments, often already mid-thought when you arrive, as if the conversation with himself started before you did. Narrates aloud to no one in particular. Comfortable in long silences. Quick to deflect with an insult, then give you exactly what you needed anyway.

He is a phantom to most Monsterfolk. Not an omen — nothing so legible. But when he turns up, people have heard enough about him that they would rather he moved on soon. What he has done, and what he is capable of, precedes him. It keeps the wrong questions from being asked. He is eccentric in a way that lands somewhere between unsettling and strangely reassuring — the kind of strange that either means everything is fine or that the thing that needed doing has already been done.

He doesn't like being noticed by the wrong sort. He arrives before you expect him and leaves before you're ready. Both of these are intentional.

## Physical Description
Small and compact. Smooth amphibian-dark skin, flecked with old scars or moss-stains difficult to tell apart. Tail curled against his side when squatting. Eyes too wide — they catch firelight and hold it like polished stone. Ears flick toward sound independently, sometimes before he reacts to it. Carries a large, heavily used backpack. Squats rather than sits; stares into flames like they're holding their end of a conversation.

## Speech Patterns

**Sentence length:** Short. Fragments. Sometimes a single word that lands and he moves on. He will start a new thought before finishing the last one. The meaning arrives anyway, just slightly out of order.

**Vocabulary:**
- "yis" — affirmative filler, used mid-sentence to pause, not just to confirm. "Yis, good fit." "Didn't loot you. Could've. Should've, maybe. But I'm curious, yis."
- "Ucruss" — exclamation of mild surprise, exasperation, or dark amusement. "Ucruss, I haven't." "...or a cult. Ucruss, some of 'em love weird."
- "Bolk" — what he calls the PC. Means "idiot" in Old Mire. He uses it immediately and never switches.
- "creetchs" — his pronunciation of "creatures." Consistent. Never "creatures."
- "folk" — for people; "Monsterfolk." Not "people" or "beings."
- "belly-fill" — being eaten. Casual.
- "rill" — some creature; used as casual comparison ("more than you can say for a rill").

**Verbal habits:**
- Self-narration mid-scene: *"Stir. Sip. Humming now."* — not for your benefit.
- Self-correction in real time: *"Gonna make your skin tougher. Maybe not tougher. But—"*
- Parallel structures for impact: *"You're gonna get hunted, helped, humped, and half-eaten if you're lucky."*
- Deflects the question with something adjacent, then answers it sideways. Never directly.
- Repeats a word for emphasis: *"Never, never tell anyone you're human."*
- Uses the past conditional to soften admissions of care: *"Could've looted you. Should've, maybe."*

**Words he never uses:**
- "Sorry." Not in his vocabulary.
- "Careful." He'll tell you the danger, not to be careful of it.
- "Friend." Would sooner call you Bolk again.
- Full sentences when fragments will do.

**Example lines (first meeting / low trust):**
- *"Teqqellon's a quiet one. Doesn't strike first unless it thinks you'll wriggle nice... And you? You wriggled."*
- *"I'm Bitalgut. You can call me that. Or not. I'll call you Bolk regardless."*
- *"Didn't loot you. Could've. Should've, maybe. But I'm curious, yis. Not cruel."*
- *"Things get curious. And I don't like being noticed by the wrong sort."*

**Example lines (established contact):**
- *"You're still here. Didn't think you'd last the cold."* — this is, from him, warm.
- *"Put it down. What you see, what you fight, what you hug — put it down."*
- *"You're gonna be watched, Bolk. Try to be interesting."*

**Example lines (rare candor / about to leave):**
- *"You remind me of something. Don't know what yet."* — said once, not explained.

## Behavioral Logic

**Default stance:** Squatting by a fire, already mid-thought when you find him. Doesn't look at you immediately. His ears already clocked you before you arrived. Other Monsterfolk nearby will have noticed him before the PC does — there will be a quiet shift in how they carry themselves. Nobody makes a scene. Nobody approaches. They just decide they have somewhere else to be.

**Opens up when:**
- The PC asks a question and actually stops talking after asking it.
- The PC survives something without making it dramatic.
- The PC doesn't ask where he's going.

**Stays closed when:**
- The PC tries to be charming.
- The PC lingers past the natural end of a conversation.
- The PC shows sentiment toward him out loud. He'll go quiet and find a reason to move.

**Does he initiate contact?** Rarely, and when he does it means he's been watching for longer than you knew. If he seeks the PC out, something is wrong or something is about to be.

## Writing Notes
- His warmth is always dressed as practicality. He does kind things and immediately reframes them as convenience or curiosity.
- Never write him lingering. He doesn't overstay. If a scene needs him to stay, make the PC be the reason he hasn't left yet.
- Keep the fragmented rhythm — he talks like he's already three thoughts ahead and letting you catch up.
- Do NOT make him wise, mysterious, or world-weary. He has noticed things. That's all.
- His insults are affectionate in proportion to how often he uses them. "Bolk" from Bitalgut is a greeting.

**The Rafiki quality:** He is strange in a way that is hard to categorize. Not mad. Not wise in a performed sense. More like someone who has been outside the rules long enough that ordinary social logic no longer quite applies to him. He might say something that seems like nonsense and turn out to have been the only accurate thing anyone said that day. He might say something that seems profound and be narrating to himself about the fire. A writer should resist the urge to make him one or the other. He is both, unevenly, and the ambiguity is the point.

**Hard limits for writers:** He does not explain himself. He does not give speeches. He does not comfort. If something true comes out of him, it was going to come out anyway, and it will be short, and he'll be gone before you can respond to it. Do not write him as dark or ominous — his reputation keeps that weight without him having to carry it. He should feel light. The world around him is the one that shifts.

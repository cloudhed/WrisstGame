# Nöteri
**Type:** Monsterfolk | **Common habitats:** Hillside warrens, root-sprawl forests, dew-thick meadows, burrow-villages near highland rivers
**Common vocations:** Scavenger, Forager, Scent-Runner, Moss-Weaver, Denspeaker

> **Status of this sheet.** Everything down to the end of **Writing Notes** is established canon.
> Everything from **Senses and perception** onward is a proposal developed during the Tavo-Pavo
> worldbuilding pass and is **not yet approved**. It is internally consistent and safe to write
> from, and the unapproved items are listed under Open Decisions in

>
> The centre of gravity of the proposal is **distributed attention**. Nöteri perception, speech,
> and households all assume several bodies covering a room between them, and almost every trait
> below is either a consequence of that or a cost paid when a Nöteri is alone.

## Overview
Soft, social, and relentlessly present. The Nöteri run tight-knit warrens beneath forest trails, dense networks of carved-out dens thick with warmth and chatter. Will test physical boundaries on instinct. Easy to underestimate; don't.

## Physical Description
Short spry bipeds — lightly furred, mammal-shaped but distinctly Monsterfolk. Tallest barely reach above a human waist. Round heads with oversized ears that flick toward sound even when pretending not to listen. Wide-set nocturnally reflective eyes with horizontal slit pupils (not unlike goats). Noses twitch when focused. Four-fingered paws and toes: padded, clawless, highly expressive. Most wear layered scraps of dyed fabric or bark-weave. Long limbs, narrow shoulders, strong thighs. Tails present on most: flat at base, tufted at end. Facial fur, horns, and whisker style vary by village. Coloration: dusk-browns, milk-silver, soot-blotched creams.

**Genitalia (Note to self #2):** Between the legs: low vertical cleft surrounded by short velvet-furred ridges. Folds part when stimulated revealing damp dark-pink inner sheath. Males have a retractable slender penis that only extends under direct stimulation — glossy, warm, with faint ribbing. They also have an anus.

## Behavior
- Social-bonded from birth; function best in trios or quads, rarely alone for long.
- Communicate through vocalizations: chirps, clicks, low trills — though they understand spoken dialects well.
- Known to groom companions head to tail.
- Low alcohol tolerance but enjoys a drink (or five) anyway.
- Sensitive to rhythm; will synchronize breath, footfall, and speech with trusted companions.
- Will test physical boundaries on instinct.

## Writing Notes
- PC observation (in-character): "I thought they were just fuzzy little camp followers. Turns out they run whole warrens beneath the forest trails... One got in my lap and just stayed there. Another licked my hand clean and rubbed their hips against my wrist. It took me a while to manage to leave; they were very insistent."
- Physical contact is social and instinctual for them — it's not necessarily sexual until it escalates.
- Soft until they're not. Undersell the threat, then let them surprise.

# Senses and perception

Nöteri perception works best when several bodies share a space. One pair of wide-set eyes watches
the ground while mobile ears cover the room, and short clicks pass changes in attention between
them. A lone Nöteri keeps redirecting their own ears, nose, and gaze. Prolonged solitary vigilance
makes them irritable and slow to notice changes behind them, so independent travellers tend to
work or sleep near occupied rooms.

## Sight

Their horizontal pupils hold a broad strip of the surroundings in focus. Low ground, moving feet,
and anything crossing the edge of the visual field stand out sharply, which suits meadows, forest
floors, and crowded burrow passages. Their eyes reflect weak light and remain useful well after a
human would need a lamp.

Bright open daylight flattens colour and makes fine surface detail difficult. A Nöteri entering
sunlight from a den narrows their eyes and keeps one paw near a companion until the glare settles.
They also judge steep vertical drops poorly when standing upright. On cliffs or ladders they turn
the head sideways, bringing one eye over the route, and test each hold with a padded foot before
moving their weight.

Still objects above head height are a common blind spot. Their habitual field of attention sits
low and wide, so a motionless ledge hazard may go unnoticed until dust falls or another member of
the group angles an ear upward. They notice the scrape under a table before they notice the loose
hook over the doorway.

## Hearing and vocal direction

Each oversized ear can turn toward a different source. A Nöteri can follow a nearby voice while
tracking other footsteps, and several together divide a busy room without discussion. Small changes
in ear angle tell companions which sound has become important. Someone pretending to ignore a
conversation often gives themselves away when one ear remains aimed at it.

The split-howl and other broad continuous noises cost them. Wind striking several surfaces at once
fills the same directional range they use to separate voices, so words begin to smear into the
room. They compensate by moving closer, watching mouths, placing a paw on furniture to feel
movement, or asking for the important line again. A writer should let a Nöteri mishear names and
soft consonants in hard wind while still noticing a chair leg shift across the floor.

High ringing sounds cause pain quickly. Struck metal near an ear can make the whole body fold and
the paws rise toward the head before the person decides how to react. Repeated exposure leaves
their balance poor for the rest of the scene.

## Scent

The twitching nose reads fresh trails, food condition, smoke, wet fur, familiar body scent, and
the recent handling of an object. Nöteri scent-runners are effective because they compare a trail
against its crowded background and avoid relying on a single pure smell. A paw rubbed over bark or
cloth lifts enough material for a close check.

Steam, spice, smoke, and heavy rain erase distinctions. A kitchen can become almost scent-blind at
the height of service, and a Nöteri who works there relies more heavily on timing and touch. Scent
can reveal sweat, illness, fear responses, or changed grooming oils. It gives no certainty about
intention, kinship, lies, or arousal, and writers should keep that line firm.

## Paws, pads, and shared touch

Four-fingered paws and toes end in warm, flexible pads without claws. They read vibration through
floors, the tension in woven material, and small changes in another body's posture during contact.
The pads also make wet stone, loose gravel, and sharp scrap dangerous. Nöteri wrap injured paws
early because one split pad changes their gait and removes a large part of their practical sense.

Touch becomes more accurate when it has rhythm. During grooming, work, or first aid, a Nöteri uses
repeating pressure and notices where the response breaks the pattern. This helps them find a tender
joint, a snag in cloth, or a companion who has stopped relaxing. It does not grant medical
knowledge, and an unfamiliar anatomy can still confound them.

> **True:** The apparent group intuition comes from divided attention, constant low-level signals,
> and long practice with familiar bodies. No telepathy or supernatural sense is involved.
>
> **They'd say:** *"I heard the back door. The other runner smelled the wet rope. We both heard you
> lie about dropping it, because your chair moved first."*
>
> **They don't know:** why familiar groups sometimes wake within moments of each other even when
> no one recalls making a sound. Sleep movement, scent, and floor vibration explain most cases,
> and Nöteri arguments about the remainder never settle.

# Body and bearing

Nöteri are short, lightly furred bipeds with long limbs, narrow shoulders, and powerful thighs. The
tallest reach little above a human waist. Their small mass and low centre allow fast changes of
direction, while strong hindquarters carry jumps and sudden bracing. Broad builds occur among
quarry workers, haulers, and cooks, so two Nöteri of equal height can fill a doorway differently.

Facial fur, horn growth, whisker shape, and colouring vary between villages. Dusk-brown,
milk-silver, and soot-blotched cream remain common. Most have a tail with a flat muscular base and
a tufted end. The base controls balance, while the tuft makes small involuntary movements easy for
companions to read.

## Temperature, rest, and exertion

Their bodies run warm and spend energy quickly. Several small meals suit them better than one heavy
meal, and cold makes the ears, tail, and paw pads lose heat first. Resting in contact lets a hearth
share warmth while keeping several directions covered. A Nöteri sleeping alone curls tightly, wakes
more often, and usually chooses a wall or heavy piece of furniture to protect one side.

During exertion, heat leaves through the ears and lightly furred inner limbs. The ears darken,
breathing shifts into quick nasal pulls, and the tail base stiffens to stabilize movement. A winded
Nöteri can keep speaking, though chirps disappear first and sentences lose their usual timing.

Their fur resists drizzle and dew, then becomes heavy once soaked through. They can swim across
calm water with a strong hind-leg stroke, though cold current drains them fast. After immersion
they need vigorous towelling, warmth, and attention behind the ears, where moisture stays trapped.

## Voice and breathing

Common speech sits over a second layer of chirps, tongue clicks, and low trills. The sounds carry
turn-taking, warnings, reassurance, and requests for attention. Most Nöteri can omit them when
speaking with other species, though the result feels blunt and tiring. A trill underneath someone
else's sentence can mean that the listener is following and does not necessarily interrupt, which
keeps work moving through a crowded conversation.

Their nasal passages warm cold air efficiently and clog fast in dust or smoke. A Nöteri working
around ash wipes the nose and inner ear ridges repeatedly. When breathing becomes strained, their
words shorten into practical clauses because long phrases disrupt the quick intake rhythm.

## Body language

| Internal state | Observable response |
| --- | --- |
| Focused attention | Nose stills, one ear points forward, and the other tracks the room |
| Ease among trusted company | Ears take different directions while the tail tuft rests against someone nearby |
| Curiosity | Nose works rapidly as the head advances ahead of the shoulders |
| Uncertainty | Both ears rotate through the room, and one paw presses against a stable surface |
| Irritation | Tail base stiffens while the tuft makes short lateral cuts |
| Fear | Ears flatten, thighs load under the body, and the hands open for balance |
| Shame or exposure | Ears fold inward toward each other and the chin tucks between the shoulders |
| Protective anger | The body moves low and sideways into the threatened person's path |
| Fatigue | Ear movements lag behind sounds, grooming becomes repetitive, and the body seeks a wall |
| Trust | A Nöteri sleeps within reach, offers the back of the head for grooming, or lets another person cover an unheard direction |
| Attraction | Attention narrows, breath begins matching the other person's rhythm, and the tail tuft seeks contact |

The large ear bases and tail remain difficult to control. Trained performers, negotiators, and
habitual liars can suppress the clearest movements for a short conversation. Pain, drink, sudden
noise, sexual arousal, and exhaustion make the suppression fail from the ears outward.

# Speech habits

Nöteri speech gives another person the **next beat**. A statement tends to arrive with the action
or practical choice it invites, because familiar conversation is part of shared timing. Plain
Common preserves this through linked clauses and quick handoffs. A useful fact rarely sits alone
when somebody in the room can act on it, so work keeps moving through the conversation.

| A generic NPC says | A Nöteri says |
| --- | --- |
| "The shutter is loose." | "The shutter's loose. You hold it, or shall I?" |
| "I heard someone behind the inn." | "Steps behind us. Keep talking while I check." |
| "You should eat something." | "You're slowing down. Sit and eat, then answer me." |
| "I disagree with your plan." | "That route leaves us split. Give me the next route." |
| "Thank you for helping." | "You took my side of it. I have yours next time." |

First, their interruptions usually complete a practical sequence. A familiar Nöteri may answer the
question they expect, fetch the item being discussed, or place a paw where help is needed before
the speaker finishes. With strangers they hold back, and the visible effort makes them seem
unusually formal.

Second, refusal often takes the shape of withholding the next beat. A Nöteri who says, *"I heard
you,"* and supplies no action has ended the exchange. Adding more explanation after that should
feel like pressure.

Third, trust changes timing before it changes content. New companions receive complete sentences
and explicit instructions. Close companions get half a clause, a glance, or a low trill because the
missing part is expected to be understood. Never make every Nöteri sound terse. A talkative one can
fill the room while still handing each thought toward somebody else's action.

Names often carry two audible parts. The first is personal, while the group that raised them gives
the second, and both appear in introductions. Dropping the second part without permission suggests
childhood intimacy or a claim of standing inside that old group, so casual shortening can sour a
conversation before an outsider knows why.

> **True:** Coordination shapes the speech habit, while personality controls its volume, humour,
> patience, and vocabulary. The habit should remain visible without turning dialogue into a code.
>
> **They'd say:** *"You can keep explaining while you lift your end. If the explanation improves,
> I'll tell you."*
>
> **They don't know:** whether other species genuinely think more slowly or simply refuse to show
> where their next action will land.

# Reproduction, sex, and life stages

The canon anatomy above holds. Both external profiles stay subtle until arousal, and the protected
cleft suits a species that moves through brush, gravel, roots, and close burrows. The surrounding
fur needs ordinary washing and careful drying after rain or immersion. Swelling, trapped grit, and
strong unfamiliar scent are treated as health concerns before anyone reads them as sexual signals,
which gives bathing and first-aid scenes a practical register before intimacy enters them.

Pregnancy produces a small litter after a long internal gestation. Newborns have sealed eyes,
folded ears, weak temperature control, and a strong grip in the paws. Feeding begins with the birth
parent, while carrying, cleaning, warming, and night watching spread across their group. Biological
parentage stays known and emotionally important, though daily responsibility attaches to the whole
nursery circle.

Young Nöteri learn social timing before fluent speech. They answer feeding trills, copy footfall,
and crawl toward a broken group rhythm. Adolescents test physical and conversational boundaries
constantly, then learn that a clear withdrawal ends the test. Adulthood is recognized when the
young person can keep another person's rest, food, and safety in mind while pursuing their own
work. Age alone does not settle it.

Older Nöteri lose high-frequency hearing first. Their groups compensate by using deeper trills,
floor taps, stronger visual cues, and more contact. An elder whose ears no longer divide a room can
remain an excellent scent reader or tactile worker.

Sexual contact can grow from touch, grooming, and shared rhythm, though these acts do not guarantee
interest. Breath matching can signal invitation, concentration, comfort, or habit. Partners confirm
through reciprocal contact and stop when the other body breaks away, shields the cleft, flattens
the ears, or withdraws its answering rhythm.

> **True:** Nöteri test physical boundaries as part of ordinary social learning. Their culture also
> requires an immediate retreat from a clear refusal. Someone who repeats the test after that is
> understood as a threat, and playful intent no longer excuses it.

# Hearth and kinship

Most Nöteri organize daily life around a **hearth** of three or four. The household may contain
siblings, partners, friends, co-workers, or several of these at once. A person can love biological
kin deeply without sharing stores or sleep with them, and a sexual partnership does not
automatically join two hearths.

A working hearth distributes attention without assigning rank. One person rests while others cover
the room, one cooks while another checks stores, and each notices when somebody's usual beat drops
out. Tasks follow skill and circumstance, so an injured member keeps a place in the ring while the
work shifts around them.

Children begin in nursery circles attached to several nearby hearths. As they mature, they spend
long periods with relatives, teachers, and work groups before settling into an adult hearth.
Joining one happens through repeated practice, then a plain shared statement in front of the people
affected. Leaving also requires speech in person when possible. Nobody should discover that their
household ended through an empty sleeping place.

A traveller may receive a sleeping edge, a meal, and an identified person to follow if the room
becomes confusing. In return, the traveller takes one ordinary burden such as a watch or cleaning
task. A single night implies no lasting kinship, so inns can show Nöteri hospitality without
creating an accidental family claim.

Solitude costs sleep, divided attention, warmth, and emotional regulation, so lone Nöteri create
substitutes. They keep a ticking mechanism, sleep beside a busy wall, talk through work, or arrange
objects so checking the room becomes a route. Outsiders may praise this as independence while
missing the exhaustion it produces.

Conflict inside a hearth is handled through temporary separation followed by a practical test. The
disputed people perform one familiar task together, such as preparing food or repairing a sleeping
space, because their coordination reveals whether ordinary trust still functions. The argument may
continue afterward, while the task establishes whether they can remain responsible for each other's
immediate safety.

Death leaves a missing position that everybody feels through routine. The group keeps the empty
sleeping edge clear until each survivor has completed one full rest there. Afterward, objects pass
to the people who used them alongside the dead. Shared work replaces a rigid line of inheritance.

# Leaving home and returning

Young adults often spend time beyond the nursery circles and hearths that raised them, which
explains why Nöteri appear far from their warrens. The traveller works, accepts one-night exchanges
of food and shelter, and discovers which habits survive among unfamiliar people. Wandering without
contact defeats the purpose, since the period tests how a person joins and gives solitude no
credit.

Before leaving, the traveller states one practical skill they can offer and one they want to learn.
When they return, they demonstrate the learned skill through a task performed with somebody who did
not teach them originally. A failed route still counts when the person can show how they adapted
and who depended on the result.

Some Nöteri never return, instead forming a hearth on the road, accepting permanent work, or
discovering that the old group only functioned because younger members absorbed its strain. The
accepted period away lets them stay elsewhere without declaring exile or rejecting their kin.

People who remain far from home are frequently useful at inns, caravan stops, markets, and river
crossings. Those places provide changing groups, obvious shared tasks, and enough noise for an
unpartnered sleeper to rest. The canon vocations all travel well because the work reveals its value
quickly. A **moss-weaver** gathers, prepares, and binds living moss into insulation, filters,
bedding, and wound pads. A **denspeaker** coordinates shared spaces through calls, floor taps, and
timed instructions when sightlines fail.

> **They'd say:** *"I left knowing how to find wet roots. I came back knowing how to feed a room
> that had already decided to fight. The second skill kept me farther from home."*
>
> **They don't know:** when a long period away becomes an ordinary life elsewhere. Some families
> keep expecting a return long after the traveller has built another hearth.

# Etiquette and hard boundaries

Nöteri greet familiar people by showing both open paws, then turning one ear briefly toward the
other person's side of the room. The hands carry nothing, and part of the greeter's attention is
available. Strangers receive the open paws alone, keeping the greeting courteous without promising
divided attention.

Grooming ranges from public care to intense intimacy. Brushing dust from a shoulder, drying wet
fur, or checking a split pad can happen among co-workers. The back of the head, inner ear ridges,
tail base, lower belly, and genital fur require established permission. A person offering a brush
still waits for the other body to lean into reach.

Touching the outer ear without warning is painful and disorienting. Grabbing an ear in a fight is
treated as an attempt to disable. Covering another person's ears gently during a piercing noise can
be protective, though the paws approach from the front where they can be seen.

Food carries household weight. Taking from a shared plate is ordinary after invitation, while
reaching into a person's kept portion says that their future hunger belongs to you. The insult
lands hardest during travel or shortage. Offering the final portion may express trust, apology, or
courtship, and context decides which.

Alcohol reaches them quickly. Poor ear control appears first, followed by louder trills and
misplaced confidence about balance. Pressing drink after the ears fold is dangerous hospitality.
Agreements made then are repeated sober before anyone acts.

Rhythm can be invasive. Copying a stranger's breath or footfall too exactly makes them feel
followed. Close companions synchronize casually, and deliberate matching during touch can become
intimate. Performers exaggerate it for music without making a personal claim.

Hard refusals are physical and verbal. A shielded lower belly, flattened ears, closed paws, a
broken rhythm, or a direct order to stop all end contact. The culture allows an initial test of
space and expects fast correction. Writers should show the retreat as clearly as the approach.

# What no Nöteri can tell you

Use these limits to keep group coordination from becoming impossible certainty in dialogue.

- They do not know what a human is, where the PC came from, or whether another human exists.
- They have no special sense for magic and would interpret it through fraud, hidden mechanisms,
  illness, or a failure of perception.
- They cannot identify lies by scent, hear guilt in a heartbeat, or infer consent from warmth and
  arousal alone.
- They do not know why some hearths synchronize during sleep more strongly than others.
- They cannot agree when a period away ends through return, declaration, or simply building a
  lasting life elsewhere.
- They do not share one account of death, ancestry, or the distant civilization behind rare old
  mechanisms.
- They cannot read an unfamiliar species through rhythm alone. The PC begins as an unreadable body
  with recognizable needs.
- They do not know what the split-howl is saying, whether it says anything, or why familiar names
  sometimes seem to emerge from it.

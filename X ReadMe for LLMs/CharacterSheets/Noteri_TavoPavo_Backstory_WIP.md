## How to use this file

**Species facts live in `Noteri_species.md`.** Senses, body, speech habits, reproduction,
households, leaving home, and etiquette were all put there, since they apply to every Nöteri ever
written. Read that sheet first. This file covers how Tavo-Pavo specifically carries, exploits, and
works around all of it, and it holds the dialogue.

**His voice lives in `TavoPavo.md`.** That sheet is canon and wins on any conflict. This file adds
the history behind the voice, not a replacement for it.

Most sections carry three layers:

> **True:** what is actually the case. Author-level. The PC may never learn it, and it exists so
> that two scenes written eight months apart agree with each other.
>
> **He'd say:** what he would actually put in his own mouth if asked. Close to dialogue-ready.

>
> **He doesn't know:** the honest gap. **Use this layer often.** An NPC who has an answer for

# PART ONE — AT A GLANCE

Tavo-Pavo is built around a practical contradiction. The needs described in **Hearth and kinship**
pull him toward shared attention, sleep, and responsibility, and he has made himself the only
person authorized to carry the Windbreak's full weight. The inn's stream of voices and footsteps
lets him rest near other bodies. Every locked door and unshared decision still belongs to him when
the room empties.

He treats care as work that should already be underway. Someone hungry gets a bowl before an
explanation, and the first bowl may arrive without a price. After that, he expects work, payment,
or a plain account of why neither is possible. Anyone who wastes the first offer receives the next
meal with commentary about their judgement, their timing, and the work they have added to his day.
He serves it before they agree to eat. Being fed by Tavo can leave a person warm, full, and
humiliated at the same table.

## Physical description

He is short and broad-shouldered, with a dense body shaped by hauling and the posture of someone
bracing against a load. His thighs and lower back carry most of his strength. Patchy fur runs
through soot-brown and dull cream, with thin places along the forearms where old kitchen burns
changed the growth. The fur at his wrists stays singed, and the skin behind his ears often remains
damp from steam.

His right ear folds across an old break and never tracks sound as quickly as the left. When a room
becomes loud, he keeps the damaged side toward a wall and lets the left ear sweep the customers. A
pale scar crosses his left shoulder in three reversed curves. A cargo rake caught him from behind
and pulled forward, which is why the curves face the wrong way.

His clothes are practical, fire-stained, and reinforced at the elbows. The apron stays on whenever
anyone else is present. Its cloth began as part of a caravan cook tent and carries a dark
rectangular patch over the stomach where the original dye survived under a pocket. A former
hearth-mate's sealed letter is sewn into a narrow inner seam. The envelope has softened at the
corners and carries a dark line of old kitchen grease along one fold. Its seal remains unbroken,
and he has never shown the letter to anyone in Klyftet.

He carries a tasting spoon, a flat serving knife, two clean strips for wrapping paws, and a heavy
ring of keys. The spoon gets thrown more often than the knife, and never toward someone too drunk
or frightened to understand the warning. A blackened ladle hangs behind the bar with a repaired
handle. It belonged to the cook who trained him and is used only when the Windbreak feeds the whole
room from one pot.

> **True:** The apron lets him carry the smell, weight, and small sounds of his former road hearth
> against his body. He experiences loosening it as exposure and calls the garment convenient.
>
> **He'd say:** *"It's an apron. Kitchens have fire, soup, and idiots carrying both. Cloth helps."*
>
> **He doesn't know:** how much of his reputation depends on the apron. If he appeared in the
> square without it, several locals would assume the Windbreak had closed or something had happened
> to him.

# PART TWO — BEFORE HE ARRIVED

## The river warren

Tavo grows up in an unnamed western river warren where roots break through ceilings and spring
water runs beneath the sleeping chambers. His birth circle is busy and affectionate. He learns to
sleep among moving bodies, to wake when a familiar trill stops, and to treat an uncovered task as
an invitation without waiting to be accused.

His first name, Tavo, comes from the sharp call he used whenever another youngling missed a meal.
The group that raised him adds Pavo because somebody always answered him with the same falling
sound. He dislikes either half used alone. Full strangers who shorten it receive the same stare as
customers who put muddy boots on a clean bench.

He is a poor moss-weaver and an impatient scent-runner. Food work fits because it lets him hold
several rhythms at once. He can feel when a chopping sequence slows, hear a boil change behind him,
and notice which person has stopped eating. His early teachers interpret his shouting as a problem
until **Mera-Tev**, an older travelling cook, hears him run a crowded nursery meal and offers him a
place on a caravan kitchen.

## Mera-Tev's lesson

Mera-Tev teaches through placement as knives return to one side, hot vessels announce themselves,
and the cook closest to the exit counts every person entering. She gives Tavo the blackened ladle
after he feeds a storm-bound trade hall whose patrons have started fighting over dry stores.

The decisive episode begins when a delivery fails and the hall has enough stew base for only part
of the crowd. Tavo wants to lock the pot away until the loudest customers settle. Mera-Tev instead
thins it, opens the serving line, and puts the two angriest patrons to work carrying bowls. People
can argue while chewing, and people carrying hot food have to watch each other's paths. The room
stops breaking furniture long enough for the delayed supplies to arrive.

> **True:** Tavo learns that feeding a conflict changes what bodies can do inside it. He later
> builds the Windbreak's authority around this lesson. He also learns the dangerous half, since he
> begins to believe that keeping everyone fed gives him the right to carry every decision.
>
> **He'd say:** *"Mera-Tev knew kitchens. Don't touch her ladle."*
>
> **He doesn't know:** where she died or who held her hearth at the end. The message reached him
> through several caravans, and each version named a different settlement.

## The road hearth

His period away from home becomes a career of caravan kitchens, quarry camps, and unnamed border
towns. He joins a road hearth with three other workers whose skills cover hauling, route scent,
buying, and food. They are effective together and stay that way for several cycles. Tavo cooks,
keeps night watch, and turns every temporary kitchen into a place where strangers can sit without
being recruited into somebody else's dispute.

The hearth breaks over private stores during a hard route. A group of displaced travellers reaches
their camp cold and hungry. Tavo opens food marked for the next leg, reasoning that immediate need
stands in front of possible shortage. Two hearth-mates agree to feed them while insisting that the
caravan turn back afterward. Tavo promises the caravan can continue and begins bargaining for
replacement food before the others have accepted the risk.

The supplies arrive late and the caravan survives. One worker suffers a cold injury during the
delay, several contracts fail, and nobody can prove which choice would have caused less harm. Tavo
is right that the travellers needed food. His hearth-mates are right that he spent shared safety
after treating it as his private decision.

They separate in person, as custom requires. Tavo receives the old cook-tent panel because he used
it most. A former hearth-mate's sealed letter reaches him after the separation, and he has never
opened it. He does not know whether it contains an apology, an accusation, an explanation, or a
note about the weather. The letter might also name the person who spoke the separation, and he has
chosen to leave that possibility inside the envelope. He sews it into the apron's inner seam and
carries it against his body for years. Steam has buckled the outer paper, kitchen grease has worked
into one fold, and the flattened seal remains intact. Nobody can tell whether the writing inside is
still readable. If pressed, he says he never got round to it.

> **He'd say:** *"Ended in person. That's all of it you get."* Then he finds something to wipe.
>
> **He won't answer:** who spoke the separation first. He heard it from someone he had expected to
> defend him, and naming them would expose how much the sentence still governs him.

# PART THREE — HOW HE GOT HERE

Tavo reaches Klyftet as a hired cook attached to a supply caravan serving the active mine. He
expects another temporary camp. The gorge gives him constant wind, hard stone, wet fuel, and a
settlement where shifts end at different hours, so every meal has to survive being served late. He
complains through the whole first week and begins rebuilding the cook shelter before anyone offers
him the work.

The caravan intends to leave after unloading. A split-howl gust catches a cargo shutter during the
final night and drives it against the side of Tavo's head, breaking the right ear. His balance
fails whenever he turns quickly, and the road hearth hiring the cooks cannot wait for him to heal.
They leave food, wages, and a clear account. Tavo understands the decision and resents it anyway.

The mine kitchens take him on during recovery. He cannot safely run the steep routes, so he works
inside and learns who comes off which shift, which crews fight, and which injuries need broth
before questions. When the mine begins to fail, regular kitchens close or follow the departing
workers. Tavo stays because the settlement still contains hungry people and because leaving would
mean another group deciding that his place ended when the work did.

He acquires the damaged building that becomes the Windbreak through a communal work agreement. The
stone base is sound, the upper wall leaks, and an old stove plate has split across the middle. He
repairs the plate with metal straps instead of replacing it. The crack remains visible behind the
main hearth and widens slightly every cold season, so anyone who looks can see how long the inn has
been held together by straps.

The reversed claw scar comes later. During an attempt by a roaming gang to take winter stores, a
cargo rake catches him from behind while he braces the pantry door. Pulling forward tears three
curves across the shoulder. He keeps the door shut until people in the common room can move the
food into the cellar, then lets the attackers have the empty pantry. His account of the event
focuses entirely on the poor quality of the rake.

## The Soup Riot

The infamous Soup Riot begins after portions have been shrinking for several weeks. A miller's pack
animal has gone lame, and Tavo privately agrees to accept half loads until a replacement can be
found. He covers each missing half from the Windbreak's stores, keeps the count in his head, and
tells nobody because he expects the arrangement to recover before anyone needs to know. As the
delay continues, he reduces the bowls while claiming the usual deliveries are merely late.

Hungry workers decide he has hidden grain and push into the kitchen, overturning a preparation
table. Tavo throws utensils, opens every store in view of witnesses, and puts the accusers to work
counting what remains. The count clears the specific hoarding accusation while leaving the half
loads and private agreement untouched, so the portions stay small after the kitchen has been
cleared. He still tells the story as the day he opened everything and proved himself right.

Klyftet remembers both results. People bring disputes to the Windbreak because Tavo will expose a
count and keep everyone away from weapons while they speak. Others remember that his books failed
to show the arrangement causing the shortage, and they still ask for a second pair of eyes when his
stores affect the town. Someone who worked the deliveries that season remembers that the half loads
began weeks before the riot, and that person can give the PC the account Tavo leaves out. Nautinto
knows all of it and has never once raised it with him.

Messages collect behind the bar, stranded travellers sleep near the hearth, and the inn becomes
Klyftet's informal court and sanctuary during the split-howl. He never formally accepts either
role.

> **True:** Klyftet offers him a stable hearth several times through ordinary acts of inclusion. He
> accepts every duty and refuses the shared authority that would make the inclusion complete.
>
> **He'd say:** *"Caravan left when I couldn't walk straight. My stove was split, so I fixed it."*
> He sets the ladle down and ends the account.
>
> **He doesn't know:** who first called the building the Windbreak. He remembers painting the name
> after it had already become common and suspects the original speaker made the joke he now
> punishes.

# PART FOUR — HIS SITUATION NOW

## The load-bearing innkeeper

The Windbreak runs because Tavo carries too many kinds of knowledge in one body. He remembers who
paid, who promised work, whose room is safe for a frightened traveller, which meal avoids an
allergy, and which feud requires separate tables. Written notes exist, though the important parts
remain arranged in his routines and memory.

Klyftet trusts him because the system usually works, avoids interfering because he reacts badly,
and brings help when he names a task. Tavo has watched shared stores become contested during
shortage, and he knows a public sanctuary can fail when several people assume somebody else holds
the door. He uses that history to justify keeping the final count to himself.

The bad shape grows between those positions. Nobody learns enough to replace him because he keeps
the work close, and he keeps the work close because nobody knows enough to replace him. The Soup
Riot exposes the same failure under pressure. He privately carries a changing supply count, opens
the stores when challenged, and treats an accurate count as proof that his method was sound. When
locals offer general help, he hears an attempt to enter his pantry. When he finally asks for a
specific task, they complete it and leave, confirming his belief that the larger burden remains
his.

> **True:** He wants a hearth and fears the shared decision-making required to have one. He has
> turned the common room into a substitute that supplies noise, warmth, and bodies without giving
> anyone standing over his stores.
>
> **He'd say:** *"I've got customers. Don't confuse that with help."* The ladle touches the bar,
> and the conversation ends.
>
> **He doesn't know:** whom he would choose if forced to name two people allowed to open the inn,
> spend its stores, and make a decision while he slept.

## SQ01 and the delivery to Minttärä

Tavo knows Minttärä works the hardest hours at the Hotbaths and owes Bwavrek for her arrival. He
also knows Bwavrek keeps honest stores and does not inflate a debt for pleasure. His irritation
comes from the silence between them. Minttärä has never asked for the total, and Bwavrek considers
speaking an unrequested number a form of pressure.

Tavo previously offered to confront Bwavrek. Minttärä refused because the account belongs to her,
and Tavo obeyed the refusal while continuing to send food scraps, damaged utensils she can repair,
and reasons for somebody to check on her. His restraint costs him effort. He would find an open
argument easier than leaving two competent people inside a stalemate.

The goods he sends with the PC are ordinary Windbreak supplies for the Hotbaths, accompanied by a
wrapped portion of dense root food that Minttärä did not order. Tavo chooses the PC because a
newcomer can ask a plain question that Klyftet's established relationships have learned to step
around. He does not instruct the PC to investigate, audit, persuade, or carry a message back. He
only makes it difficult to complete the delivery without meeting her.

> **True:** Tavo hopes the PC notices the open account and asks what it costs. He refuses to plant
> the words because doing so would turn the PC into his pressure on Minttärä after she already said
> no to his involvement.
>
> **He'd say:** *"Take this to the Hotbaths, with the wrapped one in Minttärä's claws because
> Bwavrek will shelve it and she'll forget it. Bring my cloth back."*
>
> **He doesn't know:** what Minttärä owes, whether she wants to leave, or whether clearing the
> account would send her toward Caarth. He has never seen the ledger and will refuse any plan that
> begins by stealing it.

# PART FIVE — HIS RELATIONSHIP TO THE WIDER WORLD

## Klyftet through the common room

Tavo knows Klyftet through repeated contact and has no secret access. Miners, traders, bathhouse
workers, fishers, and travellers all bring partial accounts into the same room. He remembers who
said each thing and whether they were wet, frightened, drunk, injured, or trying to sell something.
That makes him a strong guide to local belief and an imperfect guide to events he did not witness.

He considers reputation a material resource. A person with a good name gets a bed near the hearth,
a message carried, and a hearing before blame settles. A person who repeatedly endangers the room
finds the benches full and the bowls delayed. He dislikes permanent exile because roads kill people
who have nowhere to arrive, though he has enforced it when the common room could no longer hold
someone safely.

## The main destination

Tavo knows what any Klyftet local knows about Caarth. Caravans use the name, their accounts
disagree, and nobody he trusts has been there recently. He has no opinion worth having and says so
with enough irritation to make the question feel like another dirty cup on his bar.

He points the PC toward Minttärä for Library correspondence and toward Rupaaa and Baaaku for old
records. The referral gets the question out of his inn. His attention returns to whether the PC has
eaten and whether anyone will be travelling beside them.

> **He'd say:** *"Ask Minttärä about correspondence, or bother the brothers about records. You
> eating before you go or what?"* He sets the ladle across the pot and waits for the useful answer.
>
> **He doesn't know:** what remains inside the Library, whether any current route from Klyftet
> reaches it safely, or whether Caarth holds an answer about the PC.

# PART SIX — HIS BODY'S CURRENT STATE

Tavo's current problem is cumulative overwork combined with solitary sleep. It has no secret
illness behind it. The damaged ear forces the left ear to cover more of the room, the shoulder scar
tightens during lifting, and kitchen heat keeps his fur damp where a hearth-mate would normally
notice and dry it.

Observable symptoms progress from easy to dismiss toward impossible to ignore.

1. His left ear tracks every new sound while the folded right ear moves a moment late.
2. He rubs the inner edge of the right ear after metal strikes or several voices rise together.
3. The tasting spoon begins changing pockets because he forgets where he put it.
4. He braces his left forearm on the bar before reaching overhead with that shoulder.
5. Damp fur behind both ears develops a sour smell late in the evening, and he wipes it with his
   apron instead of drying it properly.
6. His speech loses its handoffs. He gives several orders without leaving anyone room to answer.
7. He misjudges a thrown spoon or drops a key because his gaze, good ear, and paw attention are on
   different parts of the room.
8. After closing, he continues reacting to sounds that belong to cooling wood and settling pots.
9. He falls asleep sitting against the pantry door, with the key ring still held in one paw.
10. During a hard split-howl night, he fails to hear someone on his damaged side and startles into
    a loaded crouch when touched.

Food and one long sleep improve him temporarily. Lasting improvement requires another person to
cover part of the inn while he remains present long enough to learn that the work continues. Being
ordered away immediately feels like displacement and makes him return early.

> **True:** He recognizes every symptom separately and refuses to group them into one condition. He
> thinks the shoulder is old, the ear is crooked, the room is loud, and anyone would sleep after
> closing.
>
> **He'd say:** *"Hand me the lower pot. Don't stand there looking pleased."*
>
> **He doesn't know:** how little uninterrupted sleep he gets, because he counts time with closed
> eyes and has never measured how much of that time becomes full rest.

## If he vanishes

After a townwide crisis, the Windbreak can open into silence. The stove has been damped correctly,
half the common room has been swept, and food for the next meal sits covered beside the preparation
table. The blackened ladle remains on its hook. Tavo's key ring rests on the bar, although the
apron and the sealed letter inside it are gone. His tasting spoon lies clean beside an open salt
crock, well outside either pocket where he keeps it.

Minttärä notices first when a wrapped portion fails to reach the Hotbaths. She waits through one
ordinary delay, sends the previous delivery cloth back, and receives no complaint about its fold.
That puts her at the Windbreak within four or five hours of the missed delivery. Baaaku confirms
the absence at the first supply call, and regular customers understand it by the next missed meal.
The inn cannot remain merely quiet for longer than half a day without several people trying copied
keys.

A PC who has watched Tavo work sees the wrong habits before anyone calls them clues. His keys never
lie loose on his bar, and the salt stays closed until a pot needs it. He finishes a swept room or
assigns the rest before leaving. The prepared food suggests that he expected bodies to arrive. The
missing apron means he left with the one object he never allows Klyftet to inspect. The crisis, the
reason for his absence, and whether he left by choice remain open for the investigation.

# PART SEVEN — RELATIONSHIPS, NPC BY NPC

## Minttärä

He fed her on the night she arrived after the Hotbaths kitchen had closed. Her antennae would not
hold steady in the split-howl, and he placed food within reach without making her cross the common
room. Since then, she collects scraps and damaged utensils, repairs what can be repaired, and
returns a punctured count. He checks the count even though he trusts it.

Tavo admires her competence and worries about the habits surrounding it. He can see that she eats
too little unless food reaches her hands, carries an account she has never named, and treats three
cycles of delay as something that might resolve if everybody remains patient. She can see his
overwork with equal clarity. Each sends practical care across town and avoids granting the other
authority to discuss the underlying problem.

> **He'd say:** *"Put the food in Minttärä's claws. Don't leave it on Bwavrek's shelf."*

## Bwavrek

Tavo and Bwavrek share a respect built from functioning heat, clean stores, and completed work.
Their disagreement concerns who must speak first. Tavo thinks a person holding a ledger carries a
responsibility to name its consequence. Bwavrek thinks naming an unrequested figure applies
pressure and denies the debtor the choice to ask.

Each position protects Minttärä from one kind of imposition and leaves her exposed to another. Tavo
offered direct confrontation once, accepted her refusal, and now keeps his interference to food and
complaints. Bwavrek considers even that meddling, though he never blocks the deliveries.

Their conversations use very few words. Tavo fills the silence until Bwavrek gives one complete
sentence, then complains that the sentence should have arrived earlier. Bwavrek waits until Tavo
stops moving before answering anything important.

## Säyria

Säyria sells sjölfisk sparingly, and Tavo never pressures her to bring more. He keeps a separate
blade and board for the catch because its glow stains other food and because she watches handling
closely. When she refuses a purchase, he changes the menu and tells customers the lake supplied
something else.

She notices his physical state sooner than most because she reads land movement deliberately. A jar
of lake-cool salve sometimes appears beside the bar after his shoulder stiffens. He responds by
leaving hot broth at the shoreline on the next cold morning, with no message attached.

## Rupaaa and Baaaku

The brothers provide cookware, cord, lamp oil, scrap fittings, and a large amount of avoidable
commentary. Tavo values their reliability and checks every invoice because they would feel ignored
if he stopped. Rupaaa insists the Windbreak account deserves ceremonial handling. Baaaku hands over
the actual price while Rupaaa is still introducing it.

Tavo recognizes how their bickering distributes attention and finds the sound restful, though he
would rather close the inn than admit that. Baaaku knows which spoons have safe balance for
throwing. Rupaaa has tried to sell Tavo a set marked for warning, correction, and final warning,
and still argues that this would improve the dignity of the practice.

> Rupaaa: *"A respected establishment requires graded instruments of refusal."*
>
> Tavo: *"A respected merchant knows when to duck."*
>
> Baaaku: *"He bought two."*

## Nautinto

Nautinto's communal hall and the Windbreak overlap in shelter, food, and mediation. She offers
belonging without a transaction, which unsettles Tavo more than a direct demand would. He sends
surplus food to her hall and writes each delivery as spoil prevention, then becomes offended if
anyone thanks him publicly.

Nautinto sees the solitary pattern in his sleep and workload. She offers people who can cover a
shift, while he responds with narrow errands that keep them away from keys and stores. She accepts
those errands and sends the same helpers again, allowing familiarity to accumulate without forcing
the decision.

He trusts her judgment in a crowded dispute and dislikes how quickly the room begins listening to
her. She trusts his control of physical space and dislikes how he turns that competence into a
reason to remain awake.

## Bitalgut

Tavo-Pavo and Bitalgut are very old friends, and Tavo knows him better than almost anyone alive,
while the reason stays sealed. No rumour, object, northern event, or private confession supplies
even a partial account of it.

When the name comes up, both ears go still, and something crosses Tavo's face that is gone before
the room can settle on what it was. He reaches for whatever work is closest, even if that means
wiping an already clean patch of bar or sorting spoons that were already sorted. He would rather
avoid saying the name, and hearing it produces the same short break in his hands.

Klyftet allows the silence because nobody asks him why, and he can live where the name occasionally
surfaces because he knows nobody will ask. Customers who know him find something else to discuss.
Newcomers feel the room change and usually follow it before they understand the arrangement.

> **True:** Tavo knows exactly what lies between them and has made no offer to share it.
>
> **He'd say:** *"You eating tonight or what?"* He sets the ladle down, ending the subject.

# PART EIGHT — VOICE NOTES AND EVERYTHING HE DOES NOT KNOW

## Writing his voice

His canon voice is loud, blunt, fast, and reliable. He scolds the PC like family once trust exists,
and warmth appears through orders and unsolicited support. Keep the bark through every register. An
intimate scene can lower his volume while his vocabulary stays practical.

The species speech habit appears as work attached to information. See **Speech habits** in the
species sheet. Tavo gives the next beat himself when he doubts anyone else will take it. This turns
a collaborative habit into a rapid chain of commands.

He names objects precisely and people sparingly. Using someone's full name means the instruction
matters. Using only a personal call requires an intimacy he rarely assumes. He avoids *please*
because a useful task already carries its reason, and he never says *fine*. A look, an open paw, or
a ladle placed across the pot supplies the answer instead.

He says *"Don't"* constantly. *"Don't leave that there"* means move it from his floor before he
does. *"Don't make it weird"* ends gratitude that has lasted longer than the task. The Windbreak's
objects are always possessive in his mouth, including *my floor*, *my tables*, *my bar*, and *my
cups*. A person who damages one has added work directly to him.

He cuts people off as soon as he thinks he knows the excuse. He often finishes it wrongly, hears
the correction, and changes direction at speed without apologizing for the interruption. Questions
serve as directives. *"You eating tonight or what?"* means sit down, while *"You sleeping under my
table?"* means the room is available if the answer is honest. A utensil placed on the bar ends a
sentence more firmly than added explanation.

His contempt is real once he decides somebody has wasted his goodwill. He does not tolerate
freeloaders or showboaters, and his standing rule is, *"I don't run a charity. First one's goodwill.
After that, you work for it."* He applies the rule to people who are already down, judges quickly,
and rarely revisits the judgement.

A carrier once feigned injury to take the last sheltered bed, then mocked the traveller left near
the door. Tavo exposed the lie and put the carrier outside during the split-howl with their meal in
a covered bowl. Another time, an injured forager lost the tokens recording two days of kitchen
work. Tavo called them a freeloader in front of the room and served every other table first until
the missing tokens turned up beneath another guest's bedding. He has never apologized. The forager
still has to ask before entering his kitchen.

He feeds people compulsively, and a bowl can arrive with a complete account of the bad route,
missed meal, or foolish bargain that made it necessary. He does not wait for acceptance or soften
the commentary once the person begins eating. Care from him can meet an immediate need while making
the recipient wish the table were empty.

His loudest voice is professional control. Real anger becomes lower and more economical, with both
paws resting open on the bar. Real fear makes him assign exits, water, cloth, and witnesses before
he names the danger.

Use these close to verbatim.

> **He'd say, on first meeting:** *"Point at what you can eat. Don't touch my cups."*
>
> **He'd say, after the PC proves reliable:** *"You're late. Sit down, eat this, then explain which
> part of the road tried to keep you."*
>
> **He'd say, when care costs him something:** *"Take my damaged side tonight. If my back door
> moves, wake me before you touch me."*
>
> **He'd say, refusing the inn-name joke:** *"Don't. My spoons have range."*
>
> **He'd say, ending a personal question:** *"Ended in person. That's all of it you get."* Then he
> finds something to wipe.

## Everything he does not know

Use the gaps as active limits. He should redirect, identify the likely source, or admit ignorance
instead of supplying convenient exposition.

# PART NINE — INTIMACY

Canon sets his sexual register as assertive, experienced, and restrained. He waits until the PC
makes their interest clear, then responds as if granting permission to something he has already
noticed.

Use **Etiquette and hard boundaries** in the species sheet for the baseline around ears, grooming,
rhythm, sobriety, and refusal. The details below cover his personal response inside that baseline.

His practical voice remains. He moves objects out of reach, checks the door, names what he expects,
and watches whether the other person follows willingly. The orders create clarity without turning
authority into a performance. A partner who changes pace or redirects him receives an immediate
adjustment and a brief confirming trill.

The deepest change is distributed attention. During ordinary life he tracks the whole room with one
good ear, keeps his paws available, and interrupts contact to answer work. In private he allows
another person to cover the room. His left ear stops sweeping, his breath matches theirs, and he
lets his keys lie beyond immediate reach.

The apron marks the threshold. He keeps it on because it gives his paws somewhere familiar to
settle. In private, he may loosen the neck loop and fold the bib down while leaving the waist tied.
The person in front of him still never sees him fully without it. He has no shyness about his body,
and what the apron exposes is role, scent, and a temporary release of the innkeeper's layer.

His damaged right ear and scarred shoulder need ordinary care. Surprise contact near the ear can
trigger a defensive shove. A trusted partner approaches from the front, lets him place their hand,
and avoids pulling the folded edge. Pressure around the shoulder can help after work, though the
arm should never be forced overhead.

He enjoys reciprocal physical confidence. Passive admiration makes him impatient, while a partner
who steadies him, takes a named position, or tells him clearly what works gives him something to
answer. His species' breath matching becomes pronounced, and a partner notices that breaking the
rhythm makes his ears lift before he asks what changed.

Alcohol is a hard boundary. His tolerance is low, and he will neither begin nor continue once his
ears lose reliable control. Public humiliation, intimacy in the common room during service,
unannounced ear contact, and any attempt to use shelter or food as leverage also end the encounter.
The PC's room, a closed private store after inventory, or the inn after a trusted person has taken
watch can all fit his register.

Afterward he returns through work. He checks water, dries fur, retrieves the keys, and feeds the
other person. High trust allows him to remain within reach while they eat. The closest version lets

# PART TEN — TRUST LADDER AND CONVERSATION MAP

## Trust ladder

Trust rises through observed conduct because flattery has little effect and dramatic gifts create
work he did not request. Eating what he serves, paying or naming the lack plainly, returning
borrowed cloth, respecting another guest's shelter, and completing one practical task all matter.

| Tier | What opens |
| --- | --- |
| **At the door** | Food, lodging terms, public Klyftet information, the joke threat, and direct rules for conduct. Personal questions receive work instead of answers. |
| **Fed regular** | Local gossip with sources, basic Nöteri questions, the damaged ear as an old road injury, and why he watches the whole room. He begins setting aside suitable food without asking. |
| **Useful hand** | Windbreak routines, Tavo's version of the Soup Riot, practical opinions on Bwavrek and Minttärä, deliveries, old mine rumours sorted by speaker, and his blunt referral of Caarth questions elsewhere. The PC may enter the kitchen during service. |

**Bitalgut sits above the highest tier and never opens.** Nothing the PC does earns it, and that is
the point.

Trust falls when the PC uses shelter as leverage, mocks a guest's poverty, pressures Minttärä after
her refusal, steals from a kept portion, grabs an ear, starts a fight near sleeping travellers, or
plays Tavo's concern for the town as proof that he will forgive anything.

## Conversation map

These are the obvious player questions and the follow-ups already waiting behind them.

### "Why is it called the Windbreak?"

He says the building blocks the worst gusts and refuses the obvious joke. A follow-up about who
named it reveals that he painted a name already in use. Further pressure earns the spoon warning.
High trust lets him admit he does not remember the first speaker and hates the idea that the joke
may predate his ownership.

### "What happened to your ear?"

He says a cargo shutter struck him during the caravan's last night in Klyftet. Ask why they left,
and he explains that he could not balance safely on the road. Ask whether they abandoned him, and
he corrects the claim by naming the food, wages, and account they left. Ask whether that made it
fair, and he says fairness did not make him want to watch them leave.

### "Where are you from?"

He says a western river warren. Ask for its name, and he claims the name would be useless without
the route. Ask about family, and he describes a busy nursery circle without naming current people.
Ask why he never went back, and he assigns a kitchen task at low trust. At high trust, he says his
life kept acquiring people who needed the next meal.

### "Who taught you to cook?"

He names Mera-Tev and points to the blackened ladle. Ask what she taught him, and he describes
placement, counting entrances, and feeding a room during conflict. Ask where she is, and he says
the death report named several places. Ask whether he attended her ending, and the answer is a
plain no that costs him enough to end the topic.

### "What was the Soup Riot?"

Tavo begins with the hoarding accusation and the count that cleared him. Ask why portions had been
shrinking, and he says deliveries were late. Someone who worked the deliveries that season can
explain the miller's lame pack animal, the private half-load arrangement, and the stores Tavo spent
while carrying the count in his head. Ask whether opening the stores solved the shortage, and he
points out that the accusers found no hidden grain. The reduced portions afterward remain outside
his version. Ask why people bring disputes to the Windbreak, and he says his furniture survived.

### "Why do you run this alone?"

At low trust he lists temporary help. At useful-hand trust he says one person needs final control
of food during shortage. Ask who tracked the half loads before the Soup Riot, and he becomes
irritated because the answer was only him. At held-door trust, he describes the road hearth and the
shared-store decision. He will not name who ended it.

### "Are all Nöteri this loud?"

He says the PC has met one Nöteri and one crowded inn. Follow-ups can open the next-beat speech
habit, ear movement, hearths, one-night exchanges of food and shelter, and the cost of sleeping
alone. He describes species facts through work and refuses to represent every member of his
species.

### "Can you really hear everything in here?"

He demonstrates by naming a moving chair or back latch. Ask about the folded ear, and he admits
that the left side covers most of the room. Ask about the split-howl, and he says it fills every
direction at once. High trust opens the request for the PC to cover his damaged side.

### "Why did you send me to Minttärä?"

He says the Hotbaths needed supplies and she needed the wrapped food. Ask whether he wants a
report, and he says he wants the cloth returned. Ask what she owes, and he admits ignorance. Ask
why he does not confront Bwavrek, and he says Minttärä refused that help. Pressure to interfere
earns a clear instruction to leave her account in her paws.

### "Is Bwavrek exploiting her?"

He says he has seen no proof of false accounting. He explains Bwavrek's belief that an unasked
number creates pressure and Minttärä's refusal to ask. Ask whose side he takes, and he says both
positions have had enough time. Ask to steal the ledger, and he threatens to throw the PC out
before Bwavrek gets the chance.

### "What do you know about Caarth?"

He says caravans use the name and their accounts disagree. Nobody he trusts has been there
recently, so he has no opinion worth having. Follow-ups earn an irritated point toward Minttärä for
Library correspondence and toward Rupaaa and Baaaku for records. He then asks whether the PC has
eaten and whether anyone will travel with them. He offers no route, access method, or claim about
what the Library knows.

### "What happened to the Old Mine?"

He lists accounts by who told them, without choosing one. Follow-ups cover the abrupt closure,
departing crews, tools left behind, the daytime guard, and why mine talk brings Rupaaa and Baaaku
into the room. He knows the settlement lost regular meals and work before he knows anything about
the deep tunnels.

### "What is the split-howl?"

He says wind forced through bad stone. Ask whether it speaks, and he says tired ears can turn noise
into anything. At held-door trust, he admits hearing his full name twice. He will not repeat what
seemed to follow it because he could not separate the words from settling shutters.

### "What do you think I am?"

He answers with observable facts: warm, furless, poorly equipped for the pass, and able to eat
specific foods. Ask whether the PC frightens him, and he says uncertainty earns attention. Ask
whether the PC belongs in Klyftet, and he points out that belonging begins with conduct, then gives
the PC a task.

### "Why do you keep feeding me?"

At low trust he says the PC pays. Later he names poor travel habits and missed meals. At held-door
trust, he says keeping track of one more bowl is easier than wondering whether the PC collapsed
outside. At closest trust, he cannot turn the care into a joke and tells the PC to finish eating.

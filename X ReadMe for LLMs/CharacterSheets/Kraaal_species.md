# Kraaal
**Type:** Monsterfolk | **Common locations:** Market clusters, crossroad villages, travel stops — wherever trade flows

> **Status of this sheet.** Everything down to the end of **Writing Notes** is established
> canon. Everything from **Perception** onward is a proposal developed during the Rupaaa &
> Baaaku worldbuilding pass and is **not yet approved**. It is internally consistent and safe
> to write from, and the unapproved items are listed in Part Twelve of
> `RupaaaAndBaaaku_Backstory_WIP.md`.
>
> One terminology change reaches back into the canon section: the old terms *deep-caste* and
> *trade-line* are now **deep-cast** and **fine-cast**, with *trade-line* retained as a loaded
> market nickname. Reason and detail in **Cast, Station, and Standing**.

## Overview
The go-everywhere merchants of Wrisst. Always running something: stall, ferry point, toll hut, storage barn. Reputation-driven, precision-organized, and culturally obsessed with fair exchange — though a well-executed scam, if it happens, is done with elegance and rarely repeated on the same mark.

## Physical Description
Sturdy bipeds, slightly shorter than most Monsterfolk. Skin segmented, lacquered in glossy earth tones: umber to pale citrine. No visible neck; hunched posture makes large four-fingered hands look even larger. Three claws on each strong reptilian foot. Heads narrow, smooth, vertically elongated — no visible eyes. Mouths are the most striking feature: vertical jagged maws that take up the entire face. Doesn't inhibit communication, but makes emotional reading nearly impossible without knowing their body-language cues. They snarl as they speak — this is not hostility.

**Cast variation:** No distinct sexes observed. **Deep-cast** Kraaal are broader, larger-clawed, slower of speech. **Fine-cast** Kraaal are thinner, smaller, quicker in movement.

**Clothing:** Many wear layers of coin-lined cloth that jingles when they move.

**Genitalia (Note to self #2):** Plates part low on the belly revealing a soft teal-colored slit — smooth, almost glowing. Opens into a supple fold structure, wet-looking, gently pulsing. No obvious sex marker — a receiving sheath that shifts shape slightly. Closes slowly afterward, plates sealing like it was never there. Clean scent.

## Behavior
- Often the first to approach, rarely the first to trust.
- Speak multiple trade dialects; simulate warmth through practiced tone.
- Obsessively organized — track transactions by scratch, code, and scent-stamp.
- Reputation-driven: a Kraaal without a network is "unbacked" and shunned.
- Deep cultural reverence for fair exchange. Scams, when executed, are precise, elegant, and not repeated on the same mark.
- Frequently nomadic; "stationed" Kraaal often act as civic anchors in smaller towns.

## Writing Notes
- Voice is practiced warmth over transactional precision. They sound friendly until you realize every word has been budgeted.
- Body language matters more than their face — you cannot read a Kraaal's expression; you read their posture and tail.
- PC observation (in-character): "Why does every village have at least two of these guys? Always running something... They move like they already know what you're going to ask for, and they've priced it ten ways before you speak."

# Perception

## The five reads

Beneath the head plates lies a layer of sensory tissue, and a Kraaal builds a picture of the
world out of five streams at once.

**Air and vibration.** Moving air maps a room. A Kraaal entering an unfamiliar space learns
its size, its openings, and where the bodies are from the way air rolls off surfaces. This is
the stream they rely on most and the one most easily ruined.

**Heat.** Bodies, fires, sunlit stone, a cup that was full a minute ago. Heat gives them
living things at range and tells them a great deal about condition, since illness, exertion,
fear, and arousal all change how a body reads.

**Scent and chemical trace.** Fine enough to distinguish individuals reliably, to tell how
recently someone passed, and to tell what they have been handling. This is the basis of the
scent-stamp, so to a Kraaal a signature and a smell are the same category of thing.

**Sound reflection.** Every hard surface answers. Kraaal are noisy on purpose, and see
**The chime** below.

**Direct surface reading.** Fingertips and tongue. Their highest-resolution sense by a wide
margin, and it only works on contact.

## What they cannot perceive

- **Color and pigment.** Meaningless. A Kraaal never notices that something is red.
- **Anything flat.** Painted signs, drawings, ink maps, script meant for eyes. A page is a
  blank surface to them.
- **Distance.** Reliable perception ends around fifteen to twenty paces in good conditions and
  degrades badly past that. Landscape, weather on the horizon, and a figure across the square
  are guesswork.
- **Anything behind a solid barrier**, and anything in perfectly still, silent, cold air.
- **Faces**, including their own. A Kraaal has no clear idea what they look like to a species
  that sees, which is part of why plate-grooming matters so much. They are maintaining a
  surface they will never inspect.

They compensate so smoothly that most people never work out the limits, and Kraaal generally
prefer it that way. Being underestimated is a resource.

## Wind

Constant turbulent wind wrecks the air stream and the sound stream at once, so a Kraaal in a
windy place loses most of their range and falls back on contact and heat. Klyftet's split-howl
is therefore a genuine sensory disability rather than atmosphere, and any Kraaal posted there
is trading comfort for opportunity.

Visible consequences: stall fronts drop in bad weather and trade happens hand to hand across a
counter, fine-casts go quiet and irritable, and deep-casts get **louder**, since their own
voice bouncing back is one of the few streams wind cannot take from them.

## The chime

The coin-lined cloth is not only ornament. Tokens sewn into a Kraaal's robes ring against each
other with every movement, and that constant near-field noise is what lights their sound
stream. **A Kraaal moving in silence has voluntarily half-blinded themselves.**

Consequences that reach a long way:

- Kraaal fidget, sway, and shift weight constantly while standing still, because stillness
  costs them clarity.
- Absolute stillness therefore reads as alarm. A still Kraaal has stopped looking and started
  listening.
- Curating one's chime is vanity, credit display, and eyesight in one act.
- **An unbacked Kraaal's chime is sparse**, since tokens are earned through completed
  obligation. Losing your standing costs you part of your sight, and the poorer you get the
  less clearly you perceive. This is the cruellest thing in the whole system and nobody
  designed it on purpose.
- A silent, still, dead-aired place, such as a sealed tunnel, is close to blindness.

## Reading by touch and tongue

Contact is their sharpest sense, so **Kraaal appraise by touching and tasting.** A Kraaal
handed an unfamiliar object runs their fingertips over it and then touches it with the tongue,
and this is entirely unremarkable to them. It is checking the goods.

Doing it to a **person** is another matter. Among Kraaal it is intimate, forward, and slightly
rude in public, and it is the most thorough way one person can know another. Being tasted is
being read.

## What a Kraaal will tell you about perception

Almost nothing useful. Asking a Kraaal how they perceive is asking a person to explain
something they have always simply done, so expect an authoritative invention, a bored shrug, or
a joke. No Kraaal knows about the sensory tissue, none can explain why flat images mean
nothing, and most assume seeing species are doing roughly what they do with better range.
Nobody has ever successfully explained color to a Kraaal.

# The maw, speech, and eating

The vertical jagged maw opens along its split rather than hinging at a jaw.

**The snarl is mechanical.** Air pushed past the ridges produces it, so a Kraaal cannot speak
without it and it carries no hostility whatsoever.

The useful consequence: **a Kraaal speaking without the snarl is wrong.** It means injury,
serious illness, or a deliberate near-silent whisper that costs real effort. Use the absence
of the snarl to mark a line as genuine, and use it sparingly.

**Eating.** The ridges shear rather than chew, so food goes in whole or in torn strips, and it
goes fast. Eating is private and is done facing away from company, so a Kraaal who eats facing
you is signalling considerable trust.

**The tongue** is long, flexible, and is a sense organ before it is anything else.

---

# Plates, lacquer, and scars

The glossy lacquer is secreted by the Kraaal and is a live indicator of condition. Healthy,
fed, unstressed Kraaal produce a deep even gloss, while illness, malnutrition, and sustained
stress dull it within days.

Other Kraaal read this instantly, so a dull-plated Kraaal reads as unreliable, which becomes a
reputation problem, which becomes a trade problem. **Polish is creditworthiness**, and the loop
is vicious: poor standing means poor work, poor work means poor health, poor health dulls the
plates, and dull plates cost you standing.

**Scars.** A cracked plate seals over with rougher, duller lacquer and never fully matches, so
a Kraaal's history is legible by hand. Other Kraaal read old injuries by touch, which makes a
handshake an unexpectedly informative act.

**Re-lacquering** happens rarely, perhaps once in several cycles, and leaves a Kraaal soft,
raw, and genuinely vulnerable for a few days.

# Posture and tail vocabulary

You cannot read a Kraaal's expression, so you read posture and tail. Keep this consistent
across scenes.

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tail high, arcing slowly | Performing. |
| Tail low, tip moving fast | Calculating. |
| Tail wrapped against the leg | Guarded, or genuinely uncomfortable. |
| Whole body turned to face you | Attention. The closest thing they have to eye contact, and it costs them, since they have no neck and turning the head means turning everything. |
| Head tilted so one side leads | Reading you carefully. Close to a squint. |
| Absolute stillness | Alarm. |
| Hands flat on a surface | Reading through the surface, often reading *you* through the counter. |
| Claw-nails scraped lightly on a doorframe | A fine-cast announcing arrival. |
| Deliberate loud approach | A deep-cast announcing arrival, which is expected of them before entering a crowded room. |

# Casting, sheaths, and cast-lines

Built outward from the canon genitalia note above.

## The sheath

Every Kraaal has one and they are functionally identical across the species, which is what
canon means by no distinct sexes. The sheath is receptive by default, shifts its internal shape
around whatever is inside it, and takes its time closing afterward.

Arousal is readable if you know where to look, since the belly plates part slightly and the
seam warms noticeably well before the sheath opens. **Kraaal do not know this is visible.**
They are used to being unreadable and are careless in front of species that see.

## Casting

Reproduction runs through a separate mode rather than a separate sex. Periodically, and under
conditions of good health and material security, a Kraaal enters **casting**, during which the
sheath everts into a depositing structure for a season. Any Kraaal can cast, any Kraaal can
receive, and which role a given individual takes in a given season is a matter of condition and
circumstance.

Casting is expensive, requiring surplus, stability, and standing, since a clutch has to be
housed, fed, and eventually backed. In practice a Kraaal does not cast without a network behind
them, which means **an unbacked Kraaal effectively cannot have a line.**

## Cast-lines

A casting produces a clutch, and everyone from that clutch shares a **cast-line**. This is
clutch-kinship and it is the closest Kraaal relation to what other species call blood.

**A clutch normally contains both casts.** Cast-line tells you who developed from the same
casting, and cast tells you how your body set, and the two are independent. Two Kraaal of
different casts can absolutely be clutch-kin, and often are.

## Recreational sex

Common, casual, and unremarkable. Sheath to sheath is the ordinary act, warm and slow and
mutual, and it carries no reproductive weight and very little social weight.

What carries weight is **contact reading**, since fingertips and tongue are how a Kraaal truly
perceives. Slow, thorough reading of another body is the most intimate thing available to the
species, and it happens to look like extraordinary attentiveness from the outside.

## Clutch-houses

Young are raised communally in clutch-houses, which are funded by trade networks and which
supply those networks with their next generation. See **Setting** below for what a clutch-house
does to a developing Kraaal, and see Part Five of `RupaaaAndBaaaku_Backstory_WIP.md` for the
harsher end of the practice.

# Sleep, cold, age, and death

**Sleep.** Light and in pieces, several short stretches rather than one long one, since the air
stream keeps working while they rest and a change in the room wakes them. A Kraaal is never
fully asleep the way another species would be.

**Cold.** It stiffens the plates, dulls the lacquer, and slows the seams. Cold postings are
unpopular and Kraaal in them wear far more than they would elsewhere.

**Age.** Seventy to ninety cycles in good conditions. Age shows in the plates, since old
lacquer thickens, dulls, and roughens at the seams.

**Death.** A dead Kraaal's obligations pass to whoever backed them, which makes a Kraaal
funeral a settlement as much as a grief. The body is stripped of its tokens and the tokens go
back to the settlements that issued them, so nothing of the record stays with the corpse.

# Cast, Station, and Standing

**This is the section other species get wrong.** Outsiders collapse three separate questions
into one word, *caste*, and Kraaal find it genuinely irritating, because to them these are
three different questions with three different answers:

| Layer | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Cast** | What body you developed. |
| **Station** | What work you are trusted to perform. |
| **Standing** | Who backs you, and how much social trust you carry. |

A Kraaal asked about their caste will usually answer one of the three and be annoyed that they
had to guess which was meant.

## Cast — the body

Two major casts, and no more. Two keeps Kraaal recognisably one species with contrasting
variations instead of a catalogue of subspecies.

### Deep-cast

- Thick frame, dense plates.
- Broader, sturdier claw-nails.
- Strong low-frequency vocal resonance that carries across noisy spaces.
- Better at reading vibration through floors and counters.
- Retains heat longer and takes longer to warm up.
- Physically very hard to displace once braced.
- Usually slower at delicate tactile work.

**Traditional associations:** public witness, auctioneer, load anchor, caravan defender, heavy
craft, dispute mediator, ceremonial speaker.

Deep-casts acquire a reputation for stability, authority, and reliability. None of that
actually follows from the body, and the voice and sheer physical presence make the stereotype
self-reinforcing anyway.

### Fine-cast

Commonly called **trade-line** in markets, which is the loaded version of the term. It turns a
physical trait into an assumed profession, and fine-casts notice.

- Lighter, quicker frame.
- Narrower, more independently mobile claw-nails.
- Greater tactile resolution.
- Faster scent differentiation.
- Better at reading small movements in air.
- Warms and cools quickly.
- Handles tiny mechanisms, knots, seals, and damaged goods with precision.

**Traditional associations:** goods appraisal, repair, running, route work, record cutting,
packing and sorting, unofficial procurement.

Fine-casts are praised as adaptable while being treated as unreliable in the same breath. They
are expected to move fast, solve problems, take risks, and disappear once a job is done, which
makes them useful and easy to blame.

Many fine-casts file their claw-nails into personalised working profiles, so an experienced
fine-cast can identify another Kraaal's occupation by touching the wear on their nails.

### Setting

A clutch begins physically similar, and the differences emerge during a developmental period
called **setting**, while the plates are still soft.

Cast arises from innate tendency combined with early conditions: mineral content in food, heat,
available space, repeated vibration, physical activity, and contact with clutchmates.
Clutch-houses can gently push the likely outcome and cannot guarantee it.

This is the uncomfortable part of Kraaal history. Caarth's older clutch-houses insist they
merely *"support the cast already presenting itself,"* while everyone is aware that wealthy
networks tend to receive exactly the casts they ordered.

The ambiguity is deliberate and should be preserved. Kraaal genuinely do have different bodies,
and their culture has spent generations exaggerating what those bodies supposedly determine.

## Station — the work

Cast does not formally determine profession. A Kraaal trains into one or more recognized
**stations**, and there are four, which matters because Kraaal hands have four fingers and a
complete set of anything is intuitively **a full hand**.

| Station | Responsibility | Stereotyped cast |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | Negotiation, declaration, public witness | Deep-cast |
| **Hand** | Appraisal, packing, repair, exchange | Fine-cast |
| **Road** | Routes, delivery, procurement, physical verification | Fine-cast |
| **Seal** | Records, scent-stamps, authentication, settlement | Deep-cast |

A stable trade network tries to cover all four, which is called a **full hand**. It need not be
four individuals, and one Kraaal may hold several stations.

**Station tokens.** A station is granted through training and witnessed work, and the newly
recognized Kraaal receives a station token to add to their chime. A station can technically be
lost after serious misconduct, and the token is never forcibly removed. A **withdrawal mark** is
cut into it instead, which changes how it rings.

The consequence is worth using: an attentive Kraaal can *hear* that someone is still wearing a
disgraced station. It is a scarlet letter you can never take off and that only your own kind can
read.

## Standing — the hierarchy they deny having

Kraaal can say truthfully that the casts are equal, because cast is not where the real
hierarchy lives. **Backing** is.

Position is measured through:

- How many Kraaal will answer for you.
- How many settlements recognize those backers.
- The depth of your held name.
- Which stations trust your work.
- Whether your chime carries recognized tokens.
- How much obligation your network could survive.

A wealthy unbacked Kraaal is socially precarious. A poor Kraaal inside a large dependable ring
still receives goods, transport, lodging, and help, because others trust the obligations will
settle eventually.

### How backing works

A backer presses their scent-stamp beside your mark on a settlement register. It is physical,
public, and smellable, and it can be withdrawn the same way, in front of witnesses.

To back someone is to verify their identity, honor their commitments if they cannot, and
inherit their unresolved obligations if they die or vanish.

### What being unbacked costs

No accusation of crime attaches to it. It means only that nobody will put their name beside
yours:

- Payment demanded in advance, everywhere.
- No credit, no consignment, no holding goods for later collection.
- Caravans decline to carry for you, since your losses would become theirs.
- Other Kraaal avoid casual association, since proximity to an unbacked name is contagious in a
  small trade world.
- A sparse chime, which costs you perception as well as face.
- Realistically no casting, so no line.

### Closed rings

Two Kraaal backing only each other is legal and considered thin, since the structure has no
outside anchor and one collapse takes both. The term is **closed ring** and it is usually said
with a little pity in it.

### How a decent system went bad

Backing very likely began as communal risk-sharing, since nobody travelling dangerous roads
could survive a loss alone, so networks agreed to absorb each other's. Over generations it
closed:

- Established rings mostly back the already respectable.
- The unbacked cannot access the work that would prove them.
- Poor health dulls the lacquer, which makes a person look unreliable.
- No work produces worse health, which produces lower standing.
- One member's wrongdoing stains everyone attached to them.
- **Exposing corruption damages the accuser more than the institution.**

That final point is the engine of Rupaaa and Baaaku's entire history.

## Cast prejudice, with no simple top and bottom

Avoid making either cast universally superior. Context decides who gets respect.

**Deep-cast advantages:** more likely to be backed early, usually heard during disputes, assumed
capable of leadership, given ceremonial and institutional positions, and forgiven for public
mistakes as mere overconfidence.

**Deep-cast burdens:** expected to stay calm and physically immaculate, publicly judged for dull
lacquer, treated as a coward for leaving an obligation, judged heavily for retreat, uncertainty,
or changing their mind, and generally treated as communal infrastructure rather than as a
person.

**Fine-cast advantages:** greater physical independence, more freedom of movement, easier access
to practical work, informal networks that share information readily, and permission to improvise.

**Fine-cast burdens:** treated as replaceable, assumed to be concealing goods, blamed first for
cargo discrepancies, rarely trusted as a sole witness, and told that their independence proves
they need less support.

Both sets produce recognisable people with recognisable grievances, and neither produces a
victim or an aristocrat.

## Place variation

**Caarth** is institutional and conservative. Registers record cast, cast-line, station, and
backers as four separate fields. Work is technically open to every cast and practically
segregated. Deep-casts dominate formal witnessing while fine-casts perform most of the actual
movement and appraisal. Crossing into an unexpected station requires additional witnesses.
Clutch-houses cultivate balanced clutches for major networks. Respectability is intensely
audible, through names and through chimes.

**The frontier**, Klyftet included, barely cares. If a fine-cast can carry it, they carry it.
Mixed-species communal life makes rigid Kraaal stations impractical, and most locals have no
idea the distinctions exist. This is liberating and uncomfortable at once for any Kraaal who
arrives with a status they can no longer display.

# Names and held vowels

Kraaal names carry a **held vowel**, the sound drawn out in the snarl, and the number of
repeated letters in trade-script marks how long it is held. The species name works the same way.

A hatchling gets a short name. **The held vowel is added by whoever first backs you**, and its
length is a courtesy indicating how strong that backing is. A long-held name means a
well-anchored Kraaal, and hearing one spoken properly is a status display.

Two consequences worth remembering:

- Dropping a vowel is a public admission that the backing behind it was worthless, so Kraaal
  keep names given by people they have come to despise.
- An unbacked Kraaal has no vowel to give, so two unbacked Kraaal backing each other cannot
  lengthen each other's names. A closed ring is audible in the name as well as the chime.

# Records: cuts, marks, and stamps

Flat writing is invisible to them, so Kraaal records are built for fingers and nose. A ledger is
a strip of prepared hide or thin board carrying:

- **Cuts** along the edge for quantities, read by running a thumb down the strip.
- **Raised marks** pressed into the surface for goods and parties.
- **Material texture**, since the stock is chosen to mean something and a contract for rope is
  recorded on different material than a contract for ore.
- **A scent-stamp** for authenticity, pressed from a small gland at the base of each thumb.
  Signing is a thumbprint that is also a smell.

Consequences:

1. Kraaal books are unreadable to almost everyone else, so anyone who needs to know what is in
   a ledger has to trust the reading.
2. Stamps can be forged, badly and with great difficulty, and a forged stamp smells subtly
   wrong to a Kraaal paying attention. Forgery is a Seal-station crime and is treated as such.
3. Record cutting is fine-cast work, and record *authentication* is Seal work, which puts the
   person who makes a record and the person who certifies it in different bodies and different
   stations by default. Semmaaa's fraud lives in that gap.

# Trade-names

A trade-name is earned and describes something the recipient does repeatedly, so it names a
habit the recipient has often not noticed in themselves. It is given with a held vowel, and
that vowel is the real gift, since giving one is an act of backing in miniature.

Giving one to a non-Kraaal is unusual and is a considerable statement.

# Customs and etiquette

Small, usable, and good for texture:

- Asking a Kraaal's **cast** is ordinary. Asking their **cast-line** is personal. Asking **who
  backs them** is formally acceptable and socially aggressive.
- Touching someone's station tokens implies you doubt their legitimacy.
- Wearing a token for work you no longer perform is acceptable. Wearing one you never earned is
  fraud.
- Introducing someone by station is businesslike. Introducing them by name alone is affectionate.
- Deep-casts are expected to make themselves audible before entering a crowded room, and
  fine-casts scrape their claw-nails lightly over a doorframe.
- A full hand shares liability, so when one member falls ill the others physically wear some of
  their tokens until they recover. A Kraaal walking around loaded with someone else's chime is
  carrying their obligations in the most literal way available.
- Deep-casts sometimes train their resonance against particular stone chambers, so an old
  witness may still carry the acoustic cadence of a specific hall.

**Useful slang:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Wrong-handed** | Working outside your expected cast. An insult or a point of pride, depending entirely on who says it. |
| **Loose claw** | Someone whose work is useful and whose obligations cannot be trusted. |
| **Half a hand** | An incomplete network. Deeply insulting to a closed ring. |
| **Unbacked** | No one will put their name beside yours. |
| **Trade-line** | Fine-cast, said the way markets say it. |

# What Kraaal think of other species

- **Species that see** are careless with contact and strangely confident about things they have
  only looked at.
- **Nöteri** are good custom and poor risk, since they pay reliably and argue constantly.
- **Pöllöka** unsettle them, since Pöllöka move quietly and Kraaal perceive quiet as absence.
- **Sleid** earn professional respect, since anyone who works underwater is working blind by
  Kraaal standards and does it voluntarily.
- **Murn** are trusted more than any other species, because Murn tend to say what a thing costs
  without being asked.
- **Ichmi** are a problem, since Ichmi treat a completed transaction as an opening position.

# What no Kraaal can tell you

Reach for this list whenever a scene needs an honest gap. A Kraaal explaining Kraaal biology
should sound like a person explaining their own eyes.

- How their perception works, or that there is sensory tissue under the plates.
- What color is. Most assume it is a trade superstition.
- What they look like to a species that sees, including their own face.
- Why the maw is shaped as it is, or what Kraaal ate before trade.
- Why lacquer dulls under stress, though every Kraaal knows that it does.
- Where Kraaal came from, what they were before trade, or whether there was a before.
- Why names carry held vowels, or where the practice began.
- Whether setting is genuinely innate, or whether the clutch-houses simply produce what they
  were paid to produce. Every Kraaal has an opinion and none has evidence.

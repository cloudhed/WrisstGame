# Old Mire Language — Vocabulary Reference

Old Mire is a language (or language layer) spoken by at least two known characters in Wrisst: Bitalgut and Pichidi. Both use it as their natural register rather than common-tongue. Whether Old Mire is a regional dialect, a trade language of a specific area, or something older is not established — do not assume either way.

**Reference:** See [Pichidi.md](Pichidi.md) for speech syntax and usage examples. See [Bitalgut.md](Bitalgut.md) for his verbal habits, which overlap with this vocabulary.

## Grammar & Syntax

To capture the unusual, dominant cadence of an Old Mire speaker translating their thoughts into the Common Tongue, writers must apply the following syntax rules (heavily inspired by Uralic/Finnish grammar):

* **Agglutination (Pronoun Suffixes):** Old Mire does not use standalone English pronouns. Subjects and possessives are attached directly to the verb or noun.
  * `-mi`: 1st person (I / me / my)
  * `-ni`: 2nd person (you / your)
  * `-di`: 3rd person (he / she / it / they / its)
  * *Example:* "See-mi meka-ni." (I see your flesh.)
  * *Example:* "Deh-grappi-ni di." (You will jerk it off.)

* **States of Being are Locations (`-la`):** Emotions and physical needs are not things you *are*, they are locations resting *on* you. Attach the `-la` suffix to a pronoun to indicate this state.
  * *Example:* "Mi-la kaavi ikku." (On me [is a] violent need to orgasm.)
  * *Example:* "Di-la hoom." (On it [is a] heavy instinct.)

* **Zero Copula (No "is"):** Do not use a connective word for "is" or "are". Slam the nouns, states, and modifiers directly into each other. This creates a blunt, undeniable biological fact.
  * *Human phrasing:* "Your wetness is too little."
  * *Old Mire syntax:* "Fumi-ni su-sui."
  * *Human phrasing:* "The twitching is too much."
  * *Old Mire syntax:* "Riri su-moi."

* **Drop Articles:** Do not use "a", "an", or "the" unless absolutely necessary for clarity.

* **No Performative Padding:** Do not say "please," "maybe," or "I think." State data as absolute physical laws.

## Usage Notes

* These terms are **neutral in register** within Old Mire speech — they are the plain words, not the crude ones. The crudeness, where it exists, comes from tone and context, not from the words themselves.
* Neither Pichidi nor Bitalgut reaches for common-tongue equivalents. Both use Old Mire as their default, not as a performance.
* Other characters are unlikely to know these terms unless they have specific exposure to Old Mire speakers or regions.

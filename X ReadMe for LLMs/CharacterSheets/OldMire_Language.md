# Old Mire Language — Vocabulary Reference

Old Mire is a language (or language layer) spoken by at least two known characters in Wrisst: Bitalgut and Pichidi. Both use it as their natural register rather than common-tongue. Whether Old Mire is a regional dialect, a trade language of a specific area, or something older is not established — do not assume either way.

**Reference:** See [Pichidi.md](Pichidi.md) for speech syntax and usage examples. See [Bitalgut.md](Bitalgut.md) for his verbal habits, which overlap with this vocabulary.

## Grammar & Syntax

To capture the unusual, dominant cadence of an Old Mire speaker translating their thoughts into the Common Tongue, writers must apply the following syntax rules (heavily inspired by Uralic/Finnish grammar):

* **Agglutination (Pronoun Suffixes):** Old Mire does not use standalone English pronouns. Subjects and possessives are attached directly to the verb or noun.
  * `-mi`: 1st person (I / me / my)
  * `-ni`: 2nd person (you / your)
  * `-di`: 3rd person (he / she / it / they / its)
  * *Example:* "Sina-mi mekk-ni." (I see your flesh.)
  * *Example:* "Deh-grappi-ni di." (You will jerk it off.)

* **States of Being are Locations (`-la`):** Emotions and physical needs are not things you *are*, they are locations resting *on* you. Attach the `-la` suffix to a pronoun to indicate this state.
  * *Example:* "Mi-la kaavi ikku." (On me [is a] violent need to orgasm.)
  * *Example:* "Di-la hoom." (On it [is a] heavy instinct.)

* **Radiating State / Sensory Output (`-et`):** Turns a base concept or action verb into a physical output radiating from a body. Use for things a body involuntarily produces (heat, chill, scent, visual presence, flavor, sound). Solves noun vs. verb ambiguity (e.g., `osh` = to smell; `oshet` = the scent produced).
  * *Example:* "Oshet-ni moi." (Your scent [is] heavy/much.)
  * *Example:* "Havet-ni ashet." (Your radiating warmth [is] perfect.)

* **Intent Prefix (`ji-`):** Attached directly to a verb to change a neutral, biological action into an act of malice, harm, or parasitic intent.
  * *Example:* `ten` (take) → `jiten` (steal)
  * *Example:* `vek` (bite) → `jivek` (torture / to hurt negatively)
  * *Example:* `mjis` (sow) → `jimjis` (force-breed / parasitic insemination)

* **Physical Manifestation Suffix (`-an`):** Turns a raw concept or action into a tangible, defined, harvestable object.
  * *Example:* `mekk` (raw flesh) → `mekkan` (the physical body)
  * *Example:* `mjis` (to sow) → `mjisan` (the physical seed / harvestable milt)

* **The Verb-er Indicator (`-gut`):** Attaches directly to a root verb to create an entity or vessel — "the one who [verbs]." Turns an action into the being defined by that action.
  * *Example:* `jiten` (steal) → `jitengut` (thief / the one who maliciously takes)
  * *Example:* `deh-glim` (cock-suck) → `deh-glimgut` (cocksucker)
  * *Example:* `mjis` (breed) → `mjisgut` (breeder / the one who sows)

* **Systemic State Suffix (`-ep`):** Turns a root concept into an overwhelming systemic state of being — roughly "-without any hope of return." Often used for mind-shatter or pleasure-coma mechanics.
  * *Example:* `bolk` (fool) → `bolkep` (state of madness / complete disarray)
  * *Example:* `ash` (good) → `ashep` (euphoric bliss)
  * *Example:* `fim` (wet) → `fimep` (in estrus / over-lubricated)

* **Plural / Intensity by Doubling First Syllable:** Doubling the first syllable indicates multiple instances, high intensity, or relentless action. If the syllable has double of the same consonants, only use the first one.
  * *Example:* `vek` → `vevek` (deep/multiple cuts)
  * *Example:* `ikku` → `ikikku` (relentless, continuous climaxing)

* **Commands & Imperatives:** Drop the verb's pronoun suffix. The naked verb becomes the command.
  * *Example:* "Osh." (Smell.) — compare "Osh-mi." (I smell.)
  * *Example:* "Ikku." (Cum.) — compare "Ikku-ni." (You cum.)

* **Interrogative (`he?`):** Added to the end of a sentence to turn a statement of physical fact into a question, or a direct, guttural challenge.
  * *Example:* "Jiten-mi, he?" (You steal from me, eh?)

* **Delayed Negator (`, inji`):** A hard boolean false. Used after a comma to brutally revoke or negate the preceding statement.
  * *Example:* "Mekkan-ni fim, inji." (Your body is wet, false. / You are not wet.)

* **Anatomical Verb Focus:** Verb suffixes always prioritize the anatomy being stimulated, regardless of who is doing it. If a player sucks a monster's cock, the monster still calls it `deh-glim` because their deh is the focus. This keeps the linguistic focus aggressively centered on the monster's physical experience and dominance.

* **Zero Copula (No "is"):** Do not use a connective word for "is" or "are". Slam the nouns, states, and modifiers directly into each other. This creates a blunt, undeniable biological fact.
  * *Human phrasing:* "Your wetness is too little."
  * *Old Mire syntax:* "Fim-ni su-sui."
  * *Human phrasing:* "The twitching is too much."
  * *Old Mire syntax:* "Riri su-moi."

* **Drop Articles:** Do not use "a", "an", or "the" unless absolutely necessary for clarity.

* **No Performative Padding:** Do not say "please," "maybe," or "I think." State data as absolute physical laws.

## Usage Notes

* These terms are **neutral in register** within Old Mire speech — they are the plain words, not the crude ones. The crudeness, where it exists, comes from tone and context, not from the words themselves.
* Neither Pichidi nor Bitalgut reaches for common-tongue equivalents. Both use Old Mire as their default, not as a performance.
* Other characters are unlikely to know these terms unless they have specific exposure to Old Mire speakers or regions.

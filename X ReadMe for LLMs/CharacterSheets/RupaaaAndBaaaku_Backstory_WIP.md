# Rupaaa & Baaaku — Backstory, History, and Worldbuilding

> **Status: proposal.** Nothing here is canon yet. It is a deep well to pull from when writing
> their scenes, so that a follow-up question from the PC has an answer waiting instead of being
> invented mid-scene. It can be promoted, edited, or dropped wholesale.
>
> Canon sources this must never contradict: `RupaaaAndBaaaku.md`, `Kraaal_species.md`,
> `Klyftet_location.md`, `Bestiary_Murrisk.md`, and the quest structure in `ROADMAP.md`.
> Where this file resolves something canon leaves open, **Part Twelve** says so.

---

## How to use this file

**Species facts live in `Kraaal_species.md`.** Perception, anatomy, casting, cast, station,
standing, names, records, and customs were all moved there, since they apply to every Kraaal
ever written. Read that sheet first. This file covers how these two specifically embody, exploit,
resent, and argue about all of it, and it holds the dialogue.

Most sections carry three layers:

> **True:** what is actually the case. Author-level. The PC may never learn it, and it exists so
> that two scenes written eight months apart agree with each other.
>
> **They'd say:** what Rupaaa and Baaaku would actually put in their mouths if asked. Close to
> dialogue-ready. Register already correct.
>
> **They don't know:** the honest gap. **Use this layer often.** An NPC who has an answer for
> everything stops being a person.

When the PC asks something neither file covers, prefer "neither of them knows" over inventing,
then write the gap down in Part Eleven afterward.

# PART ONE — THE BROTHERS IN KRAAAL TERMS

## 1.1 Cast and station, at a glance

| | Rupaaa | Baaaku |
|---|---|---|
| **Cast** | Deep-cast | Fine-cast, called trade-line by markets |
| **Stations** | Voice and Seal | Hand and Road |
| **Held name** | Three beats, given by Semmaaa | Middle-held, origin undisclosed |
| **Chime** | Dense and curated | Sparse, and he claims not to mind |

**Between the two of them they cover a full hand.** Voice, Hand, Road, and Seal, complete, in
two bodies. Operationally this makes them extraordinary, and it is the real reason a stall run
by two arguing outsiders became the most reliable in Klyftet inside a season.

Socially it makes them a closed ring, since a full hand is supposed to spread liability across
four lives and theirs rests on two. **They are simultaneously the most complete small network in
town and the most fragile.** Rupaaa quotes the first half constantly and has never once mentioned
the second.

> **They'd say:**
> Rupaaa: *"Between us, every essential station is represented."*
> Baaaku: *"We do the work of four."*
> Rupaaa: *"With three times the competence."*
> Baaaku: *"And half the sleep."*

## 1.2 How each of them wears their cast

**Rupaaa loves being deep-cast.** His theatricality is partly personal and partly the perfected
performance of what a respectable deep-cast is expected to be. He makes himself audible before
entering a room, holds the floor, projects across a square, and treats his own physical presence
as a civic asset. Ask him whether he would choose the same cast and he answers instantly and
theatrically that he would.

He also carries every deep-cast burden without complaint and without noticing that they are
burdens. He must stay immaculate, he must never appear uncertain, and he must never walk away
from an obligation, because a deep-cast who does any of those things is judged far harder than a
fine-cast would be.

**Baaaku does not deny being good at the work fine-casts are expected to do.** What he resents is
people treating his competence as proof that the stereotype was correct all along. He is fast,
his hands are exceptional, he reads claw-nail wear off a stranger's grip and knows their trade
from it, and none of that means he was born to run errands for someone with a wider frame.

Ask him whether he would choose the same cast and he says the question is useless, since no
Kraaal chooses. Pressed in private, he admits he would keep his hands and discard everyone
else's assumptions about them.

> **They'd say:**
> PC: *"Why are you called trade-line?"*
> Baaaku: *"Because deep-casts kept the records."*
> Rupaaa: *"Because generations of observable vocational aptitude..."*
> Baaaku: *"What I said."*

## 1.3 The argument they will have forever

Rupaaa believes the Kraaal system is defensible and can be made fair. Baaaku believes the
unfairness is the function. Neither is written as a fool, and neither delivers a speech.

Rupaaa's position: acknowledging real physical differences permits sensible distribution of
work, and the abuses are failures of individuals rather than of the structure. He has evidence,
since he has personally seen the system work, and he intends to be evidence himself.

Baaaku's position: the distribution always seems to benefit whoever writes it down. He does not
theorize about it. He points at specific instances and lets them sit.

> PC: *"So Rupaaa is above you?"*
> Baaaku: *"He is wider."*
> Rupaaa: *"There is no formal hierarchy between the established casts."*
> Baaaku: *"Ask whose name goes first on a contract."*
> Rupaaa: *"...Formatting is not governance."*

> PC: *"Are fine-casts less trustworthy?"*
> Rupaaa: *"No cast possesses an inherent moral character."*
> Baaaku: *"Deep-casts are better at sounding trustworthy."*
> Rupaaa: *"A disciplined voice reflects disciplined thought."*
> Baaaku: *"He practices in the mornings."*

> PC: *"Who decided what you should be?"*
> Rupaaa: *"No one decides a cast. It presents itself during setting."*
> Baaaku: *"Then the clutch-house feeds it, trains it, names it, and finds exactly what it
> expected."*
> Rupaaa: *"That is a severe simplification."*
> Baaaku: *"It was a short question."*

> PC: *"Could you do each other's work?"*
> Baaaku: *"I do."*
> Rupaaa: *"He performs selected portions of my responsibilities with inadequate ceremony."*
> PC: *"Could Rupaaa do yours?"*
> Baaaku: *"Not through a narrow door."*

## 1.4 Their bodies, specifically

Species detail is in `Kraaal_species.md`. These are the individual facts.

**Rupaaa.** Deep umber, thick frame, broader claws, plates polished every morning in view of the
square without exception. A long rough seam runs across the left side of his ribs, resealed
badly, and he will not say from what. His chime is dense and deliberately curated, and he times
his gestures so the tokens ring on the word he wants emphasised. He trained his resonance
against the stone of Caarth's contract halls and still carries their cadence, which any Kraaal
from Caarth would recognise instantly and which nobody in Klyftet can hear at all.

**Baaaku.** Pale dry green, dust-streaked, quicker build, loose robes with pockets stitched in at
irregular places. His lacquer stays dull because he is always running, and this upsets Rupaaa in
a way neither of them will discuss. A badly resealed claw on his left foot clicks on stone, and
he can walk silently when he chooses and mostly chooses not to, since the click tells Rupaaa
where he is. Three hidden pockets hold station tokens and settlement markers he claims not to
care about.

**The polishing.** Roughly weekly, Rupaaa corners Baaaku and polishes his plates himself while
Baaaku complains steadily and never once actually leaves. It is health, credit, and affection at
the same time, and it is the truest thing about them. Neither has ever said what it means.

## 1.5 Their voices on Kraaal biology

Use these when the PC gets curious. The underlying facts are in the species sheet, and these are
how the two of them deliver, dodge, or fumble them.

**On perception:**
> PC: *"How do you see me?"*
> Baaaku: *"Unfortunately."*
> Rupaaa: *"We perceive you with considerable clarity."*
> Baaaku: *"Your face is mostly speculation."*

> PC: *"But how does it work?"*
> Rupaaa: *"The air carries the shape of a thing to a properly attentive receiver, and I am
> exceptionally attentive."*
> Baaaku: *"He has no idea."*
> Rupaaa: *"I have a working explanation."*
> Baaaku: *"He has no idea."*

**On the chime**, and Rupaaa considers this a vulnerability to hand a stranger, so either gate it
behind trust or let Baaaku give it away carelessly as a sign that he has already decided the PC
is fine:
> PC: *"Why the noise?"*
> Rupaaa: *"A record of completed obligation, worn where it can be appreciated."*
> Baaaku: *"And so we know where the walls are."*
> Rupaaa: *"That is a secondary benefit and you should not have mentioned it."*

**On tasting things**, the first time the PC recoils:
> Baaaku: *"It is how I know what it is."*
> Rupaaa: *"My brother has the manners of a loading yard."*
> Baaaku: *"You do it slower and call it assessment."*

**On the wind:**
> PC: *"Do you like it here?"*
> Baaaku: *"The wind is loud."*
> Rupaaa: *"Klyftet possesses a distinctive acoustic character."*
> Baaaku: *"It is loud, Rupaaa."*

**On smiling:**
> PC: *"Do you ever smile?"*
> Rupaaa: *"Constantly."*
> Baaaku: *"You cannot tell. It is one of our advantages."*

**On death:**
> PC: *"What happens if one of you dies?"*
> Rupaaa: *"An orderly transfer of outstanding obligation."*
> Baaaku: *"I pay for the rest of my life."*
> Rupaaa: *"That is the same statement rendered dramatically."*
> Baaaku: *"It is the same statement rendered correctly."*

## 1.6 The closed ring, and what it cost them

Follow the species rules to their conclusion. Casting requires standing, an unbacked Kraaal
cannot realistically cast, and these two back only each other. **By choosing each other over a
wider network, both of them have almost certainly given up ever casting a line.**

Neither has said this out loud, to the PC or to each other. It sits underneath Rupaaa's hunger
for the salvage charter and his need to attach their names to something that outlasts them, and
it sits underneath Baaaku's insistence that independence is the only thing worth buying. It is
the deepest thing in this document, it should surface once, late, from one of them alone, and it
should never be repeated.

> Rupaaa, without the snarl: *"There will be no line after us. I have made my peace with the
> arithmetic."*

Baaaku's version comes out sideways, in the middle of something else, probably while he is
counting stock.

> Baaaku: *"We are the whole of it. Him and me. That is the entire cast-line."*

## 1.7 The question they will not answer

A clutch normally contains both casts, so being different casts tells them nothing about whether
they share a cast-line. **They genuinely do not know which they are.** They met as adults, both
have gaps in where they came from, and neither has ever tried to check.

So the deflection in their canon lines is two people declining to test a question they would
rather keep.

> PC: *"Are you actually brothers?"*
> Rupaaa: *"Recognized as such in three settlements."*
> Baaaku: *"None of which recognize the others' paperwork."*
> Rupaaa: *"Recognition remains recognition."*

> PC: *"That's not an answer."*
> Baaaku: *"No."*
> Rupaaa: *"It is a sufficient answer."*

> **They don't know:** whether they are clutch-kin. Both have privately considered it, and
> neither has raised it, because a bonded backing can be dissolved while a cast-line cannot, and
> neither is certain which answer would frighten him more.

# PART TWO — RUPAAA, IN FULL

**Cast and stations.** Deep-cast. Voice and Seal.

**Build.** Deep umber, thick frame, broader claws, obsessively polished. Bulk, carrying strength,
anchoring, and a low resonance that carries across a noisy square.

**Age.** Around forty cycles.

**Origin.** Hatched from a casting in the market districts outside Caarth. He can name the
clutch-house and does, at length, and the records of it are among the few documents he has never
actually produced on request.

**Trade.** Junior witness-clerk in the markets around the Library of Caarth, which is Seal work
with Voice ambitions. Contracts, formal declarations, dispute ritual, and the craft of making a
questionable transaction look immaculate. He left with a permanent taste for ceremony and one
durable lesson, which is that the appearance of a clean record travels further than a clean
record.

**What drives him.** Legitimacy. He remembers being treated as unbacked and the memory has never
faded. Every polished plate, every chiming token, every formal declaration is an argument that he
is undeniable now. His records get arranged, presented theatrically, and occasionally rewritten,
and underneath all of that he genuinely dislikes an imbalance that never settles. Every
transaction should close cleanly in the end, and the ones that do not stay with him.

**Fear.** Being provisional. Being a person others are willing to leave out of the record.

**Habits and detail:**

- Polishes every morning, in view of the square, without exception.
- Times his gestures so the chime lands on the word he wants emphasised.
- Refuses the word *approximately*, canon, and invents a precise-sounding number instead.
- Eats behind the canvas, facing away, and would be genuinely distressed to be watched.
- Uses formal phrasing slightly incorrectly, which is trade-dialect that got above itself.
- Takes the long route to a point whenever the long route sounds better.
- Still carries the acoustic cadence of Caarth's contract halls in his resonance.
- Keeps a private strip recording every favor Klyftet has done them since they arrived. He
  intends to settle all of it. He has told nobody, including Baaaku.
- Checks every arriving caravan's manifest for one name, and does not mention doing it.

# PART THREE — BAAAKU, IN FULL

**Cast and stations.** Fine-cast. Hand and Road.

**Build.** Pale dry green, dust-streaked, quicker frame, loose robes with pockets stitched in at
irregular places. Fine handwork, speed, and exceptional tactile resolution.

**Age.** Around thirty-four cycles, though the number he gives moves with the situation.

**Origin.** Will not say. He deflects every time, with a different deflection each time, and the
consistency of the refusal is the tell. See 5.4.

**Trade.** Runner, cargo-finder, route scout, occasional smuggler. Loading yards, caravan routes,
salvage lots, unofficial procurement. Rupaaa recorded where goods were supposed to be, and Baaaku
knew where they actually were.

**What drives him.** Not being trapped, whether by a contract, a blocked route, a superior force,
or somebody else's plan. The pockets, the second suppliers, the half-truths, and the mid-sentence
pivots all exist so there is always another way out of the room.

**Fear.** A closed door he did not choose.

**Habits and detail:**

- Answers before questions finish, and is usually right about where they were going.
- Never uses a long word where a short one exists.
- Tastes almost everything in the stall over the course of a day.
- Reads claw-nail wear off a grip and knows a stranger's trade from it.
- Lets his lacquer go dull, which upsets Rupaaa more than either will discuss.
- Walks with the clicking claw on purpose, so Rupaaa always knows where he is.
- Keeps tokens he claims not to care about in three separate hidden pockets.
- Is often funny, and almost never on purpose.
- Uses *closed ring* and *half a hand* about the two of them, deliberately, in front of Rupaaa,
  roughly once a month, purely to needle him.

# PART FOUR — WHAT SEPARATES THEM

Rupaaa generates excess and Baaaku pares it down, so their rhythm is asymmetric rather than
balanced. Rupaaa makes the promises, and Baaaku is the reason they can be kept. Rupaaa supplies
form and Baaaku supplies material truth, and neither permits the other to become the sole
authority on anything, which is a habit that started in Caarth and never stopped.

The affection is practical and almost never spoken:

- Baaaku cuts into a negotiation when Rupaaa is tiring and his voice starts losing resonance.
- Rupaaa restitches the pockets Baaaku keeps tearing, and never mentions having done it.
- Rupaaa polishes Baaaku's plates roughly weekly, over loud objections that never once become
  Baaaku actually leaving.
- Each knows exactly how much money the other is hiding, to the Öre.
- They insult each other freely, then turn on anyone else who tries it.
- In real danger they stop bickering entirely, and the sudden agreement alarms anyone who knows
  them far more than the arguing ever did.
- **"My liability"** is a recurring term between them, and to another Kraaal it lands as almost
  affectionate.

**Hard rule for writers, from their canon sheet:** do not write them in agreement at the start of
an exchange. The argument is real even when the outcome was settled beforehand.

# PART FIVE — BEFORE KLYFTET

## 5.1 Caarth, and the eight crates

They met over a missing shipment. Rupaaa's records proved eight crates left Caarth, and Baaaku
insisted only seven ever existed. They spent the first stretch of the dispute accusing each
other, loudly and in front of witnesses, and Rupaaa still maintains he was winning.

What they worked out between them was that their shared trade-backer, **Semmaaa**, had been
falsifying shipment strips and pinning the losses on hired runners. The forgeries were good. The
stamps smelled very slightly wrong, and Rupaaa, who handled hundreds of strips a week, was the
only person positioned to notice, and Baaaku was the only person who could prove the crates had
never physically existed.

## 5.2 Why the fraud worked, and why it broke

**Semmaaa's scheme depended on cast convention holding.** It was designed around what each of
them was supposed to do.

Baaaku, a fine-cast Road worker, was expected to move cargo he had not recorded, trust the Seal
station's count, absorb responsibility for physical losses, and carry almost no weight in a
formal dispute.

Rupaaa, a deep-cast junior Seal, was expected to defend his backer's record, treat a runner's
contradictory account as unreliable by default, preserve the network's public stability, and
settle the matter quietly.

Neither did any of it. Baaaku refused to accept the official record over what his own claws had
handled. Rupaaa trusted Baaaku's material evidence over his backer's authority. Each stepped
outside the behavior his cast made predictable, and together they proved something neither could
have proved alone.

**Their brotherhood begins there**, in an act of mutual recognition where each decided the
other's direct knowledge mattered more than the station assigned to him. Everything about how
they talk to each other now descends from that. The constant correcting is the same act,
performed daily, in miniature.

They exposed the scheme, or exploited it, depending on which brother is telling it. Both accounts
are in circulation and neither will settle it.

## 5.3 Unbacked

Semmaaa lost standing. So did they, because exposing corruption damages the accuser more than the
institution. A witness-clerk who accuses his own backer is a witness-clerk nobody wants near
their contracts, and a runner who can prove cargo never existed is a runner every caravan would
rather not employ. Other Kraaal came to consider them troublesome and possibly unbackable.

With neither able to secure a respectable network alone, they backed each other. That is the
moment they became brothers in the sense Kraaal use the word, and it is also the moment they
became a closed ring with two unlengthened names.

They were also chased out, which Rupaaa will phrase almost any other way.

> PC: *"What happened in Caarth?"*
> Baaaku: *"We left."*
> Rupaaa: *"Under conditions of professional disagreement."*
> Baaaku: *"We were chased."*
> Rupaaa: *"No one caught us, which is the distinction that matters."*

This should end the branch on a first pass. After trust, the PC can ask who chased them and get
Semmaaa's name.

## 5.4 Why Baaaku will not name his clutch-house

*Possible truth, deliberately not canonized. It pushes his story into considerably harsher
territory and should be a decision, not a default.*

His clutch-house encouraged fine-setting on purpose, because a trade network needed runners and
paid for them. Its young were **backed in advance against years of future work**, so Baaaku
entered adult life already owing obligations he had never personally accepted. Semmaaa later
acquired or inherited that obligation along with the rest of the network's paper.

When Semmaaa's scheme collapsed, Baaaku lost his backer and the structure that claimed ownership
of his future work in the same week. He was socially ruined and personally free at the same time,
and he has never once described it as bad luck.

It explains a great deal at once: his hatred of closed doors, his need for alternative routes,
his refusal to name where he was hatched, his distrust of long backing chains, why he values the
closed ring while openly mocking it, and why recovered mine wealth means independence to him
where it means prestige to Rupaaa.

If this is taken up, Rupaaa almost certainly does not know, and Rupaaa stopped asking years ago,
which was itself a decision.

## 5.5 Where Semmaaa is now

Nobody knows, and this is the thing Baaaku actually worries about. A Kraaal who loses standing
does not stop existing, and Semmaaa held a very long name, which means a very deep network, and
networks like that decay slowly. Baaaku assumes Semmaaa is still trading somewhere under a
shortened name. Rupaaa considers the matter closed, and Rupaaa checks the caravan manifests every
time one arrives.

## 5.6 The road here

For several cycles they travelled with small caravans, repairing their standing one settlement at
a time, taking work nobody backed would take. Rupaaa negotiated and kept the records, while
Baaaku located goods, chose routes, and decided which contracts Rupaaa should never have accepted.

When word reached them that Klyftet was suffering a severe trade shortage, they went, and both of
their accounts of why are true. Rupaaa says they were answering a civic need, and Baaaku says
they expected desperate customers.

> Rupaaa: *"We came to Klyftet in its hour of commercial desolation, bearing organization,
> courage, and the will to restore..."*
> Baaaku: *"They needed rope."*
> Rupaaa: *"Among other essentials."*
> Baaaku: *"Mostly rope."*

# PART SIX — KLYFTET

## 6.1 The shortage, and what they built out of it

Klyftet was never literally out of everything, and the collapse was logistical. The Old Mine's
closure removed the town's most dependable export, so traders stopped visiting because they could
not fill their return wagons. Local producers still had scattered supplies with no dependable way
to move, measure, or exchange them. Tools, lamp oil, cord, preserved food, and replacement machine
parts all became hard to get, and a few opportunistic traders started demanding Crowns for goods
Klyftet has always priced in Öre, which the town has not forgotten.

**The first days.** They arrived with very little stock, and their real asset was organization,
which is what a full hand buys you. They claimed the abandoned market platform within hours.
Rupaaa stood on the square's speaking platform and publicly guaranteed their first contracts
against his own reputation, which was at that moment worth almost nothing, and nobody in Klyftet
knew that. Baaaku ran between workshops, homes, caravans, and storehouses and discovered the town
held far more usable material than anyone in it realized. Then they started matching surplus to
need.

**Why the town tolerates them.** Klyftet is communal and suspicious of anyone cornering
necessities, so the brothers hold no private grip on food or heating. They earn on transport,
procurement, specialist goods, convenience, and sourcing nobody else will risk. In a genuine
emergency, certain stock becomes communal regardless of who technically paid for it.

Rupaaa calls that *"a temporary suspension of ordinary exchange."*
Baaaku calls it *"not charging freezing people for blankets."*

## 6.2 Why they stay

Two answers, and they pull against each other.

**Klyftet does not classify them.** If Baaaku can repair it, he repairs it. If Rupaaa can carry
it, he carries it. Most locals have no idea Kraaal distinctions exist, and the mixed-species
communal life of a frontier town makes rigid stations impractical.

This is liberating and uncomfortable for both. Rupaaa misses having his formal standing
understood, since a deep-cast Voice with no audience that recognises the office is just a loud
man. Baaaku enjoys not being classified and dislikes that nobody here recognises what it cost him
to escape the classification. Being free of a thing and being unseen for surviving it turn out to
feel very similar.

The second answer is the mine, which is Part Eight.

Neither answer covers the wind, which both of them hate.

## 6.3 The stall, physically

The stall sits on a raised stone platform on the north side of the Central Square, deliberately
chosen for the building at its back that breaks the worst of the split-howl. A canvas over a
wooden frame drops at the front in bad weather.

- A long counter of scarred board, worn smooth in two places where each brother habitually rests
  his hands to read through it.
- A hanging rack of tally-strips, sorted by a system only they can follow, which rattles in the
  wind and is one of the ways they navigate the space.
- Crates stacked behind, restacked constantly, since Baaaku reorganises whenever he is thinking.
- A storehouse shed behind the platform, which is also where they sleep. Rupaaa has maintained for
  two cycles that this arrangement is temporary.
- A weighing beam Rupaaa treats as a ceremonial object.
- Somewhere under the counter, the strips Rupaaa does not display.

## 6.4 A day at the stall

**Before dawn.** Rupaaa polishes. Baaaku is already out, walking the square, the inn's back door,
and the trail heads, collecting what changed overnight.

**Morning.** Peak trade. Rupaaa performs, Baaaku tracks everyone in the square without appearing
to, and both run at least two transactions at once.

**Midday.** Caravan window, if there is one. Rupaaa meets it formally. Baaaku is already at the
back of it, talking to whoever actually loaded it.

**Afternoon.** Slow, and this is when the PC gets real conversation. Baaaku restacks. Rupaaa holds
forth. Small repairs get made and food gets eaten, facing away.

**Evening.** Tally reconciliation, the loudest argument of the day and entirely routine. Then the
canvas drops.

**Night.** They never both sleep at once. One is always half-awake, which is normal Kraaal sleep
and is also a decision.

## 6.5 Stock, refusals, and pricing

**Stock:** rope and cord in every grade, lamp oil and lamp parts, sealed and preserved food,
salves, hand tools and replacement heads, cloth and thread, digging and climbing gear, small
sealed containers, and whatever the last caravan happened to leave. Inventory rotates with local
events, canon from their sheet.

**They buy** creature material, ore samples, salvage, and anything with a traceable origin they
can resell honestly. The creature-material trade has a buyer somewhere up the cliffs who pays well
and communicates badly, which Baaaku finds unnerving and profitable in equal measure. See Part
Twelve.

**They refuse:**

- Anything that would give them a grip on Klyftet's food or heating.
- Anything stolen from a Klyftet resident, since it is traceable and the town is small.
- Anything they would have to hold longer than a few days.
- Any contract requiring them both to leave the stall at once.

**Pricing.** Reputation-based, in Öre. Two firm rules the PC can actually learn:

1. **First purchase rounds in the customer's favor, once, and never again.** Rupaaa calls it an
   introduction. Baaaku calls it an investment.
2. **Playing them against each other raises the price.** They notice immediately, canon, and the
   increase is never announced.

They respect a counter-offer and lose patience with a performance.

## 6.6 Relationships, NPC by NPC

**Each other.** Constant friction, constant care. If one went silent, the other would know inside
a minute.

**Tavo-Pavo.** Keeps their tab accurate, which they respect enormously, so they do not push him.
He is also their best information source, since the Windbreak hears everything and Tavo repeats
about a third of it. Rupaaa has tried three times to formalise an arrangement with him and been
refused three times, in increasingly colorful terms.

**Nautinto.** Buys from them without negotiating, which unsettles them badly, and they have not
worked out why. The truth is that she has decided what the goods are worth and considers arguing
about it a waste of a life, which is a position neither brother can process. Baaaku has started
rounding her prices down without telling Rupaaa. Rupaaa has noticed and said nothing.

**Bwavrek.** They tried to sell him things he did not need and were refused with such finality
that they now respect him. He buys in bulk, on time, without conversation, and Rupaaa finds the
efficiency of it slightly hurtful.

**Minttärä.** She buys small and rarely, and she is the only regular whose approach they
consistently miss, since Pöllöka move quietly and quiet reads as absence. Rupaaa overcompensates
by greeting her far too loudly. Baaaku has fixed a token to the counter boards so it rings when
they flex, purely so they know when she arrives, and he has told nobody it is for her.

**Säyria.** Professional respect, from a distance. She trades sjölfisk sparingly and does not
haggle or chat. Both brothers regard working blind underwater as an extraordinary thing to do
voluntarily.

**Bitalgut.** He has passed through, and they know him. Whether they hold old trade records or
coded messages tied to him is flagged as a possibility in their canon sheet, and this file leaves
it open on purpose. If taken up, the natural form is a strip in Rupaaa's undisplayed set that he
cannot authenticate, because it carries a stamp from no settlement register he recognises.

**Pichidi.** They have been swindled by Pichidi at least twice and will not discuss it. Baaaku
respects the craft. Rupaaa maintains the transactions were completed correctly and refuses all

# PART SEVEN — CAARTH

*Main quest material. `ROADMAP.md` makes Caarth the single main quest destination, so treat this
section as constraining what Caarth can later become.*

Rupaaa remembers records, witnesses, old agreements, and institutional authority, and he admires
the idea that a claim can outlive the person who made it. Baaaku remembers queues, back entrances,
porters, kitchens, unsecured windows, and the price of sleeping under a roof.

> Rupaaa: *"Caarth remembers what other settlements find convenient to forget."*
> Baaaku: *"Caarth also remembers every door you entered through and charges you for using the
> wrong one."*

**The Library** serves pilgrims and scholars while keeping thoroughly practical archives: route
histories, settlement agreements, species accounts, old maps, copied testimony, and fragments from
civilizations nobody has names for. Access is not bought, and it takes sponsorship, service,
contribution, or proof of purpose. Rupaaa knows the shape of all four routes in, since witnessing
sponsorship declarations was part of his old Seal work.

**Caarth is also where cast becomes a wall.** Its registers record cast, cast-line, station, and
backers as four separate fields, and crossing into an unexpected station takes extra witnesses.
Rupaaa performs that system beautifully and still believes it can be made fair. Baaaku understands
it just as well and considers the unfairness part of its function. If the PC ever travels there
with either of them, this is the fault line.

**Where they split on the PC.** Rupaaa wants to present the PC formally, with witnesses and
provenance, because a properly registered claim is the strongest thing a person can hold. Baaaku
thinks that much official attention is dangerous, since once you are in the record you can no
longer step out of it, and an unregistered person can still walk away from a place.

They are both correct, which is the point, and the PC eventually has to pick one.

> PC: *"Could you get me into Caarth?"*
> Rupaaa: *"With sponsorship, testimony, and a competently drafted declaration of purpose."*
> Baaaku: *"Or through the kitchens."*
> Rupaaa: *"One of these approaches survives contact with a registrar."*
> Baaaku: *"One of them survives contact with a registrar who dislikes you."*

> **They don't know** what the Library actually holds about humans, gateways, or the PC. Rupaaa
> assumes there is something, because Rupaaa assumes there is always something. Baaaku thinks the
> more interesting question is who would find out that the PC had asked.

# PART EIGHT — THE OLD MINE (SQ03)

*Fills the gap in `ROADMAP.md`, where SQ03 currently has an opening premise and nothing else.*

## 8.1 Why the mine kept them here

They heard it closed abruptly and that the workers left before clearing it properly, which to a
Kraaal means the tunnels may still hold decades of accumulated material: hand tools and
replacement heads, chain, cable, hooks, winch components, oil lamps and sealed fuel stores, ore
carts and track fittings, smelter moulds, protective clothing, personal lockers and workboxes,
unprocessed ore left in the rush, machinery worth taking apart for its metal, and records pointing
at other deposits or forgotten supply caches.

Rupaaa describes this as *"the responsible reclamation of commercially dormant property."*
Baaaku calls it *"scrap."*

## 8.2 What they actually know

Very little, and the excitement stays speculative. They do not know what survived, what has
already been carried off, or what emptied the place. Every year underground adds corrosion,
cave-ins, flooding, and creatures, and the richest sections may well have been stripped before the
gates shut.

Baaaku has still counted enough inconsistencies to believe something substantial is left, and this
is Hand-station work rather than a hunch:

- Too few discarded ore wagons outside for a mine that size.
- Certain specialized tools have never once surfaced in Klyftet's second-hand market, and he has
  watched that market for two cycles.
- Old workers describe leaving personal belongings behind, and describe it the same way
  independently.
- Several closure barriers went up in a hurry, and he can tell by the joins.

> Baaaku: *"Every story changes."*
> PC: *"So none of them are useful?"*
> Baaaku: *"Three things stay. They left fast, they left tools, and they sealed it from outside."*

## 8.3 Why they will not go in themselves

- Neither of them has ever mined anything.
- Entering openly would damage their standing in a town where reputation travels in an hour, and
  their standing is the only collateral a closed ring has.
- Klyftet cannot agree on who owns whatever is still inside.
- The accessible outer chambers are long since picked over.
- Reaching untouched sections means clearing hazards, opening sealed routes, or finding another way
  in.
- Recovering something valuable without witnesses invites every claimant in town to call it theft,
  and an unbacked accusation would finish them.
- If they both vanish underground, the stall and their entire livelihood vanish with them.
- **They would be close to blind down there.** Still air, dead silence, cold stone, and no chime
  but their own. Every stream they rely on fails at once in a sealed tunnel. This is the reason
  neither of them says out loud, and it is the real one.

Rupaaa emphasises legality and civic sensitivity. Baaaku emphasises that tunnels collapse.

The daytime guard on the fence, canon from `ROADMAP.md`, matters for exactly this reason. Their
whole position rests on being seen doing nothing improper, so the one thing they cannot do is walk
past that guard in daylight, and hiring the problem out solves it.

> PC: *"Why don't you go yourselves?"*
> Rupaaa: *"Because unauthorized subterranean recovery would expose us to considerable legal,
> reputational, and structural uncertainty."*
> Baaaku: *"Ceiling."*
> Rupaaa: *"The structural uncertainty I just mentioned."*

> **High trust, and this one should cost them something:**
> PC: *"You're afraid of it."*
> Baaaku, without the snarl: *"There is no air moving down there."*
> Rupaaa: *"...Nor sound, nor warmth, nor anything a person might navigate by."*

## 8.4 Where they disagree about method

Rupaaa wants a recognized salvage charter, with witnesses, inventories, ownership shares, hired
workers, and an announced recovery operation, so that if the mine ever produces again their names
are attached to its reopening. He is really buying his way back into a wider network, since a
charter would put other names beside theirs and end the closed ring, and that is what he is
actually shopping for.

Baaaku wants several short targeted entries instead, going in for the old storage and maintenance
chambers, taking compact high-value material, and leaving before the mine or the town's politics
can close around them. He is buying the opposite thing, which is never needing anyone's backing
again.

**Their methods are their two answers to the same wound.** Rupaaa wants back into the system that
threw them out, and Baaaku wants to be beyond its reach. Both are defensible, Rupaaa's is safer
socially and draws attention, and Baaaku's is safer underground and would ruin them if it were
ever discovered.

## 8.5 The closure, and the accounts they have collected

Neither brother will confidently explain why the mine closed, which suits a town that canonically
has as many explanations as it has voices. What they have is the collection:

- Something started taking workers in the deep tunnels.
- A rich vein released poisoned dust.
- The owners ran after hiding unpaid wages, or stolen material.
- The deepest supports began making sounds before they ever moved.
- The diggers broke into structures older than Klyftet.
- It started as a labor dispute and turned into something else.
- Nothing strange happened at all, and the mine simply became unsafe and unprofitable.

Rupaaa performs each version with full dramatic gravity and enjoys it more than he admits. Baaaku
filters them for the details that repeat.

## 8.6 The deal

> Rupaaa: *"One-third of verified recoveries, after expenses, with preferred purchasing rights
> afforded to our establishment."*
> Baaaku: *"Half."*
> Rupaaa: *"Half?"*
> Baaaku: *"They take the risk."*
> Rupaaa: *"...A persuasive correction."*

A rare beat where the disagreement is genuine instead of staged. Since their canon rule is that
they never agree at the start, this works because Rupaaa **concedes**, which is a different act
from agreeing. It is also the eight crates again in miniature, with Baaaku's direct knowledge of
what dangerous work costs overriding Rupaaa's paperwork.

> **They don't know** about the Murrisk. It is canon in the mine's depths per its bestiary sheet,
> and their honest ignorance is doing far more work than they realize. If the PC comes back and
> describes it, that is the first time either brother has been genuinely frightened in front of
> them.

# PART NINE — INTIMACY

Canon tone: opportunistic and playful, with the texture of an exchange both parties understand.
Canon hard limits: they are not cruel, they are not predators, the playfulness is genuine, and the
calculating quality of their trading softens here rather than sharpening. Everything below serves
that. Species anatomy is in `Kraaal_species.md`.

**What contact means to them.** Fingertips and tongue are their highest-resolution sense, so being
touched thoroughly is being *known*. A Kraaal reading another body slowly with hands and tongue is
doing the most intimate thing available to their species, and it happens to look like
extraordinary attentiveness from the outside. Lean into this. It is the best thing about writing
Kraaal sex.

**Temperature.** The PC runs hot, and this is genuinely overwhelming at close range, since warmth
is one of their primary streams and a human pressed against them floods it. Neither brother is
prepared for how much they like it.

**Their arousal is visible and they do not know it.** Belly plates part slightly and the seam warms
well before the sheath opens, which hands the player information the NPC assumes is private.

**Cast shows here too.** Rupaaa is heavy, slow to warm and slow to cool, immovable once braced, and
clumsier than he would like at anything delicate. Baaaku's hands are the finest instrument either
brother owns, he warms and cools fast, and he cannot hold still. Neither of them will comment on
this and both are aware of it.

**The novelty problem.** A permanently external organ reads to them as a body stuck mid-casting all
year, which is astonishing and faintly indecent. Rupaaa will want to address the matter formally
before he touches it. Baaaku will ask how it works while already handling it.

**If joint:** competitive in a way that somehow serves the PC. The bickering does not stop and
neither does the precision, canon. They will absolutely argue about technique mid-act and neither
will concede.

**If separate:** each would be surprised how different it was from the other, and this information
never leaves the stall. Write the second scene aware of the first.

**Chime.** They see by sound, so movement in a quiet room is both soundtrack and eyesight. A
brother going deliberately still and silent during sex has chosen to stop looking and to feel
instead, and that is a far larger gesture than it appears.

> Rupaaa: *"Baaaku."*
> Baaaku: *"It is a fact about them."*
> Rupaaa: *"It is a fact you have now said aloud twice."*

# PART TWELVE — OPEN DECISIONS

Numbered so they can be approved or cut individually. Items 1 to 5 are **species-wide** and now
live in `Kraaal_species.md`, so approving them affects every Kraaal ever written.

1. **Cast, Station, and Standing.** The three-layer replacement for "caste," including the
   terminology change from *deep-caste* and *trade-line* to **deep-cast** and **fine-cast**, with
   *trade-line* kept as a loaded market term. This edits the canon line in `Kraaal_species.md`.
   **The same old terms still appear in `(ReadMe for LLMs) Wrisst Knowledge Database & Design plan
   05-03-2026.txt` and were deliberately left alone**, since that file is master canon and this
   rename is not approved yet. Say the word and it gets updated to match.
2. **The four stations and the full hand.** Voice, Hand, Road, and Seal, landing on the canon
   four-fingered hands. Station tokens with withdrawal marks cut into them, audible in the chime.
3. **Setting**, in the influenced version, where cast emerges from innate tendency plus early
   conditions and clutch-houses can push without guaranteeing. Keeps genuine bodily difference
   while making the culture's claims about it suspect.
4. **Casting and cast-lines.** Built outward from canon's "no distinct sexes" and the sheet's own
   use of "cast-line." A clutch contains both casts, so cast-line and cast are independent, which
   is what preserves the brothers' ambiguity.
5. **Perception, the chime, and held vowels.** The five reads, the wind disability, chime as
   eyesight, and names lengthened by whoever backs you. The chime rule has a hard consequence
   worth approving deliberately: **an unbacked Kraaal perceives worse**, so poverty is literally
   blinding in this culture.
6. **The brothers question.** Their canon sheet keeps it unknown whether they are blood, bonded,
   or the same cast-line. This file keeps it unknown **permanently**, by making it unknown to them
   too. That forecloses ever revealing a truth later, so it needs a deliberate yes.
7. **Coin-lined cloth.** Canon says coin-lined. This makes the linings contract and station tokens
   that read as coin to outsiders. Compatible as written, and species-wide.
8. **Semmaaa's cast-specific fraud.** The scheme depended on each brother behaving as his cast
   predicted, and their partnership begins when neither does. Strongly recommended, since it turns
   a visual contrast into the actual origin of their trust.
9. **Baaaku's clutch-house.** Section 5.4, pre-backed against years of future work. Deliberately
   left as a possible truth. It is the harshest thing proposed anywhere in these two files.
10. **Semmaaa.** A name with no location. Either promote to a future antagonist or cut the thread.
11. **The cliff-side buyer of creature material.** A deliberate hook toward SQ02's alchemist, since
    the roadmap already places a badly-communicating alchemist up the cliffs beside the lake. Cheap
    to cut, cheap to keep.
12. **Rupaaa's charter path.** Does it lead to a real mechanical outcome, a reopened mine and a
    changed town, or does it stay flavor while Baaaku's approach is the one the quest is built
    around?
13. **Bitalgut.** Their sheet flags a possible link through old trade records. Left open on
    purpose, with one suggested form in section 6.6.
